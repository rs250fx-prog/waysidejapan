Between 2023 and 2026, several of the rules that decide what a visit to Japan costs, and how you book it, were changed or are about to change. Tax-free shopping moves to a refund system on 1 November 2026, the departure tax tripled on 1 July 2026, and more prefectures and towns now charge an accommodation tax. This column sets out each change with its date and the body that made it. It is the second of three columns on inbound tourism; the first, on [crowds and local rules](/en/column/crowds-and-local-rules-in-japan/), covers the numbers and what individual places have done.

Where an amount appears below, it is either a tax or fee fixed by law or ordinance, or a price the official source marks as tax-included. JR Pass prices are given as listed by JR.

## Tax-free shopping: a refund system from 1 November 2026

**What changes.** Under the system that runs until 31 October 2026, eligible visitors buy at the tax-free price in a registered shop. From purchases made on 1 November 2026, according to the Japan Tourism Agency and the National Tax Agency, you pay the tax-included price, customs confirms at departure that you are taking the goods out of Japan, and the consumption tax is then refunded. The National Tax Agency's English leaflet says there will be no period in which the two systems run together.

The finance ministry gives the reason as dealing with fraud such as tax-free goods being resold inside Japan.

**Who and what.** The buyer must be a non-resident staying under a status such as "temporary visitor", or a Japanese national who has lived abroad for two years or more. The minimum is ¥5,000 before tax, in one shop on one day, as before. The separate categories for general goods and consumables, and the ¥500,000 upper limit on consumables, are abolished, so there is no upper limit. The tourism agency says you may buy only as many items as you can carry out of Japan yourself when you leave.

**What you do.**

1. Show your passport in the shop and pay the tax-included price. The shop explains how to register for the refund.
2. When you leave Japan, have the goods confirmed by customs within 90 days of the day after purchase. The tax agency's example: goods bought on 1 November have a deadline of 30 January the following year.
3. Before you check in your luggage, scan your passport at a tax-free kiosk in the international departure area. A green result ends the procedure; a red one means an inspection at the customs counter.
4. At Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chūbu, Fukuoka, New Chitose and Naha, the procedure can also be done online through Visit Japan Web.
5. The refund comes from the shop or a refund company it uses. The method varies by shop; the agency lists bank transfer, credit card, app payment and cash at the port after the customs check as examples.

**Exceptions.** The tourism agency's English leaflet says you "will not be eligible for a tax refund" if you use or consume food, drink, cosmetics or other consumables in Japan. If you have consumed any, the agency asks you not to use the kiosk but to tell the customs officer. If one item from a receipt is not with you, none of the items on that receipt can be confirmed. Once your bags are checked in, the procedure can no longer be done. For food souvenirs, see [food souvenirs (omiyage)](/en/column/food-souvenirs-omiyage/).

## The proposed 1% tax on food: not yet law

On 15 September 2026 the cabinet adopted an outline to cut the consumption tax on food to 1% (national and local combined) from 1 April 2027 to 31 March 2029. The outline describes it as a temporary cut for two years "in view of the current rise in prices". The National Tax Agency's Q&A says the range is the same as the food now taxed at the reduced 8% rate: food bought to take away or delivered, but not meals eaten in a restaurant and not alcohol.

The bill was received by the House of Representatives on 9 October 2026. As of 15 October 2026, the parliamentary record shows no committee referral, vote or promulgation. The tax agency's special site states that its explanations apply only if the bill is passed.

Until then, nothing changes for visitors: takeaway food is taxed at 8% and eating in at 10%. See [reading menu prices](/en/column/reading-menu-prices-in-japan/), [paying at restaurants](/en/column/paying-at-restaurants-in-japan/) and [convenience stores](/en/eat/conbini/).

## Accommodation taxes

**How they work.** Under the Local Tax Act, a prefecture or municipality may levy a tax for a specified purpose by ordinance, with the prior consent of the Minister for Internal Affairs. Accommodation tax is one of these. In most places the hotel collects it with the room charge and pays it to the authority. Tokyo notes that you may be asked to pay it at the front desk, separately from a package price.

**Where.** The internal affairs ministry's list as of 1 October 2026 shows accommodation tax in force in seven prefectures (Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka, Miyagi, Hokkaido, Hiroshima, Nagano) and 42 municipalities, including Kyoto, Kanazawa, Kutchan, Fukuoka, Kitakyūshū, Sapporo, Hakodate, Hakuba, Karuizawa and Kumamoto. Ordinances have been passed and approved, but not yet brought into force, in Okinawa Prefecture (from 1 February 2027), Fujiyoshida and Fujikawaguchiko (1 April 2027) and other places.

**Examples** (all set by ordinance; amounts per person per night):

- **Tokyo.** Now ¥100 on a room charge of ¥10,000 to under ¥15,000 and ¥200 from ¥15,000; nothing below ¥10,000. From 1 April 2027 (planned; ordinance promulgated 1 July 2026) the tax becomes 3% of the room charge, with nothing below ¥13,000, and simple lodgings and minpaku are added. The room charge means the room-only rate before tax. Tokyo says flat amounts had left guests in higher-priced rooms paying a lower share.
- **Osaka Prefecture.** Since stays on 1 September 2025, nothing below ¥5,000, then ¥200, ¥400 or ¥500 depending on the room charge. School trips are exempt.
- **Fukuoka Prefecture, Fukuoka City and Kitakyūshū.** Since 1 April 2020, ¥200 in total; in Fukuoka City, ¥500 on a room charge of ¥20,000 or more. In the two cities, the city collects the prefectural share too.
- **Kanazawa.** Since 1 April 2019, ¥200 below ¥20,000 and ¥500 from ¥20,000, with no tax below ¥5,000 since 1 October 2024. The ordinance sets these as fixed amounts, not a percentage.
- **Kutchan (Niseko area).** 2% of the room charge from November 2019, then 3% from stays on 1 April 2026, which includes Hokkaido's new tax collected by the town.

## Departure tax: ¥3,000 from 1 July 2026

The International Tourist Tax was introduced on 7 January 2019 at ¥1,000. An amendment promulgated on 31 March 2026 sets it at ¥3,000 per departure from 1 July 2026. It is paid by passengers leaving Japan by air or sea. Airlines and shipping companies collect it, usually by adding it to the fare. The National Tax Agency says that departures under certain transport contracts made before 1 July 2026 are taxed at ¥1,000. Transit passengers leaving within 24 hours and children under two are not taxed.

The tourism agency says the revenue is being used, among other things, to strengthen measures against excessive crowding and breaches of manners.

## Dual pricing

**Two meanings.** In Japanese consumer law, "dual price display" refers to showing a sale price next to a comparison price, such as a former price. The tourism agency uses "dual pricing" for something else: setting different prices for residents and non-residents.

**The government's wording.** On 3 March 2026 the transport minister said that whether to adopt dual pricing should, in the first instance, be decided by each facility's operator, taking local residents into account, and that the ministry would work towards guidelines. On 18 March the tourism commissioner called it "one of the methods of setting prices" and said it was "extremely important" to explain the reasons for any difference to users. The agency's study group on pricing met four times between 27 April and 14 July 2026. As of 15 October 2026 no guideline has been published.

**Examples.** The agency's list of cases, compiled from facilities' websites, includes residence-based prices at Himeji Castle, Odawara Castle and Sapporo TV Tower; prices for Nara Prefecture residents, Japan residents and overseas residents at the Nara Prison Museum; a charge for overseas residents at Nanzō-in temple in Fukuoka; and a lower price for foreign visitors at the Iwami Ginzan World Heritage Centre.

**Himeji's choice.** At the study group's second meeting, Himeji City explained that it first considered prices by nationality. The minutes record that, after discussion of whether that would be appropriate under the equality clause in Article 14 of the Constitution, the city chose residence instead, citing the taxes and planning rules residents bear. This is the city's own explanation, not a legal view from the national government.

Where prices differ by residence or nationality, you may be asked for ID: the agency's documents mention ID cards at Himeji and a passport at Iwami Ginzan.

## Reservations and ID checks

- **Ghibli Museum, Mitaka.** The museum says admission is "by advance reservation only", with no tickets sold at the museum. Tickets go on sale through Lawson Ticket on the 10th of the previous month.
- **Naoshima.** Benesse Art Site Naoshima introduced online tickets at all its art sites from 1 October 2024, giving the reasons as easing crowding on the island and waiting times. The Chichu Art Museum is by timed reservation; when online tickets sell out, there are no tickets at the door.
- **Katsura Imperial Villa, Kyoto.** Visits are guided tours of about 60 minutes, booked online from the first day of the month three months ahead. A limited number of same-day places are given out from 8.40. English tours run five times a day. All visitors' identity is checked.
- **Shiretoko.** The walk up the Kamuiwakka hot waterfall has required booking and a fee since the 2023 financial year. The environment ministry's guidelines say its national park fees are "not intended to limit visitor numbers as such".

**Passports at hotels.** Since 1 April 2005, the Hotel Business Act rules require the nationality and passport number of guests who are foreign nationals without an address in Japan to be entered in the guest register. The health ministry's guidelines ask hotels to see the passport and keep a copy. From 1 April 2025, hotels may check identity through automatic check-in machines and keep the passport copy electronically.

## Minpaku (private lodging)

Under the Private Lodging Business Act in force since June 2018, a home-sharing property may be let for no more than 180 days a year, and local authorities can restrict dates further by ordinance. Special-zone minpaku, a separate scheme, has no annual limit but requires stays of at least two nights. The Cabinet Office states that special-zone minpaku can be used by Japanese and foreign guests alike.

Osaka City stopped accepting new applications for special-zone minpaku after 29 May 2026. Properties certified before then continue to operate, so existing listings can still be booked.

JR says prices on its own sales site stay unchanged "for a certain period", with an end date to be announced. Children aged 6 to 11 pay half. To ride *Nozomi* and *Mizuho* trains, pass holders need a separate ticket, which JR announced in 2023. For a regional pass, see the [Setouchi Area Pass](/en/rail/setouchi-area-pass/).

**Large luggage on the shinkansen.** On the Tōkaidō, Sanyō and Kyūshū shinkansen, bags with three sides totalling over 160 cm and up to 250 cm need a reserved "seat with oversized baggage area", at no extra charge. JR Central says that if you bring one on board without reserving, a fee of ¥1,000 (tax-included) is charged. Since 1 July 2025, as a trial, some deck areas on 16-car Tōkaidō and Sanyō trains can be used for luggage without booking, though not for oversized bags.

**Ride-hailing and "white taxis".** In March 2024 the transport ministry created a scheme in which taxi companies use private cars and ordinary drivers at times and places where taxis are short; it began in April 2024. The ministry states that carrying passengers for money without a permit or registration, known as a white taxi (*shiro-taku*), is "a serious illegal act". Licensed buses and taxis have green number plates; private cars have white ones.

## Medical costs and insurance

The health ministry says it "strongly recommends" private medical insurance, warning that treatment can be expensive and that leaving a bill unpaid may lead to stricter immigration checks on later visits. The tourism agency and the ministry publish a list of hospitals that accept foreign patients, last updated on 31 March 2026.

In the tourism agency's survey of 3,069 departing visitors (October 2023 to February 2024), about 4% said they had fallen ill or been injured during the trip, and about 73% said they had private medical insurance. Of those who had not bought insurance and did not know about it, 30% said they would have bought it had they known.

The health ministry's survey for the 2022 financial year found that 469 of 2,352 hospitals (19.9%) that had treated foreign patients had unpaid bills from them, with 83.2% of cases at ¥50,000 or less. The ministry's note says these "foreign patients" include residents of Japan and people travelling for treatment, so the figure is not for tourists alone.

## What this means for a visitor

- **Keep tax-free goods sealed and together.** From 1 November 2026, do not open or eat food bought tax-free, and check through customs before you check in your bags.
- **Read the 1% proposal as a proposal.** Tax on food stays at 8% for takeaway and 10% for eating in until a law says otherwise.
- **Expect taxes outside the room rate.** Accommodation tax may be collected at the front desk, and the departure tax is usually in the fare.
- **Carry your passport.** Hotels must record it, and some places with residence-based prices check ID.
- **Book first.** Several sites sell no tickets at the door, and large bags need a reserved seat on the main shinkansen lines.
- **Use licensed transport, and get insurance.** Look for green plates, and buy medical insurance before you travel.
