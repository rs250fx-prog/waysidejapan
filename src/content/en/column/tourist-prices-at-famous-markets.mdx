Three markets appear on almost every first-time itinerary: Kuromon in Osaka, Nishiki in Kyoto, Omicho in Kanazawa. All three are real working markets with long histories. All three now have a second layer built on top of them — stalls that exist for visitors, priced accordingly.

This is not fraud. The prices are posted, nobody is deceived, and the stalls are within their rights to charge what the traffic will bear. The useful question is narrower: **how far above the normal Japanese price are they, and where is normal?**

That second half is the part English-language guides never answer. They will tell you a market is "pricey" — but pricey compared to what?

## The line is ¥2,000

There is no government statistic for seafood bowls. There are statistics for the things a visitor can anchor against, and they are published monthly by the Statistics Bureau.

Against that, **a ¥4,500 seafood bowl is six ramen.** The fish is better than it would be in a ¥1,000 bowl. It is not six times better.

## Why the gap exists

Three things, none of them conspiratorial.

**No reference price.** A visitor eating a seafood bowl for the first time has no idea what one normally costs. Without a reference, any number is plausible. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why this article exists.

**One visit, not fifty.** A neighbourhood restaurant survives on people who come back. A stall in a famous market arcade does not need you to come back.

**Dishes with no standard version.** Wagyu curry udon is not a dish most Japanese people order, so there is no familiar price to compare it against. Invented combinations carry the widest pricing latitude.

## What to do instead

Not "avoid these markets". Walk through them — some of the old trade is still there, and Nishiki still sells ingredients to Kyoto kitchens. But:

- **Eat one street back.** A set meal at an ordinary restaurant behind the arcade will cost a fraction and, more often than not, be better.
- **Use ¥2,000 as your own line.** Above it, ask what you are getting that a ¥1,500 version would not give you.
- **Be wary of a dish you have never heard of.** A famous ingredient in an unfamiliar format is the format with the most pricing room.
- **Compare at a department store food hall.** Same city, same fish, prices set for people who shop there every week.

## How this article is put together

The market prices come from the posts listed at the end and from what I saw myself in October 2026, marked where each applies. The national averages are from the government retail price survey. **Market prices move, and stalls differ from each other within the same arcade** — treat the figures as the order of magnitude rather than as a menu.
