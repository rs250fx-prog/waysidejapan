Vending machines are one of the first things visitors notice in Japan. This column sets out what the industry bodies and JNTO say about how many there are and how you pay, and what a private survey says about the unstaffed frozen-gyoza shops that spread during the pandemic. Much of what is often said about Japanese vending machines — the oden cans, the noodle machines, why there are so many — I could not confirm from an official or company source, and I say so below rather than repeat it.

## How many there are

The Japan Vending Machine Manufacturers Association (JVMA), an industry body, gives these figures for the end of December 2025:

- **All vending machines:** 2,585,200 (98.9% of the year before)
- **Drink vending machines:** 2,178,800 (99.1% of the year before)

From those two figures, drink machines make up about 84% of the total. That share is my own calculation from the association's numbers, not a figure it publishes. I could not tell from the page whether its total includes other automatic service machines.

**A second count.** The Japan Soft Drink Association says there were 1,994,000 soft-drink vending machines at the end of December 2022, based on JVMA's survey. Because it covers a different category and a different year, I do not compare it with the 2025 figure as a trend.

**What I could not confirm.** Reports that the number peaked around 2013, or that the way machines are counted changed in 2017, came to me only through news coverage, not the association's own pages. This page does not use them.

## How you pay

**Cash.** JNTO says that while cashless payment is spreading, there are still many places where cash is needed, and some places in rural areas take cash only. Its advice is to "always have some cash on hand".

**Cards and phones.** JVMA's FAQ lists three cashless methods at vending machines: prepaid cards, electronic money and code payments. It says electronic money on contactless IC cards is now the main one, and that machines taking payment from IC-equipped mobile phones are widespread, for example inside stations. JNTO says transport IC cards such as Suica and Pasmo, and their smartphone versions, can be used at many convenience stores, shops and vending machines.

**The order of steps.** With electronic money, JVMA says, the amount is deducted at the moment you touch the card, so you select the item first and then tap.

I could not find an official figure for what share of vending machines take electronic money.

## The frozen-gyoza shops

During the pandemic, small unstaffed shops selling frozen gyoza (dumplings) appeared across Japan. They are often grouped with vending machines, but the survey below treats them as unstaffed shops (無人販売店), not machines.

Teikoku Databank, a private credit research company, surveyed the 67 operators and brands for which it could get store numbers and history. It says:

- the shops grew by more than 1,000 over the three pandemic years, to about 1,300 by the end of fiscal 2022 — roughly ten times as many in three years
- another heading on the same page says they grew to 1,400 nationwide; I could not find why the two figures differ
- from fiscal 2023 the pace of openings slowed, and some shops closed
- per piece, the gyoza cost somewhat more than ordinary shop-bought frozen gyoza
- some store numbers and opening dates are estimates

This is a private company's survey, not an official statistic. It does not say how customers pay.

## What I could not confirm

These come up often, but I could not open an official or company source for them, so this page does not describe them:

- **Oden in a can**, sold from machines in Akihabara and elsewhere. I could not open the maker’s or the machine company's own pages; dates I saw elsewhere came only from search summaries.
- **Udon and soba vending machines** at old-style drive-ins. No company or local-government source I could open; no names, prices or dates here.
- **How hot and cold drinks share one machine**, and **why Japan has so many machines**. I found no official explanation.
- **Meal-ticket machines** in restaurants are a different thing; they have their own column on this site.

## What people get wrong

- **"Most of Japan's vending machines sell all kinds of food."** By JVMA's figures, about 84% are drink machines.
- **"Vending machines are cashless everywhere."** JVMA says electronic money is the main cashless method, but JNTO says cash is still needed in many places, especially rural ones.
- **"Tap first, then choose."** With electronic money, JVMA says you choose first.
- **"The gyoza shops are vending machines."** The survey counts them as unstaffed shops.
