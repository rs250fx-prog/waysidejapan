Christmas in Japan has two foods of its own: fried chicken and a Christmas cake. Neither is a household ritual in the sense that the solstice pumpkin or New Year’s *osechi* is. Christmas is not one of the traditional seasonal festivals, and the farm ministry, which explains the meaning of most food on Japan’s calendar, does not explain these two. What exists instead are company histories: Kentucky Fried Chicken Japan’s for the chicken, Fujiya’s for the cake. This page sets out what each company says, and treats it as the company’s account.

For food on the rest of Japan’s calendar, start from the [overview of food for annual events](/en/eat/seasonal/).

## Where it came from

No public source used for this page says when eating chicken or cake at Christmas became a nationwide habit in Japan. The two dates that are documented are company dates, and they are 64 years apart.

- **1910.** Fujiya, the Western confectioner, opens a shop in Motomachi, Yokohama, in November, and in December begins selling a Christmas cake, according to its own timeline.
- **1974.** Kentucky Fried Chicken Japan runs its first Christmas campaign in December, according to its company history.

Neither company says its date started the national custom, and this page does not join the two into one story.

## What makes it Christmas food in Japan

### The chicken, in Kentucky Fried Chicken Japan’s words

The company history says its first shop, the Meisei shop, opened in Nishi Ward, Nagoya, in November 1970, and that it ran its first Christmas campaign in December 1974.

Its recruitment page tells how the link began. A store manager, at a Christmas event, dressed as Santa Claus and visited nearby kindergartens and primary schools, where he performed a dance he was not used to doing, and the children loved it. The page says that, taking a hint from this, the company promoted the idea of "Christmas means Kentucky" widely, that this is said to be where the connection began, and that in 1974 it started a large-scale Christmas campaign. The page does not name the manager.

A company news release says the *Party Barrel*, its bucket set, went on sale in 1985 and marked its 40th anniversary in 2025. The company history page itself does not mention the barrel.

Other explanations for why chicken came to stand for Christmas in Japan circulate, but none appears in the company’s own pages or in a public source used here, so they are left out rather than set beside the company’s account as equals. The [chicken overview](/en/eat/chicken/) covers fried and grilled chicken in Japan more broadly.

### The cake, in Fujiya’s words

Fujiya’s timeline says Fujii Rin’emon opened a Western confectionery shop in Motomachi, Yokohama, in November 1910, and that a Christmas cake went on sale in December of the same year. The timeline does not describe the cake. It does not say whether it was anything like the strawberry and cream sponge sold in Japan today, and it does not call it the first Christmas cake in Japan, so this page does not either.

The same timeline dates Fujiya’s shortcake to 1922, at 8 sen a piece. Fujiya tells the shortcake’s origin in two different ways on two of its pages. Both are set out on the page about [Western-style cakes made in Japan](/en/eat/sweets-yogashi/), and the [sweets overview](/en/eat/sweets/) places them among the rest.

### The strawberry, and when it arrives

One public source bears on the cake from the other side. A December 2022 report by the Agriculture and Livestock Industries Corporation (ALIC) says that until the 1960s strawberries were in season around May and June. With the spread of greenhouse growing, autumn-planted plants became the norm, because they suit the year-end and New Year demand season. At Tokyo’s central wholesale market, it says, Tochigi strawberries begin to arrive in November, and arrivals rise sharply in December.

The report speaks of year-end demand, not of Christmas cake by name, and it does not say the cake caused the change. The two facts sit side by side.

## How to eat it

None of the sources describes a way of eating either food at Christmas: no day of ordering, no order of courses, no rule about who eats what. This page does not add one. Prices are not given, because no official tax-inclusive price was confirmed for this page.

## Not all of Japan does this the same way

The sources used here do not compare Christmas food between regions. The places they name are where the companies began: Nagoya for Kentucky Fried Chicken Japan’s first shop, Yokohama for Fujiya’s.

## What people get wrong

- **"Christmas chicken is an old Japanese tradition."** The documented start is a company campaign in December 1974, by the company’s own account.
- **"Fujiya sold Japan’s first Christmas cake."** Its timeline records a Christmas cake in December 1910. It does not claim it was the first, and does not say what it was.
- **"Christmas is a holiday food tradition like New Year."** It is not one of the traditional seasonal festivals, and no public source here says when the chicken and the cake became a national habit.
- **"The Party Barrel came with the first campaign."** The company dates the barrel to 1985, eleven years after the first Christmas campaign.
