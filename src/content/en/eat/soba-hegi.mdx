In Niigata, soba has a seaweed in it. *Hegi soba* is bound with *funori*, a seaweed that was once used to stiffen the threads on the region's looms, and it is served on a *hegi*, a square tray made of split wood. The farm ministry lists it among Niigata's local dishes and says it is eaten across the prefecture. For the noodle in general, see [soba](/en/eat/soba/); for the rest of Niigata's food, the [Hokuriku overview](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-hokuriku/).

## Where it came from

Two official sources tell the origin, and both tell it as something that is said, not as a fact.

- **The farm ministry.** Hegi soba is said to have begun in the Uonuma area of Niigata, born where the local textile trade met the local soba. *Funori* was used to stretch and stiffen the weft threads in weaving, and the idea of making soba with this familiar seaweed is said to be how it started.
- **The Cultural Agency.** Its 100-year food page says the dish is said to have been born when *funori*, used to size kimono thread, was put into soba as a binder in place of something else. It describes the dish as the meeting of soba, a food that kept, with the kimono trade, which was winter work.

The two accounts point the same way — thread, seaweed, soba — and differ in detail: the ministry speaks of weft threads and weaving, the agency of kimono thread and winter work. Neither gives a date, and this page gives none.

**The 100-year food.** The Cultural Agency lists hegi soba among Niigata's 100-year foods in the modern category, with a special award from its expert panel. The entry shows two fiscal years, Reiwa 3 and Reiwa 4 (2021 and 2022), and the agency's page does not make clear whether that is one entry or two. This page records both years.

## What makes it hegi soba

**The binder.** *Funori*, a seaweed. This is the defining feature in both official accounts, and the one that ties the dish to the looms.

**The tray.** A *hegi* is a square vessel made from split board — the word gives the dish its name. The ministry describes the soba as laid out on it.

**The condiments.** Spring onion and mustard. The ministry says Uonuma could not grow wasabi, so the habit there was to eat soba with mustard. At [Jindaiji](/en/eat/soba-jindaiji/) in Tokyo, by contrast, the local soba union names spring onion and wasabi as the basics.

**The reach.** The ministry gives the area as the whole prefecture, while placing the origin in Uonuma.

## Why Niigata

The official sources answer this through the textile trade. The ministry speaks of weaving culture meeting soba culture; the Cultural Agency of winter work in the kimono trade meeting a food that kept. Niigata's other 100-year foods point at the same snowbound winters: the agency also lists a snow-country fermented and preserved food culture of Nagaoka and Chūetsu, and the winter dishes of snow country. Hegi soba sits in that list as the one that came from the loom.

## How to eat it

1. **Look for the tray.** The square wooden *hegi* is what the dish is named after.
2. **Reach for the mustard.** Spring onion and mustard are the condiments the ministry names, in place of wasabi.
3. **Ask for hegi soba by name.** The ministry says it is served mainly at soba shops, and the name is the dish.

For soba at the turn of the year, Niigata's included, see [New Year's Eve food](/en/eat/seasonal-omisoka/).

## Hegi soba away from Niigata

Niigata's Tokyo shop changed in 2023. Nespas in Omotesandō closed on 25 December 2023, and its successor, Ginza Niigata Jōhōkan THE NIIGATA, opened in 2024 at 5-6-7 Ginza. The prefectural tourism association describes two floors of Niigata produce — rice, sake, sweets, kitchen knives — a takeaway counter for rice balls, a paid tasting corner with about 40 Niigata sakes, and on the eighth floor Bit, an Italian restaurant from Tsubame-Sanjō. That description does not mention hegi soba, and the restaurant is Italian, not a local-dish restaurant. The tray, the seaweed and the mustard are things to look for in Niigata itself.

## Getting there

The prefecture's travel site says you are inside Niigata 1 hour 20 minutes after leaving Tokyo: about 50 minutes to Takasaki, then about 30 minutes to Echigo-Yuzawa. There are two shinkansen routes in — the Jōetsu line through Echigo-Yuzawa and Nagaoka to Niigata, and the Hokuriku line through Nagano to Jōetsumyōkō. The site gives no time from Tokyo to Niigata station itself. Check current timetables with the operator before you travel.

## What people get wrong

- **"The seaweed is a garnish."** In the official accounts *funori* is the binder, worked into the soba.
- **"Hegi is a kind of buckwheat."** *Hegi* is the square tray of split wood the soba is served on.
- **"The origin is settled."** Both the ministry and the Cultural Agency tell it as "said", and neither gives a date.
- **"It comes with wasabi."** Uonuma's habit, in the ministry's account, is mustard.
- **"It is only an Uonuma dish."** The ministry places its origin there but lists it as eaten across the prefecture.
