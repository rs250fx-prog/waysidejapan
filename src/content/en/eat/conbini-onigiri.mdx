JNTO describes the convenience-store *onigiri* as a fist-sized rice ball covered in dry seaweed, and the franchise association names rice balls among the strongest sellers of 2025. The hard part, for the chains, is the seaweed. Nori that sits against damp rice goes limp, and each chain publishes what it does to stop that. This page covers that engineering, how to keep one, what goes inside, and what it costs.

For convenience stores in general — tax, payment, heating — start from the konbini overview.

## Where it came from

Seven-Eleven says it began selling rice balls in 1978, four years after its first store. I have not found an official history of rice balls in convenience stores beyond that.

## What makes it a konbini onigiri

**Crisp nori, by design.** The chains describe it differently:

- **Seven-Eleven** says crisp nori is the whole point of its hand-rolled rice ball, and that the top of the wrapping film is built to keep outside air away. In February 2020 it renewed the line completely, changing the film for the first time in five years to seal more tightly so the nori stays crisp and fragrant longer, and changing how it polishes rice for the first time in 14 years.
- **FamilyMart** said in 2019 that it toasts its nori in three stages, from low to high heat, uses a three-layer moisture-proof film, keeps packaging moisture-proof from the nori maker to the rice-ball factory, added a clear triangular seal to make it easier to open, and uses nori from the Ariake Sea as standard. In 2015 it described improving the film structure to keep texture and flavour.
- **Lawson**, on its 50th-anniversary site, says it changed its hand-rolled rice-ball machines for the first time in 13 years to make the rice fluffier.

How the film actually separates nori from rice until you open it is not explained on the official pages I read, so I do not describe it.

**Soft rice, kept warm-ish.** Seven-Eleven says rice in a refrigerator turns hard and crumbly, so it keeps rice balls at about 20°C from production to sale. After buying, it says, keep them at room temperature, about 15–25°C: in a fridge the starch hardens and the rice dries out.

**Heating.** Seven-Eleven heats items that show a time for its 1,500 W ovens; items marked only with household oven times are generally not heated. Its pages do not say whether rice balls are heated, so I do not either.

## What goes inside

JNTO's examples: tuna, salmon, pickled plum (*ume*), cod roe (*tarako*) and pickles, with a reminder to check labels for fish, mayonnaise, dashi, mirin or alcohol. Seven-Eleven's hand-rolled line, in October 2026: spicy cod roe, Kishū Nankō pickled plum, Hokkaido kombu, tuna mayonnaise, charcoal-grilled salmon, and seasoned-nori prawn mayonnaise. Its wrapped-style rice balls include pork and egg (*pōku tamago*), soy salmon and mixed chicken; it also sells a plain salted rice ball, wakame rice and red-bean rice.

The figures above are kept on the price reference page and update there. Seven-Eleven's list in October 2026, tax included at the takeaway rate of 8% (it shows decimals; tax is calculated on the total at the till):

- Spicy cod roe, grilled salmon: ¥213.84 (¥198 before tax).
- Pickled plum, kombu, tuna mayonnaise: ¥196.56 (¥182).
- Seasoned-nori prawn mayonnaise: ¥224.64 (¥208).
- Pork and egg: ¥340.20 (¥315); with tuna mayonnaise ¥378 (¥350).
- Plain salted rice ball: ¥145.80 (¥135). Wakame rice: ¥159.84 (¥148).

Seven-Eleven notes that size, price and release dates may vary by region, and that items may not be stocked or may end early. I did not check other chains' prices.

## How to eat it

- **Keep it out of the fridge**, as Seven-Eleven advises.
- **Read the label** if you avoid fish or mayonnaise; JNTO points out that tuna-mayonnaise and dashi are common.

## The same rice ball, a different region

- **Some lines are regional.** Seven-Eleven marks the regions for each item: the pork-and-egg rice ball for Okinawa, the seasoned-nori prawn mayonnaise for Kyushu, for example. Its list is of new and rolling releases, so I would not read it as a permanent regional map.
- **The engineering is national.** Film, nori and rice handling are chain-wide.

## What people get wrong

- **"Refrigerate it to keep it fresh."** The chain says that is how rice goes hard.
- **"The nori is soggy by design."** The hand-rolled kind is built to keep it crisp until you open it.
- **"A rice ball costs ¥100."** At Seven-Eleven in October 2026, the cheapest was ¥145.80.
- **"Every onigiri is vegetarian."** Many fillings are fish or contain mayonnaise; check the label.
