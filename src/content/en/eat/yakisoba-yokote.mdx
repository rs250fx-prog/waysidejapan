Yokote, in Akita, is a yakisoba town with a written definition. The name "Yokote yakisoba" is a regional collective trademark, and the Patent Office's entry for it describes the dish: the noodle, the toppings, the egg, the pickles and the sauce. This page is built almost entirely on that entry and on the B-1 Grand Prix's own record, because those are what I could confirm.

For yakisoba in general, start from the yakisoba overview.

## Where it came from

The Patent Office page says Yokote yakisoba's roots are said to lie in the period just after the Second World War, when a man who ran an okonomiyaki stall was looking for a new dish he could make on his okonomiyaki griddle. It gives no name and no year, and I found neither elsewhere. This page leaves it there.

## What makes it Yokote yakisoba

The trademark entry, held by the Yokote Yakisoba Noren-kai cooperative (*Kyōdō Kumiai Yokote Yakisoba Norenkai*) and last updated on 16 March 2020, describes:

- **The noodle.** Thick, straight *boiled* noodles (*yude-men*).
- **What goes in.** Generally cabbage and minced pork.
- **The egg.** A half-cooked fried egg laid on top of the noodles.
- **On the side.** *Fukujinzuke* pickles.
- **The sauce.** Worcestershire sauce with each shop's own original additions, with the heat held back to give a gentle taste.

So the definition is specific about the plate, and leaves the sauce to each shop within a Worcestershire base.

## The contest

On the B-1 Grand Prix's own record, a Yokote group, Yokote Yakisoba Sanrai's, took silver at the first contest in 2006 and gold at the fourth, held in Yokote in 2009.

Ota city's page also records that Yokote, Fujinomiya and Ota, then the only three self-styled yakisoba towns in Japan, joined in an exchange named the *Sankoku Dōmen*.

## The shops, and what they charge

I could not confirm a tax-included price at any Yokote shop on 2026-10-10, so no price is given.

## How to eat it

The sources describe the plate, not a ritual. The egg sits on top of the noodles, and the pickles are served alongside. How to combine them, I found no source for, and this page does not prescribe one.

## Not all yakisoba is this

The other two towns of the exchange are defined differently. Fujinomiya's city page describes *steamed* noodles cooled and coated in oil, with pork crackling and sardine powder. Ota's city and shop association describe no common recipe, only that each shop's taste is its own. Yokote's is the only one of the three I found set out in a trademark entry.

## Yokote yakisoba away from Yokote

The Patent Office page says the dedicated noodles and sauce were sold mainly within Yokote and were hard to buy outside it, and that they are now carried by supermarkets and other shops in Akita prefecture. How easy they are to find outside Akita, in Tokyo or elsewhere, I could not find.

Fares and travel times are not on this page yet.

## What people get wrong

- **"It is yakisoba with an egg on it."** The trademark entry also specifies the noodle — thick, straight and boiled — the usual cabbage and minced pork, the fukujinzuke and a mild Worcestershire-based sauce.
- **"The sauce is a single recipe."** The entry says each shop adds its own original elements to a Worcestershire base.
- **"You can buy the noodles anywhere."** They were hard to get outside Yokote; the Patent Office says they are now in shops across Akita prefecture. Beyond that, I could not confirm.
