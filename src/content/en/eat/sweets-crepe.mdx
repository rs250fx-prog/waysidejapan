The crepe you see on Harajuku’s Takeshita Street is rolled into a cone of paper, filled with whipped cream or ice cream, and eaten walking. In France, according to the company that says it started the Japanese version, crepes were mostly served on a plate. This page is that company’s story — it is the only account I could find — with the dates and prices it publishes.

For sweets in Japan beyond wagashi in general, start from the sweets overview.

## Where it came from

Marion Crepes (株式会社マリオン) tells it this way on its history page and in a press release of 14 September 2026:

- **The founder.** Kishi Iwao spent about seven years in France after university. When he came back, crepes in France were mainly served on a plate; he sold them instead rolled in paper, to be held in the hand. The company calls this said to be Japan’s first walking-eating style.
- **1976.** The first stand opened in a corner of a car park on Kōen-dōri in Shibuya.
- **1977.** A shop opened on Takeshita Street, Harajuku.

One caution: the history page’s wording ("two years later, in 1976") does not make clear how that date relates to the founder’s return. I give 1976 and 1977 as the company’s dates.

## What makes it a Japanese crepe

**The paper.** By the company’s account, the defining change was the format: from a plated dish to a paper-wrapped one you carry.

**The filling changed after.** Marion says its first crepes had a grown-up flavour, sprinkled with Western spirits. Later it moved to whipped cream and ice cream, and the company says this created a new genre, the "Japanese crepe".

**The anniversary revivals.** For its 50th anniversary Marion is reviving five early recipes — rum butter sugar, rum raisin cheese, rum raisin chocolate, rum chocolate, and Grand Marnier marmalade — at six shops including Takeshita Street, from 15 September to late October 2026. All five use alcohol, the company notes.

## The shops, and what they charge

The company says it has about 100 shops across Japan; the page does not say when that figure was written, so treat it as approximate.

The one tax-included price I could confirm is a promotion. On 21 October 2026, the founding anniversary, the banana-chocolate-cream crepe — first in a customer poll the company ran — is ¥390 at shops nationwide (some excluded), with a coupon from the official app. That is a one-day anniversary price, not the menu price. I could not find the normal price on Marion’s official pages, so it is not given.

## How to order it

- **Expect to hold it.** The paper wrap is the point of the format, by the company’s account.
- **Check for alcohol** if that matters to you: the revival crepes all use it.
- **For the ¥390 day**, you need the official app’s coupon, and some shops are excluded.

## Not all of Japan’s street sweets are this

The crepe is the street sweet with the clearest documented history I could find. Others have their own pages or none:

- **Roast sweet potato** has its own page, with the Edo-era roast-potato sellers and Kawagoe.
- **Taiyaki and imagawayaki** are covered with wagashi. Where taiyaki began, and how its names differ by region, I could not confirm.
- **Warabi-mochi** is on the sweet-shop page; sold from stalls, I could not confirm it from an official source.
- **Convenience stores** now sell crepes too: FamilyMart lists a raw-chocolate crepe at ¥270 tax included from 20 October 2026 (see the convenience-store sweets page).

## The crepe away from Harajuku

I could not find a source comparing Harajuku crepes with crepes elsewhere in Japan. The only "home and away" comparison in the sources is the company’s own: France served crepes on a plate, and Japan made a paper-wrapped one to carry.

## What people get wrong

- **"The Japanese crepe is the French crepe."** By Marion’s account, it was reshaped for Japan — rolled in paper and, later, filled with cream and ice cream.
- **"Harajuku came first."** The company’s first stand was in Shibuya in 1976; Harajuku followed in 1977.
- **"Japanese crepes were always sweet and childlike."** The company says its first ones were sprinkled with Western spirits.
- **"¥390 is the price."** It is the price for one anniversary day, with an app coupon.
