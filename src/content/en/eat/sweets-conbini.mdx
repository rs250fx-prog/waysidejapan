Japan’s convenience stores sell their own cakes, cream puffs and puddings, and the line-up keeps changing. This page does one narrow job: what the sweets cost at the three big chains on 10 October 2026, tax included, and how each chain brings new ones in. The store itself — what it is, how it works, the other food — is on the convenience-store overview.

For sweets in Japan beyond wagashi in general, start from the sweets overview.

## Where it came from

I have no source on the history of convenience-store sweets, so this page does not tell one. What the sources do show is the present: each chain names its own dessert line, publishes prices to the yen, and announces new items on a schedule.

## What makes it convenience-store sweets

**Three familiar formats.** Roll cakes, cream puffs and puddings are what the chains’ pages used here list most often. That is a description of the pages on one day, not a survey.

**Prices vary by region.** Each chain notes that prices may differ by shop or area. Lawson shows separate Okinawa prices for several items, and one item is not sold there at all.

**Some items are regional.** Seven-Eleven limits many sweets to a few prefectures, so the same chain stocks different puddings in different parts of Japan.

## The shops, and what they charge

All prices are tax included, as posted on each chain’s official page on 10 October 2026. As of that date, food taken away is taxed at 8% and food eaten in at 10%.

**Lawson** (its dessert page and the Uchi Café brand):

- Premium roll cake ¥214; premium roll cake with chestnut flour ¥275 (¥289 in Okinawa); Uchi Café × Milk MILK roll cake ¥279.
- Big twin cream puff ¥157; vanilla custard cookie puff ¥214 (¥243 in Okinawa); custard pie puff ¥265; twelve smooth-custard mini puffs ¥322; Uchi Café × Milk "like drinking fresh cream" puff ¥257 (not sold in Okinawa).
- Moon-viewing sweet-potato pudding ¥281 (¥297 in Okinawa). It was the only pudding on the page that day.

**FamilyMart** (a news release of 6 October 2026 — all of these were still to be released when checked). The chain says it has renewed its whipped cream for the first time in eight years, across about 16,500 shops.

- From 13 October: cream-filled double cream puff ¥199; fresh-cream roll cake ¥213 (new); "almost all cream" cake ¥248 (new).
- From 20 October: double-cream éclair ¥298; melting pudding ¥248; raw-chocolate crepe ¥270.

**Seven-Eleven** (its sweets and Western-sweets pages; prices shown with decimals, as listed):

- Melting milk pudding with Shizuoka tea ¥291.60 — from 7 October 2026, Yamanashi and Shizuoka.
- Milk zenzai pudding supervised by the Izumo Zenzai Society ¥321.84 — from 9 October, Tottori, Shimane, Hiroshima and Yamaguchi.
- Smooth kiln-baked pudding ¥267.84 — Ishikawa, Fukui, Gifu, Aichi, Mie, Tottori, Shimane, Hiroshima and Yamaguchi.
- Smooth custard pudding with selected eggs ¥267.84 — Tōhoku and Nagano.
- Vanilla custard cream puff ¥172.80 — Tokyo and Kanagawa.

I could see only the first 15 of 64 items on Seven-Eleven’s Western-sweets page, and no roll cake was among them. That does not mean it sells none.

## How to order it

There is nothing to order: you take it from the chiller. Two things are worth knowing.

- **The shelf you see is the shelf for that region.** Seven-Eleven’s puddings above are a good example.
- **New things arrive on set days.** See below.

## How the chains rotate them

Each chain brings in new sweets in its own rhythm, on the evidence of its October 2026 announcements:

- **Lawson — two a week, on Tuesdays.** Uchi Café × Milk launched two new items each Tuesday for three weeks running: 29 September, 6 October and 13 October. On 6 October they were a MILK dora-mocchi (¥268) and the MILK roll cake (¥279); on 13 October, among others, a melting fresh-cream cheese tart and a melting MILK cheesecake (¥297 each). The page reads as a rotating line-up.
- **FamilyMart — staggered a week apart.** The new cream line goes out on 13 October and then 20 October.
- **Seven-Eleven — day by day, region by region.** New items are released "in sequence" from given dates, in specified prefectures.

How many weeks an item stays, or when it is withdrawn, none of the three says on the pages I read. I can only report the release dates.

## Not all of Japan’s convenience-store sweets are this

The prices above are for the three chains’ own lines on one date. Other chains, and the other food in the stores, are on the convenience-store overview.

## Convenience-store sweets away from home

The nearest thing to "home and away" here is the region. A Seven-Eleven in Shizuoka and one in Shimane on the same week sold different puddings built on local names — Shizuoka tea in one, Izumo zenzai in the other. Lawson in Okinawa charges more for some items and does not stock one. Those are differences in what is sold where, not in quality.

## What people get wrong

- **"Convenience-store sweets are the same everywhere."** Prices and products differ by region.
- **"The price on the shelf is the price on this page."** Prices are as posted on 10 October 2026, tax included; the chains say they can vary by shop.
- **"That sweet will be there next week."** The chains release new items weekly or in stages and do not say how long each stays.
