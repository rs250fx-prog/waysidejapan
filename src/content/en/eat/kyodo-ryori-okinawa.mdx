Okinawa's food is not mainland Japanese food with a tropical accent. It grew from the Ryūkyū kingdom's own history, and above all from pork. The farm ministry's database lists 30 Okinawan dishes — among them *nakami-jiru* (offal soup), *inamudochi*, *kufa-jūshī* rice, rafute, *sōki-jiru*, gōyā chanpurū, *mimigā* (pig's-ear) sashimi, peanut tofu, pig's-trotter *tibichi*, Okinawa soba, mozuku rice bowls and goat soup. This page covers three of them.

For how Japan records local dishes, start from the local-dishes overview.

## Why pork

The farm ministry's rafute page: Ryūkyū cooking is said to "begin with pork and end with pork", using the meat, organs, face, ears and even blood. Pork cooking developed under the influence of the kingdom's exchange with China. Japan's taboo on meat reached Okinawa but did not take root, and pork is used even at memorial services. Before refrigerators it was a preserved food, strongly seasoned; the seasoning has grown lighter.

## Rafute

Skin-on pork belly braised for a long time in sugar, soy sauce and awamori. The ministry gives the ideal total cooking time as six to seven hours. Today it is a dish for guests at celebrations and memorial services, made at home, served in local restaurants, and sold in retort pouches at supermarkets and souvenir shops. The Cultural Agency made it a traditional 100-year food in 2021 with a special award, noting that it was made at home as preserved food and served at celebrations and funerals, and that the Motobu tourism association works to keep the traditional taste.

## Okinawa soba

**The bowl.** Thick noodles of wheat flour only, kneaded with *kansui*; a rich stock of pork bones and bonito flakes; topped with simmered pork belly, a stick of fish cake, spring onion and pickled red ginger. *Kōrēgusu*, island chillies steeped in awamori, is added to taste. Miyako and Yaeyama soba have their own noodles and toppings. It is everyday food, and at home people often use bought noodles.

**Where it came from.** The farm ministry gives one account: in the mid-Meiji era a man from China opened a noodle shop in Naha.

**The name.** In 1976 the Fair Trade Commission ruled that it could not be called *soba*, since it contains no buckwheat. The Okinawa noodle makers' cooperative negotiated repeatedly to protect the name and the food culture, and won a trademark registration for "Okinawa soba". 17 October is Okinawa Soba Day in its honour.

## Tibichi

Pig's trotters, a dish from the Ryūkyū kingdom. The Cultural Agency notes that after the war, in 1948, Okinawan emigrants in Hawaii sent pigs to what is now Uruma city, helping the dish survive; Uruma marks 27 September as its day. The agency made *tibichi* dishes a traditional 100-year food in 2023, with a special award.

## Okinawa's 100-year foods

Five: rafute (2021, traditional, special award), taro (*tānmu*) dishes (2022, modern), Katsuren mozuku tempura (2022, future), tibichi dishes (2023, traditional, special award), *bukubuku-chā* frothed tea (2024, traditional).

## Okinawan food away from Okinawa

- **The Tokyo shop.** Ginza Washita Shop in the Tokyo Kōtsū Kaikan, Yūrakuchō, is run by the prefecture's trading company: about 4,000 products, 10:00–20:00, with an eat-in corner. The company lists ten shops nationwide.
- **Take-home forms.** Rafute in retort pouches and Okinawa soba gift sets exist at the source, which is part of why the food travels.
- **Occasion.** At home, rafute belongs to celebrations and memorial rites, and soba to every day.
- **No taste comparison.** I found no official one, and make none.

## What people get wrong

- **"Okinawa soba is buckwheat soba."** It is wheat noodles; the name was defended by trademark.
- **"Rafute is just pork belly."** It is a celebration and memorial dish, braised for hours with awamori.
- **"Okinawan food is mainland food."** Its pork cooking grew from the Ryūkyū kingdom's ties with China.
