In Fukui, soba is a cold dish with grated daikon radish on it, and it turns up at the end of a wedding, after a Buddhist service, on New Year's Eve and late at night at the Jōdo Shinshū memorial gathering. The farm ministry lists it simply as *oroshi soba* — *oroshi* is grated daikon — and says it is eaten across the prefecture, mainly in the Reihoku area. For the noodle in general, see [soba](/en/eat/soba/); for Fukui's other dishes, the [Hokuriku overview](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-hokuriku/).

## Where it came from

The farm ministry tells the story in three steps.

1. **The warlord.** Buckwheat is held to have first been grown as an emergency food for wartime when Asakura Takakage made his castle at Ichijōdani, from 1473.
2. **The lord.** Later, Honda Tomimasa, lord of the castle at Fuchū from 1601, came with soba makers, and thin soba topped with grated daikon spread.
3. **The name.** The name "Echizen oroshi soba" spread nationwide, the ministry says, from words the Shōwa emperor spoke after eating it in Fukui.

The first step is told as "held to be". This page records the ministry's account as one account and adds no other.

## What makes it Echizen oroshi soba

**The daikon.** Grated daikon goes with the cold broth. That is the dish.

**Three ways to add it.** The ministry says Fukui has three: the broth and the grated daikon kept separate; the grated daikon put into the broth; or the juice squeezed from the grated daikon put into the broth. A writer on Fukui's tourism site makes the same point about the third, saying that in some parts of Fukui soba with the squeezed juice of grated daikon in the broth is what people call oroshi soba.

**The noodle.** Thin, in the ministry's account of the soba Honda Tomimasa's makers spread.

**The occasions.** The close of wedding feasts and Buddhist services, toshikoshi soba on New Year's Eve, and the late-night meal at *hōonkō*, which in Fukui is called *honkosan* or *okosama*. In the Tannan area, the ministry adds, it is also served at a wedding when the parents of the couple leave.

## Why Fukui

The ministry's own explanation is the history above: a warlord's emergency crop and a lord's soba makers. Its pages add the religious setting. Jōdo Shinshū is the most widely followed school in Reihoku, and *honkosan* brings a one-soup, three-dish vegetarian meal — dishes such as simmered thick-fried tofu, *sukō* and *gojiru* — which has also become everyday home cooking. Oroshi soba is the late-night part of that gathering. Fukui's area story also describes long winters when much of the prefecture is shut in by snow, which gave it a culture of preserved foods such as pickles and heshiko. For Fukui's New Year's Eve soba alongside other regions', see [New Year's Eve food](/en/eat/seasonal-omisoka/).

**Making it yourself.** Fukui's tourism federation, on its official travel site Fukui.com, lists a full soba-making session at Echizen Soba no Sato at ¥2,200 a batch, tax included, with one batch making two plates. A tempura platter can be added when booking, at ¥600 a person, tax included. These are the fees for the session, not the price of a meal out, as shown when checked on 11 October 2026. The page gives no update date, so the price may have changed.

**With sauce katsudon.** The Cultural Agency's 100-year food page for Fukui's sauce katsudon says Echizen oroshi soba and sauce katsudon are the classic pairing when eating out. Near Awara Onsen, the writer's piece on Fukui's tourism site (8 August 2023) points to a diner serving sauce katsudon and a soba shop known for *ten-oroshi* near Awara-Yunomachi station. For the katsudon, see [katsudon](/en/eat/donburi-katsudon/).

## How to eat it

1. **Expect it cold.** The ministry describes cold broth poured over with grated daikon.
2. **See which way the shop does it.** Daikon and broth apart, daikon in the broth, or the squeezed juice in the broth — all three are Fukui's.
3. **Order it after the katsudon, or before.** The Cultural Agency calls the two the standard pair.
4. **Go at harvest for new soba.** The ministry says new-soba festivals are held across the prefecture then.

## Echizen soba away from Fukui

Fukui's Tokyo shop, Fukui Shoku no Kuni 291 at 1-5-8 Ginza, has an eat-in counter, Echizen Wakasa Shoku to Sake Fuku to Homare, which its official site says serves Echizen soba and seafood rice bowls. The site lists weekday hours of 11:00–15:00 and 17:00–21:00, and 11:00–20:00 at weekends and on holidays, with irregular closing days, and it posted changes to the eat-in hours in September and October 2026, so check before going. The weddings, memorial services and *honkosan* nights where the dish belongs are in Fukui itself.

## Getting there

The Hokuriku shinkansen reached Fukui when the Kanazawa–Tsuruga section opened on 16 March 2024, JR West says. Fukui's tourism federation names four stations in the prefecture — Awara-Onsen, Fukui, Echizen-Takefu and Tsuruga — and, in an article written before the opening (4 March 2024), gives about 2 hours 51 minutes from Tokyo to Fukui. Timetables change; check with the operator before you travel.

## What people get wrong

- **"Oroshi soba is any soba with a spoon of daikon on top."** In Fukui it is a named local dish with its own occasions, and three accepted ways of adding the daikon.
- **"It is hot soba."** The ministry describes cold broth.
- **"Its history is settled."** The ministry tells the Asakura beginning as "held to be".
- **"Sauce katsudon is on the same official list."** Oroshi soba is on the farm ministry's list for Fukui; sauce katsudon is a Cultural Agency 100-year food instead.
- **"¥2,200 is a meal price."** It is the fee for a soba-making session.
