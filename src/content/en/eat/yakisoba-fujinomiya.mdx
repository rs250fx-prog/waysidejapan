Fujinomiya, in Shizuoka, is one of Japan's best-known yakisoba towns, and unlike some, it can tell you what its yakisoba is. The city describes a particular noodle and two particular toppings, and explains the noodle with a practical story about keeping food before refrigerators. This page sets out what the city and the town's yakisoba society say, and what I could not confirm.

For yakisoba in general, start from the yakisoba overview.

## Where it came from

The city's own page gives the origin as a matter of shops, not of one inventor. In the past many of the town's shops were called *yōshokuya* — Western-food shops — and in an age when cold storage was not yet common, the shops asked for a noodle that would keep, and a method that made one was established. The city writes this as what "is said". It gives no year, and I found none from another source.

What is dated is the organisation. The Fujinomiya Yakisoba Society (*Fujinomiya Yakisoba Gakkai*), whose website describes itself as official, says it was founded in 2000. I have not checked that claim against any third party, so the society is quoted here as a self-described body.

## What makes it Fujinomiya yakisoba

**The noodle.** The city says Fujinomiya's noodles are firmer than ordinary yakisoba noodles. Steamed noodles are forcibly cooled and their surface is coated in oil; this leaves little water in the noodle and gives it its particular bite. The society describes the same steps — steam, cool, coat in oil — and says the noodles come from four noodle makers in the city.

**Nikukasu.** The city names *nikukasu* as a defining topping. The society explains what it is: the pork back-fat left over after lard has been pressed out of it.

**Sardine powder.** Finished with a sprinkle of *dashi-ko*, a powder of dried sardine. Both the city and the society name the sardine.

That is the whole definition I can give from the sources. I found no official description of the sauce, the vegetables, or the method of frying, so none is given here.

## Recognition, and the contest

- **100-Year Food.** The city says Fujinomiya yakisoba was certified in fiscal 2021 (Reiwa 3) as one of the Agency for Cultural Affairs' 100-Year Foods, in the category for foods aiming at the next hundred years.
- **B-1 Grand Prix.** On the contest's own record, Fujinomiya won gold at the first B-1 Grand Prix (2006, held in Hachinohe) and again at the second (2007, held in Fujinomiya). At the fourth, held in Yokote in 2009, it placed tenth.
- **The three-town exchange.** Ota city's page says that Yokote, Fujinomiya and Ota, then the only three self-styled yakisoba towns in Japan, joined in an exchange named the *Sankoku Dōmen*.

## The shops, and what they charge

I could not confirm a tax-included price at any Fujinomiya shop on 2026-10-10, so no price is given.

## Not all yakisoba is this

Fujinomiya's definition is about the noodle and the toppings. The other two towns of the exchange describe themselves differently. Yokote's trademark describes thick, straight *boiled* noodles — not steamed — with minced pork, cabbage and a half-cooked fried egg. Ota's city and shop association describe no common recipe at all, only that each shop's taste is its own. So "regional yakisoba" can mean a defined dish or simply a town's shops.

## Fujinomiya yakisoba away from Fujinomiya

The society names four noodle makers in the city as the source of the noodles. I found no source that compares Fujinomiya yakisoba made in the town with yakisoba sold under the name elsewhere, and no rule on who may use the name, so this page does not say what changes.

## What people get wrong

- **"It is ordinary yakisoba with a town name."** The city describes a specific noodle — steamed, cooled, oil-coated, low in water — and two specific toppings.
- **"Nikukasu is bacon bits."** The society says it is pork back-fat left after the lard has been pressed out.
- **"The method has a known inventor and date."** The city says the method was established at shops' request before refrigeration was common. It names no person or year.
- **"The yakisoba society is a city body."** It describes itself as official; this page has not verified that, and quotes it as the society's own account.
