*Torimotsu-ni* is chicken offal, simmered and glazed, from Kōfu, the capital of Yamanashi. Its story is unusually specific for a local dish: a year, a soba shop and a butcher with a problem. It is also a case of a dish made famous on purpose, by a group of city staff. This page sets out what the farm ministry, Yamanashi’s tourism site, the Cultural Agency and the B-1 Grand Prix organisers say.

For B-grade local food in general, start from the [B-grade overview](/en/eat/bkyu/). Torimotsu-ni also appears on the [Chūbu local dishes page](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-chubu/).

## Where it came from

Two sources tell the same story from different sides, and both tell it as what is said.

- **The farm ministry.** Around 1950 (Shōwa 25), shortly after the war, a butcher asked the second-generation owner of a soba shop founded in 1913 (Taishō 2) whether something could be done with chicken offal that was being thrown away. That request, the ministry says, is said to be how the dish began.
- **Yamanashi’s official tourism site.** It names the shop: Okuto, a soba shop founded in 1913, where torimotsu-ni was born around 1950 before spreading around Kōfu.

The year comes from the shop’s side of the story. No independent record of it is in the sources used here, so this page gives it as the shop’s account.

## What goes in it

The farm ministry lists the parts: liver, gizzard, heart, and two more it calls *kinkan* and *himo*. They are cooked in soy sauce and sugar over strong heat for a short time, a method the ministry calls *teri-ni* — simmering to a glaze. So this is not a long stew. It is quick, sweet and salty, and shiny.

## Where it is eaten

The ministry says it is a standard at soba shops, *hōtō* shops and set-meal restaurants in the area, and that it can also be bought in supermarket deli sections. That fits its origin: a dish that began at a soba shop is still sold beside noodles.

## How it became known

**City staff.** The farm ministry says that from 2008 a volunteer group of Kōfu city office staff promoted torimotsu-ni as Kōfu’s B-grade local food.

“B-grade” is a loose label. The B-1 Grand Prix organisers say there is no clear definition of *B-kyū gurume*, only an image of food that is cheap and good, and that the B in their contest’s name stands for “brand”. They also say the contest is not a ranking of Japan’s best B-grade food, and that they no longer cooperate with projects billed as B-grade food.

**The contest.** On the B-1 Grand Prix organisers’ list, the gold at the fifth contest, held in Atsugi in 2010, went to a group called *Kōfu Torimotsu-ni de Minasama no En o Torimotsu-tai* — roughly, the team that brings everyone together with Kōfu torimotsu-ni. Yamanashi’s tourism site says only that the dish won gold “at a festival of local food”, without naming it.

The organisers say awards go to the town-promotion group that enters, not to the dish, and that visitors vote with chopsticks, two each. Many vote on taste; by the organisers’ own estimate, two or three in ten vote for something else, such as the group’s effort or how it treated the queue.

## Recognition

- **Farm ministry.** Torimotsu-ni is one of the 30 Yamanashi dishes in the ministry’s database of local dishes.
- **Cultural Agency.** It was listed as a 100-Year Food in fiscal 2024 (Reiwa 6), in the category for food culture of the modern era, not the traditional category used for Yamanashi’s *hōtō* and *Yoshida no udon*.

## Getting there

The Japan National Tourism Organization says Kōfu is about 90 minutes from Shinjuku by JR Chūō line limited express, and a little over two hours by highway bus from the Shinjuku bus terminal. Check fares and journey times with the operator before you travel.

## Torimotsu-ni away from Kōfu

In Tokyo, Yamanashi prefecture’s Tokyo office lists Fujitohachi (ふじとはち) in Ginza 4-chome, a restaurant serving local dishes of Yamanashi and Shizuoka, mostly B-grade food, and says torimotsu-ni is on its menu (page updated 18 May 2026). No source checked for this page compares the dish in Tokyo with the dish in Kōfu.

## What people get wrong

- **“It is a slow-cooked offal stew.”** The ministry describes a short cook over strong heat to a glaze.
- **“It is an old Edo-period dish.”** Its story starts around 1950, as told by the shop’s side.
- **“The B-1 gold went to the dish.”** It went to the promotion group, by the organisers’ rules.
- **“It is only restaurant food.”** The ministry says it is also sold in supermarket delis.
