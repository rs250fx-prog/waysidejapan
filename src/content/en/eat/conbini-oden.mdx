In the cold months many Japanese convenience stores set up a pot by the counter: radish, eggs, konjac, fish cakes and fried tofu simmering in broth, sold a piece at a time. JNTO calls it a staple winter dish at convenience stores across the country. It is cheap, it is hot, and it is one of the few konbini foods that openly changes from region to region.

For convenience stores in general — tax, payment, heating — start from the konbini overview.

## What it is

*Oden* is a simmered dish of many separate items in broth. At the convenience store it is a counter food, like fried chicken and steamed buns, in the franchise association's category of daily fresh food. Seven-Eleven says its counter-cooked items are meant to be eaten soon after buying.

**How it is served.** In September 2020 FamilyMart described its pot: kept covered and opened only to serve, every item served by staff, the pot placed on the counter rather than facing the aisle (at most stores). That was set out during the pandemic, and I have not found a newer description. I found no official standard for whether customers serve themselves at other chains, so ask, or watch.

**Packs.** Lawson sells a 550 g pack of oden — seven items in two kinds of bonito stock — at a standard ¥354 including tax, the price headquarters recommends to its stores; it may end without notice in some regions.

## When

JNTO places it in winter, next to the counter. The chains' product pages give no season. FamilyMart's 2020 oden went on sale from 15 September; Lawson Store 100 announced its "¥100 oden", eleven items, from 23 September 2026, at ¥100 a pack (¥108 with tax), sold only at Lawson Store 100. So "from early autumn into winter" is the pattern, but there is no single national start date.

## What it costs

The figures above are kept on the price reference page and update there. From the chains' own pages in October 2026, tax included at the takeaway rate of 8%:

- **FamilyMart**: triangle konjac, thick radish, grilled chikuwa, burdock roll (*gobō-maki*), *hanpen*, knotted shirataki ¥145; seasoned egg, fried tofu, rolled omelette, coarse sausage ¥150; rice-cake pouch (*mochi kinchaku*) ¥185; beef-tendon skewer ¥198.
- **Seven-Eleven**: seasoned egg ¥150.12, radish ¥130.68 (it shows decimals); 33 items in all.
- **Lawson Store 100**: ¥108 a pack.

**Eat in or take away.** The tax agency uses hot snacks as its example: food that can be eaten in or taken away is taxed according to what you intend, and stores may ask by sign. Eat it at an eat-in counter and the 10% rate applies.

## Why it changes by region

- **The broth.** FamilyMart said in 2020 that it blends its oden broth for four regions — East Japan (Hokkaido to Chūbu), West Japan (Hokuriku to Chūgoku–Shikoku), Kyushu and Okinawa — to suit each. Its grilled chikuwa and fried tofu differ by region, and Okinawa's sets and prices are different.
- **The items.** FamilyMart's current page lists where each item is sold: *gobō-maki* in Tōkai, Hokuriku, Kansai, Chūgoku, Shikoku, Kyushu and Okinawa; *hanpen* only in Hokkaido, Tōhoku and Kantō; fried tofu and beef-tendon skewers everywhere except Okinawa. Lawson Store 100 sells *chikuwabu* only in Kantō.
- **The same item, separate products.** Seven-Eleven lists its radish as one product for Ibaraki and another for Hokkaido, Tōhoku, Kantō, Tōkai, Kinki, Kyushu, Okinawa and elsewhere, and notes that size, price and release can vary by region.
- **Local ways to eat it.** JNTO says many convenience stores in Aomori serve oden with ginger miso, and describes Shizuoka's oden as beef-tendon stock.

I could not find an official account of *why* oden differs east to west, so this page records the differences, not their origins.

## How to order it

- **Ask staff for the items you want**; at FamilyMart in 2020, staff served every item.
- **Say if you will eat in**, because of the tax line.
- **In Aomori**, look for the ginger miso.

## What people get wrong

- **"Konbini oden is the same everywhere."** FamilyMart alone blends four broths and sells different items by region.
- **"It is sold all year."** It is a cold-season counter food; the chains announce it from early autumn.
- **"It costs ¥100 a piece."** That is Lawson Store 100's pack. At FamilyMart pieces were ¥145–198.
- **"Hanpen is everywhere."** At FamilyMart it is sold only in the north and east.
