Every other ramen in Kyushu can be traced, by someone's account, back to a cauldron in Kurume. Kagoshima's cannot. At the southern end of the island, the ramen is paler, softer and gentler than the rest, it comes with a dish of pickled radish and a small pot of tea that nobody charges you for, and the Ramen Museum records the local claim that it is the one Kyushu style that owes nothing to Kurume.

## Where it came from

The museum's Kagoshima page names the first shop as Noboruya, opened in 1947 by a woman named Michioka Tsuna, who had learned noodle-making in Yokohama; Komurasaki, the other founding name, followed in 1949. Both began within two years of Kurume's white soup, but from a different source — Yokohama rather than the Chikugo river — which is the basis of the claim that Kagoshima developed on its own.

## What makes it Kagoshima ramen

**The soup.** Pork bone is the base, but the museum says chicken carcasses and vegetables go in as well, and the result is "mild" rather than the thick, strong pork of Kurume.

**The noodle.** Soft, and whitish — made, the museum notes, without *kansui*, the alkaline water that gives most ramen noodles their yellow colour and spring. It is closer to a Chinese soup noodle than to anything else in Kyushu.

**On top.** Scorched spring onion (*kogashi negi*) is the distinctive addition, with pork and, at many shops, cabbage or bean sprouts.

**On the side.** Pickled daikon, free and refillable at most shops, and a small teapot of tea. The museum lists both as standard.

## Why Kagoshima

The founders learned in Yokohama, not Kurume, and the city at the far end of the island kept its own habits. Kagoshima is also pork country — the black-pig prefecture — which makes the gentleness of the soup more surprising, not less; this is a city that could have made the heaviest tonkotsu in Japan and chose not to.

## How to eat it

Eat the pickles between mouthfuls; drink the tea after. There is no refill and no hardness question, and the noodle is meant to be soft.

## Kagoshima ramen away from Kagoshima

Komurasaki has shops outside the prefecture, but the style has travelled less than any other Kyushu ramen, partly because it does not fit the "Kyushu = tonkotsu" story that sells elsewhere. The pickles and tea are the first thing to go at a shop outside Kagoshima; if they are on the counter, the shop has been paying attention.

## Getting there

Kagoshima-Chūō is the southern terminus of the Kyushu Shinkansen; the founding shops are in the city centre.

## What people get wrong

- **"Kyushu ramen is all one family."** Kagoshima is the exception the museum records.
- **"Soft noodles are overcooked."** Here they are made without lye water, and soft is the design.
- **"The pickles are a side order."** They are free, and they are part of the bowl.
