Banshū, in Hyōgo, home of the old Tatsuno domain, is the other great home of hand-stretched sōmen, sold under the cooperative brand *Ibo no Ito*. Its claim rests not on a legend but on records, and its makers describe a change in aged noodles that happens only through the rainy season.

For sōmen in general, start from the sōmen overview.

## Where it came from

The Hyōgo cooperative gives its history from documents:

- **1418** — the word "sōmen" appears in a temple diary of Ikaruga-dera.
- **1461** — sōmen was used to celebrate the building of the Iwa shrine.
- **The An'ei era (1771–80)** — production became serious.
- **The Bunka era (1804–18)** — the Tatsuno domain protected and fostered it, and it is thought to have become a producing area.

Its page does not say the craft came from Miwa, though the farm ministry and the Miwa cooperative say pilgrims carried it from Miwa to Banshū. This site does not choose which came first.

## How it is made

The cooperative says it uses a hand-stretching method handed down for about 600 years. Resting and twisting the noodle like a rope while stretching it, repeated over and over, builds the gluten structure. It is made, as a rule, from October to April. The cooperative says Ibo no Ito was registered as a geographical indication in August 2024; I have not checked the ministry's register.

**New and aged.** Noodles shipped in the year they are made are *shin*; noodles aged a year are *hine*. During the hot, humid rainy season, enzymes in the flour act on the noodles and the texture is said to improve further — a change the cooperative calls *yaku*.

## How to cook and eat it

- **Boil**: about a litre of water to two bundles (100 g), 1 minute 30 seconds to 2 minutes; drain; rub under running water.
- **For nyūmen or stir-frying**: 1 minute to 1 minute 30, because it will be heated again.
- **Beyond cold sōmen**: the cooperative suggests salads, thick-sauce dishes, dipping noodles and hot simmered noodles.

## Banshū sōmen away from Hyōgo

No official source compares Banshū sōmen eaten locally with the same packet elsewhere, so this page does not.

## What people get wrong

- **"Banshū learned sōmen from Miwa."** Miwa says so; Banshū's own history starts from a record of 1418.
- **"Older noodles are stale."** The cooperative says aged noodles improve through the rainy season.
