The hamburger came to Japan through two doors that are easy to tell apart on the record. One is a navy town in Nagasaki prefecture, where the city says local shops learned it from the American base. The other is the Japanese arm of an American chain, which opened in Ginza in 1971, and a Japanese chain that started a year later. Most of what is distinctively Japanese about the burger — teriyaki, rice buns, a katsu patty — is written down in the two chains’ own timelines.

For the wider group of Western foods made Japanese, start from the Western foods overview.

## Where it came from

**Sasebo.** The tourism site run by Sasebo’s convention and visitors association says Sasebo, once a base of the Imperial Japanese Navy, had a US Navy base after the war, and that making hamburgers began, around 1950, with recipes heard directly from the base. It says Sasebo is called the place where the hamburger arrived in Japan. The burgers were first sold at shops near the base to Americans; later many shops added their own touches, and a "Sasebo style" took shape.

Two shops are named on the same page:

- **Hikari**, founded in 1951 (Shōwa 26), is said to be the origin of the "jumbo burger", which it made for students.
- **Big Man**, founded in 1970, is said to be the shop where the bacon-and-egg burger began.

Whether Sasebo was the first place in Japan to make hamburgers, I could not settle; I found no official source for other claims to check it against. What can be said is that Sasebo’s tourism site gives "around 1950".

**McDonald’s Japan.** The company’s timeline says McDonald’s Company (Japan) was set up in May 1971, opened a Japanese branch of Hamburger University in June, and on 20 July 1971 opened its first restaurant, the Ginza shop, on the ground floor of Ginza Mitsukoshi. The first day’s menu was the hamburger, cheeseburger, Filet-O-Fish, Big Mac, fries, apple pie and shake. In July 1996 the company made 20 July "Hamburger Day".

**MOS Burger.** MOS Food Services’ timeline says it opened a test shop in Narimasu, Itabashi ward, Tokyo, in March 1972, and the first MOS Burger shop, Narimasu, in June 1972. The company was set up in July; the founder was Sakurada Satoshi.

## What makes it Japanese

The adaptations are dated on the companies’ own timelines.

| Year and month | Company | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May 1973 | MOS Burger | Teriyaki burger (the company calls it a big hit) |
| June 1984 | MOS Burger | Teriyaki chicken burger |
| December 1987 | MOS Burger | MOS rice burger |
| March 1989 | McDonald’s Japan | Teriyaki McBurger, which the company calls a Japan-original product |
| September 1989 | MOS Burger | Pork loin katsu burger |
| September 1991 | McDonald’s Japan | Tsukimi (moon-viewing) burger, limited time, by region |
| April 1996 | McDonald’s Japan | Teritama burger, limited time |
| February 2020 | McDonald’s Japan | Three "gohan" (rice) burgers, limited time, which the company calls a first in its history in Japan |

On these timelines MOS’s teriyaki burger comes 16 years before McDonald’s Japan’s. Whether MOS was the first in Japan, I did not check against other companies’ records, so this site does not say.

**Made to order, in Sasebo.** Sasebo’s tourism site sets three conditions for a Sasebo burger: it is made after the order is taken; the company has its head office in Sasebo; it uses fresh raw vegetables and an original sauce. It is a definition by method and by place, not by recipe.

**Growth.** McDonald’s Japan opened its 100th shop in December 1976 (Kisarazu) and its 1,000th in July 1993 (Mizuho-dōri). Its first drive-through was the Kanpachi Takaido shop in October 1977. MOS opened its first franchise shop, Aratama in Aichi prefecture, in November 1973, and says that in June 1986 it became the first in the restaurant industry to have shops in all 47 prefectures — the company’s own claim.

## How to order it

- **In Sasebo,** the local definition says the burger is made after the order is taken, at shops run from Sasebo. The tourism site describes individual shops, including Hikari and Big Man.
- **At the chains,** the Japan-only items listed above are, in several cases, limited-time products: the timeline describes the Tsukimi, Teritama and rice burgers as limited when they first appeared. What is on sale now is on each chain’s current menu.

Fares and travel times are not on this page yet.

## Not all of Japan’s burgers are this

- **Before 1971.** Apart from Sasebo’s account, I found no official source on hamburgers in Japan before McDonald’s Japan. That history is not on this page.
- **The Sasebo brand.** Accounts of Sasebo burgers being branded and sold under licence elsewhere circulate, but I could not confirm them from a primary source.

## Sasebo burgers away from Sasebo

Sasebo’s tourism site requires a head office in Sasebo, which works as a way of telling a Sasebo burger from one sold under the name somewhere else, such as Tokyo. It is a rule about who makes it, not a description of how burgers sold elsewhere differ. I found no official source on that.

For the chains, neither company’s official history compares its Japanese menu with the American one. The Japan-only items and their dates are what the record shows.

## What people get wrong

- **"McDonald’s brought the hamburger to Japan."** Sasebo’s tourism site dates the city’s burgers to around 1950, two decades before the Ginza opening.
- **"Sasebo was definitely first."** It is the city tourism site’s account. I could not check other claims, so this site does not decide.
- **"Teriyaki burgers started at McDonald’s."** On the timelines, MOS sold one in 1973 and McDonald’s Japan in 1989.
- **"A Sasebo burger is a recipe."** The tourism site defines it by being made to order, by a Sasebo-based company, with fresh vegetables and its own sauce.
- **"The Tsukimi burger has always been on the menu."** McDonald’s Japan’s timeline says it began in 1991 as a limited-time, regional product.
