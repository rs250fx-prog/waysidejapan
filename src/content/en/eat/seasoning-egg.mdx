In the United States, health authorities have advised against eating raw eggs. In Japan eggs are sold with a date that means "until this day, it can be eaten raw", and the farm ministry calls egg on rice a food culture particular to Japan. This page is about the system behind that, what the official safety advice actually says, how many eggs Japan eats, and the egg dishes the official sources describe.

For seasonings in general, including the soy sauce that goes on the egg, start from the seasoning overview.

## Where it came from

The farm ministry’s regional-food page for Tokyo says chicken and eggs were long forbidden as food at court, and eggs began to be eaten in the early Edo period when the ban was lifted — still as something precious. *Tamago fuwafuwa*, beaten egg poured into boiling dashi and steamed, was a treat of ordinary Edo people and is considered the origin of the rolled omelette. Raw eating has no date in the sources I read; what they explain is the modern system behind it.

## What makes Japanese eggs edible raw

The farm ministry’s magazine *aff* gives the reason: measures at every stage of production, processing and distribution to keep food-poisoning bacteria out — hygiene at farms and grading and packing plants (GP centres), and standards for shell eggs and liquid egg — plus consumers keeping eggs in the fridge and eating them soon after breaking, and a developed cold chain.

**The rules.** A 1998 health ministry notice, in force from November 1999, answered rising *Salmonella* Enteritidis poisoning from eggs. Restaurants serving shell eggs as they are must use eggs for raw eating within their date; otherwise egg dishes must be heated to 70 °C for at least a minute. Packs must show the date, that the eggs are for eating raw, that they need heating after the date, and that storage at 10 °C or below is desirable.

**The washing.** At the GP centre, *aff* says, eggs are washed with warm hypochlorite solution and brushes, dried, checked with light and for cracks by sound, sterilised with ultraviolet light, sorted and packed without human hands touching them. A health ministry HACCP guide (2019 draft) sets wash water at least 5 °C warmer than the eggs, because cold water could let salmonella through the shell.

**The date.** *Aff* says the best-before date is the date for eating raw, 21 days at most, including seven in a home fridge. The Japan Poultry Association says eggs past the date should be cooked and eaten soon, and cracked eggs not eaten raw.

**Do not wash them at home.** The Food Safety Commission says they are already washed; washing at home can carry bacteria in through the shell’s pores. Wipe off dirt, or wash just before cooking.

## Is it safe? The official answer

The Food Safety Commission’s 2017 Q&A notes the US CDC’s 2016 advice against raw or lightly cooked eggs, and reads it as reflecting that Americans do not have a habit of eating eggs raw. In Japan, it says, farm hygiene designed for raw eating, a date set for raw eating, and proper handling make it possible to eat raw eggs with peace of mind — if you refrigerate them at 10 °C or below, do not leave broken eggs out, and heat out-of-date or cracked ones. Older people, children aged two and under, pregnant women and people with weakened immunity should avoid raw eggs.

A commission member, writing in 2014, answered "is it safe?" with "yes and no". Washing removes contamination on the shell but not inside an egg from an infected hen. He cited an estimated contamination rate of about 0.0029% from a survey of about 100,000 eggs, noted estimates around 1990 were ten times higher, and said raw eggs are not 100% safe, though with correct storage and heating food poisoning is very unlikely.

**Out-of-date eggs, three numbers.** *Aff* says heat until the whole dish reaches 70 °C; the commission’s Q&A says 75 °C or more for at least a minute; the 1998 notice, 70 °C for at least a minute. This site does not choose.

**Bird flu.** The commission says that, as things stand in Japan, people cannot catch avian influenza from eating chicken or eggs; eggs from infected farms do not reach the market.

I could not find an official English page for visitors on raw eggs; the advice above is summarised from the Japanese originals.

## How many eggs Japan eats

A World Egg Organisation economist gave 2023 figures in September 2025: Mexico 392 eggs a person, Argentina 336, Colombia 324, Japan 320; world average 182 (2022). The Japan Poultry Association quotes the same 320, fourth in the world. The presenter warns that consumption is calculated, not measured, and a tally from FAO data gives Japan 331 and fifth. Treat the ranking as approximate.

The farm ministry’s food balance sheet gives 16.4 kg of egg per person in fiscal 2025. The poultry association says domestic production is 96%, and imports are mostly powdered egg for processing.

## How to eat it

**Tamago kake gohan.** *Aff* calls egg on rice a food culture of eating eggs raw particular to Japan. It introduces a "golden ratio" — one medium egg, 150 g of rice, 7 g of light soy sauce — and styles beyond mixing everything: "Typhoon", mixing only the white, and "Full moon", whipping the white to a meringue. These are one magazine’s suggestions. Its origin I could not confirm from an official page.

**Onsen tamago.** *Aff* explains it by temperature: egg white sets between 58 °C and 80 °C, the yolk between 65 °C and 70 °C, and the "hot-spring egg" uses the gap. Where it began I could not find in an official source.

## Not all of Japan’s egg is this

The clearest regional split is the rolled omelette. The ministry’s Tokyo page says *tamagoyaki* in Kantō means *atsuyaki tamago*, sweet and cooked until slightly browned, and in Kansai *dashimaki tamago*, with plenty of dashi, not browned, served with grated daikon. Its Kyoto page says rolling from near to far is *Kyō-maki* and far to near *Ōsaka-maki*, and that the dashi and seasoning differ by shop and household.

## What it costs

I could not confirm tax-included egg prices from an official source, so there are none here. *Aff* says egg prices barely changed for about 60 years — eggs were called the "honour student of prices" — before feed, electricity and bird flu pushed them up for a time; the poultry association says fiscal 2025 prices were higher than usual.

## Eggs away from the farm

I could not find an official comparison of farm-gate and supermarket eggs. What the sources show:

- **The supermarket egg is the system.** Shell eggs on sale are washed, inspected and packed to the national rules, and 96% are domestic.
- **Brand eggs exist.** About 1,700 of them, *aff* says.
- **Buying is uneven.** In the household survey’s 2023–25 city ranking, Tottori bought the most eggs by weight and Kōchi spent the most; Tokyo’s wards were 50th of 52 by weight. The survey covers eggs bought for home only and gives no reasons.

## What people get wrong

- **"Raw egg is always safe in Japan."** The commission’s member said "yes and no", and some people are advised to avoid it.
- **"Wash eggs before you use them."** Official advice in Japan is not to.
- **"The date is when the egg goes off."** It is the date for eating raw; after it, cook thoroughly.
- **"Japan eats the most eggs in the world."** On the 2023 figures it is fourth.
