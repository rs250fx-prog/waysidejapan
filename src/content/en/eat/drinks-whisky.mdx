"Japanese whisky" on a bottle is a promise, but not one made by law. The Liquor Tax Act says what whisky is; it does not say where it has to come from or how long it has to age. Since 2021 those questions have been answered by a standard the makers set for themselves. This page explains what the standard asks for, why the makers wrote it, and what to expect if you visit a distillery.

For drinks in Japan in general — the tax categories, the drinking age, customs — start from the [drinks overview](/en/eat/drinks/). The highball, the way most whisky in Japan is drunk, is on the [beer and highball page](/en/eat/drinks-beer-highball/).

## Where it came from

**A distillery from 1923.** Suntory says its Yamazaki Distillery, in Shimamoto, Osaka prefecture, began in 1923 from the wish of its founder, Torii Shinjirō, and calls it Japan’s oldest malt whisky distillery. It handles everything there, from raw materials through mashing, fermentation, distillation and storage to bottling.

**A model, and a different habit.** In 2021 the chairman of the Japan Spirits and Liqueurs Makers Association set out the background to the new standard. Japanese whisky, he wrote, began by taking Scotch as its model. But Japan has no custom, as Scotland has, of distilleries exchanging spirit with one another. Instead each company built up the technique to make many kinds of spirit itself — and some have distilleries abroad or have made use of imported spirit.

**A market that fell and came back.** Suntory’s own staff article says the whisky market peaked in 1983 and by 2007 had fallen to a sixth of that by volume. In 2008 the company began a project to bring back the highball; that story is told on the beer and highball page. These are the company’s figures.

## What makes it Japanese whisky

**What the law says.** In the Liquor Tax Act, whisky is a distilled spirit: sprouted grain and water, saccharified, fermented, then distilled. Nothing in that definition concerns country of origin or years in the cask.

**What the standard says.** The association’s standard on labelling Japanese whisky was set on 12 February 2021 and took effect on 1 April 2021. Under its Article 5, a whisky may be labelled Japanese whisky only if:

- its ingredients are malt, other grains, and water drawn in Japan — and malt must always be used;
- mashing, fermentation and distillation take place at a distillery in Japan, distilling to under 95% alcohol;
- it is filled into wooden casks of 700 litres or less and stored in Japan for at least three years from the day after filling;
- it is bottled in Japan, at 40% alcohol or more;
- caramel is used, if at all, only to adjust the colour.

**What a non-qualifying whisky may not say.** Article 6 goes further than the name. A whisky that does not meet the conditions may not use "Japan whisky" or similar terms, translations into other languages, or wording such as "-type" or "-style". Nor may it use Japanese personal names, Japanese place names, the national flag or era names that call Japan to mind — unless it makes clear that it does not meet the standard.

**Why.** The chairman’s 2021 statement gives two cases the association saw as confusing customers: whisky made only from foreign spirit being exported and sold as Japanese whisky, and brands that would not count as whisky under the Liquor Tax Act being sold abroad as whisky.

**Transition and logo.** Whiskies that had used such labels before 31 March 2021 could carry on as before until 31 March 2024. On 27 March 2025 the association adopted a logo for the standard, to make it more effective.

## How to drink it

**As a highball, mostly.** Suntory’s serving rules for its revived highball, from the same staff article, are a mug full of ice, chilled soda poured gently, and one part whisky to four of soda, which keeps it to 7–8% alcohol. The [izakaya overview](/en/eat/izakaya/) covers ordering drinks on a night out.

**Twenty, not eighteen.** Drinking under 20 is prohibited by a 1922 law, and anyone selling or serving alcohol must not do so knowing the drinker is under 20, and must take steps such as checking age. Japan’s age of adulthood became 18 on 1 April 2022, but the drinking age stayed at 20. In March 2022 the police agency, the health ministry and the tax agency asked sellers to check age by asking for documents such as a driving licence or a My Number card.

**Bringing bottles home.** For a traveller entering Japan, Japan Customs allows three bottles of alcohol, counted at 760 ml each, duty-free for personal use; anyone under 20 gets no alcohol allowance. What you may take into your own country is set by your own country.

## Not all whisky is this

Whisky that misses the standard is still whisky in tax law. It may simply not call itself Japanese whisky, or dress itself in Japanese names, without saying so. The standard is the association’s rule, applied by the association; it is not a law.

## Japanese whisky away from the distillery

**At the source, you book.** Suntory’s tour Q&A says its factory tours need a reservation except for walk-in courses, and that bookings are by lottery or first-come depending on the tour. Entry itself is free, but some tours charge. For moderate drinking, tours that include tasting cannot be combined on the same day. Guiding is in Japanese only; Yamazaki offers a free audio-guide app. Yamazaki is open 10:00–16:45, with last entry at 16:30.

**Age at the distillery.** The Q&A says under-20s who have finished junior high school may join tours at factories that do not serve alcohol. It does not say the same for factories that do, such as Yamazaki, and the tour fee is not on the pages used here, so this page gives neither.

**The highball spread outward.** By Suntory’s account, its revival worked through izakaya and restaurants — the beer-style mug and the serving rules — rather than through distilleries.

No official source checked for this page compares drinking whisky at a distillery with drinking it in Tokyo, so this page makes no such comparison. Check fares and journey times with the operator before you travel.

## What people get wrong

- **"Japanese whisky is defined by law."** It is defined by a makers’ association’s voluntary standard.
- **"A Japanese name on the bottle means it was made in Japan."** Under the standard, a non-qualifying whisky may use one only if it makes clear that it does not qualify.
- **"Any whisky bottled in Japan counts."** Mashing, fermentation and distillation must also be in Japan, with at least three years in wood in Japan.
- **"Japanese distilleries swap spirit, as in Scotland."** The association says Japan has no custom of distilleries exchanging spirit; each company makes its own range.
- **"Eighteen is old enough now."** Adulthood moved to 18 in 2022; the drinking age stayed at 20.
