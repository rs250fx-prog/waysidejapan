In Kumamoto and Ōita the flour goes into the soup not as noodles but as dumplings — torn, stretched, or wrapped round sweet potato. *Dagojiru* (Kumamoto) and *dangojiru* (Ōita) are home food, and the farm ministry says every household makes them differently. This page sits with udon because it is wheat dough in broth; it is really a family of soups.

For udon in general, start from the udon overview.

## What it is

**Kumamoto.** The farm ministry: *dago* means dumpling; dagojiru is flour dumplings simmered with vegetables from the field — burdock, radish, carrot — seasoned with miso or soy. It is one of the local dishes that used other grains in place of rice. Ingredients and seasoning differ by area and household; it is a "mother's taste".

**Ōita.** The ministry calls dangojiru Ōita's representative local dish: flour dough kneaded and stretched into strips, simmered with seasonal vegetables in a miso broth — barley miso — and long everyday food across the prefecture.

## Kumamoto's five kinds

The ministry lists them:

- **Tsukkiri-dago** — the standard: the dough torn off as it is stretched.
- **Nobe-dago** (Aso) — flat, long and noodle-like, made with rice flour because little wheat is grown there.
- **Ikinari dagojiru** (around Kumamoto city) — sweet potato wrapped in flour dough.
- **Ohimesama dagojiru** (Kikuchi and Kamoto).
- **Sen-dago-jiru** (Amakusa) — made with grated potato, said to have begun when the missionary Father Ferrié fed it to children at an orphanage in Kawaura.

## Why it is eaten

Another ministry page says it is quick, nutritious and filling, so it has long been eaten between farm work. It is eaten across Kyushu, but in Kumamoto many areas use sweet potato. Route 57 is nicknamed the "dagojiru road".

**When.** One ministry page quotes Kumamoto people saying it is on the table all year; another says it is eaten especially in autumn and winter, when taro and burdock are harvested. Both are given here.

## Away from Kyushu

No official source compares these soups at home and elsewhere. What the ministry stresses is variation within the region: by area, by household.

## What people get wrong

- **"Dagojiru is noodle soup."** Mostly dumplings; only Aso's *nobe-dago* is noodle-like.
- **"Kumamoto's and Ōita's are the same dish."** The ministry treats them separately.
- **"There is one recipe."** Kumamoto alone has five named kinds.
