Nanki-Katsuura Onsen is the hot-spring side of Katsuura, a fishing port in the town of Nachikatsuura at the south-eastern tip of Wakayama prefecture. Most of its baths are inside hotels and inns, many of them open to day visitors, and the town around them is built on fresh tuna. This page uses the town office, the Nachikatsuura tourism bureau, Wakayama prefecture and its World Heritage Centre, the Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau, research abstracts, the farm ministry, JR West and the national tourism organisation (JNTO). Where they disagree, both are given. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring types named below, see the guides to [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/), [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/) and [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the analysis sign, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### How many sources

The town names two hot springs within its borders: **Katsuura Onsen**, facing the sea, and **Yukawa Onsen**, beside the Yukashigata lagoon (page updated 10 August 2026). It says the town has **177 sources as of March 2018**, the most of any municipality in Wakayama by its own count. The tourism bureau also calls it the largest supply in the prefecture, without a figure.

Wakayama's Red Data Book, in its geology volume, says Katsuura Onsen has **more than 200 sources**, with no year and no boundary. One figure is for the whole town in 2018 and the other for Katsuura Onsen at an unstated date; official sources do not explain the gap.

### Spring types

There is no single official spring type for the town. The Red Data Book says the water rises mainly from the Shikiya Formation of the Kumano Group, that high-temperature springs follow an anticline running from Noroshi-yama to Natsuyama, and that most are rich in hydrogen carbonate and sodium, falling by their anions into a "salt" type and a "sodium bicarbonate" type.

The published listings differ by facility:

- **Kyūkamura Nanki-Katsuura**: sodium chloride spring (tourism bureau).
- **Yurinoyama Onsen**: hydrogen sulphide spring (tourism bureau).
- **Kiyomon-yu**, in the Yukawa area: alkaline simple spring (the town's page, updated 2009, with prices and hours dated 2015).

So the answer depends on the bath. Official sources do not give temperature, flow or pH for the town as a whole.

### Why it is hot here

The Red Data Book says the Kumano acidic igneous rocks spread from Nachikatsuura to Owase in Mie, and that a volcanic episode in the Miocene formed a huge **Kumano caldera** with a long axis of up to 40 km. It says the high-temperature springs of Nachikatsuura, Yunomine, Kawayu, Watarase and Shirahama "are thought to be related" to this igneous activity.

The prefecture's magazine *Nagomi* (vol. 40, a Nanki Kumano Geopark feature) dates the caldera to volcanic activity about 14 million years ago, estimates it at about 40 by 20 km and says it "is said to be" one of the largest in the world. It explains the many springs of the Kii Peninsula by water carried down in the subducting ocean plate, heated and warming the groundwater, which rises as hot spring water. Its map of geosites marks Katsuura Onsen.

A 2003 abstract to the Japan Geoscience Union notes that many high-temperature springs, Katsuura among them, rise in southern Kii although it is not a volcanic area. No research specific to Katsuura's heat is in the sources used here, and whether the heat is from magma or from deep fluids is not settled. The research on the wider area is set out on the [Yunomine](/en/soak/yunomine/) page.

### Not a designated health resort

The environment ministry's list of *kokumin hoyō onsenchi*, national health resort hot springs, had 79 entries as of October 2022. Its two for Wakayama are the Kumano Hongū and Ryūjin spring areas. Katsuura is not on it.

## Where it came from

### The town

Nachikatsuura town was formed in April 1955 from four municipalities: Nachi, the gate town of Mount Nachi; Katsuura, which the town calls "a town of hot springs and fishing"; and the villages of Ukui and Irokawa. Shimosato town and Ōta village joined in January 1960 (page updated 1 October 2024).

### How old is the spring?

Official sources do not give a founding year or a founding story for Katsuura Onsen itself. Two older accounts belong to the Yukawa area, not to Katsuura's harbour:

- The town says Yukawa Onsen **is said to have** flourished as a place of *yugori*, purification bathing, for pilgrims to Kumano (page updated 2016).
- Of Kiyomon-yu, the town says it **is said to have been** found about 1,500 years ago and served Kumano pilgrims for *yugori* and the sick as a place to take the waters (page updated 2009).

### Fudarakusan-ji and the World Heritage listing

Wakayama's World Heritage Centre describes the Kumano Sanzan as three shrines, Kumano Hongū Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha and Kumano Nachi Taisha, and two temples, Seiganto-ji and **Fudarakusan-ji**, linked by the Nakahechi pilgrimage route. Fudarakusan-ji stands in Nachikatsuura. The centre calls it a temple known for *Fudaraku tokai*, the faith of setting out across the sea for Kannon's paradise, and its inventory of components lists it as the site of a temple that was a base for that faith.

The **Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range** were entered on the World Heritage List on 7 July 2004, according to the centre. Fudarakusan-ji is a component of that property, separate from the pilgrimage routes themselves. The hot spring is not.

## How the town works

### A port with baths in the hotels

The tourism bureau describes the Katsuura area as spreading out from the port, which it calls "No. 1 in Japan for fresh tuna landings", with good hot springs, eateries and shops around the market where fresh tuna is auctioned each morning, and the Kinomatsushima islets in the harbour. JNTO describes a bay dotted with about 130 rocky islets.

The Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau's English page calls Katsuura a "fishing port turned hot spring resort". It says the springs are scattered through the town, mostly inside hotels and ryokan, and many can be used on a day visit. The full list of baths and footbaths is at the tourist information office next to JR Kii-Katsuura station, and the port is a few hundred metres from the station. JNTO adds that a "Yu Meguri Ticket" for bathing is sold at that office; it gives no price.

### Bōkidō, the cave bath

Wakayama's official tourism site describes **Bōkidō**, a natural cave hot spring at Hotel Urashima. It says the cave was carved from mudstone by the rough waves of the Kumano Sea, measures 25 m across the mouth, 50 m deep and 15 m high, and that hot water rises naturally inside. Nobles and shoguns are said to have rested there. The name is explained as water so pleasant it made you forget to go home; the page does not say who first said so.

For day visitors the same page gives **9:00 to 19:00 (last entry 18:00)**, **¥2,500 for adults and ¥1,250 for children aged 3 to primary school, both tax included**, with a towel and bath towel. There are scheduled and temporary closing days. The page has no update date and notes that details may have changed.

### Two day-bath prices with tax

The tourism bureau's onsen page gives two prices marked as including tax (checked 13 October 2026):

- **Kyūkamura Nanki-Katsuura**: adults (junior high and over) **¥1,000, tax included**, with a lower rate for children aged 4 to primary school. Open 11:00 to 19:30, from 15:00 on Thursdays.
- **Yurinoyama Onsen**: adults **¥400, tax included**, with a lower rate for children. Open 9:00 to 22:00, closed on Tuesdays unless the Tuesday is a public holiday.

These are given as examples of prices published with tax, not as recommendations.

### The morning auction

The town says the fresh tuna auction can be watched from **about 7 a.m.** from a viewing space called **TSUNAGOOD** in the market's second sale hall, and that the market has days off (page updated January 2023). Its older port page, last updated in 2012, says auctions run early every morning except Saturdays, with other occasional closures. JNTO gives 7 a.m. and says the market is about three minutes on foot from Kii-Katsuura station. Check the day before you go.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and how to bathe

The tourism bureau's pages and the town's onsen page say nothing on tattoos or bathing manners, and official sources give no Katsuura-specific rule.

For the Kumano area as a whole, the Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau publishes an English guide, *How to Enjoy a Japanese Bath* (undated, copyright 2020). It says tattoos are associated with gangsters in Japan and some lodgings may not accept them, but that this does not necessarily apply to overseas visitors in Kumano, and that it has not been a problem in rural areas. It suggests covering tattoos that might look aggressive with tape or a bandage, while noting that some places do not allow taped skin in the water. The same guide covers the basics, such as washing before you get in, no swimsuits, and no photographs in most baths. Ask each bath or inn before you go.

### Compared with city baths

No official source compares Katsuura's water with baths elsewhere. What you can check anywhere is the posted sign, which says whether the water is diluted, heated, recirculated or disinfected; see the piece on [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food: fresh tuna

The town says Katsuura port is No. 1 in Japan for fresh tuna landed by **longline** fishing, and the tourism bureau calls it "the town with the largest landings in Japan". Neither gives a figure.

Wakayama prefecture's press material of 28 February 2020 (food distribution section) does. It says Katsuura was first in Japan for fresh tuna by both volume and value, giving **8,979 tonnes** and **¥6,576,259 thousand** for **2018**, and citing the 2018 survey of fishery products at production sites by the Japan Fisheries Information Service Center. A draft resolution of the prefectural assembly, dated 29 June 2018, gives a different figure: about 1,500 boats a year landing about 10,000 tonnes of tuna. The years and the way of counting may differ; the two documents do not explain it.

The prefecture's magazine *Nagomi* (vol. 54) says the port limited incoming boats to longliners in the decade from 1975, that about 60% of the tuna landed is albacore (*binchō*), and that the market is the Katsuura market of the Wakayama Prefectural Federation of Fisheries Cooperatives. The farm ministry's Wakayama overview says longline tuna fishing and traditional fishing continue unchanged in Nachikatsuura.

Tuna is not, however, on the farm ministry's list of Wakayama's local dishes. The [Kinki local dishes guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-kinki/) covers what is on it.

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

- **From Shin-Osaka.** The tourism bureau gives 3 hours 30 minutes to 4 hours by limited express, and JNTO about 3.5 hours by the *Kuroshio*. JR West's line timetable for 13 October 2026 shows 237 to 256 minutes for the six through trains from Shin-Osaka, and 218 to 234 minutes from Tennōji, so allow about 4 hours.
- **From Nagoya.** The tourism bureau gives about 3 hours 50 minutes by JR limited express on the Kisei line, and JNTO just under 4 hours by the *Nanki*, which runs between Nagoya and Shingū or Kii-Katsuura. In the other direction, *Nanki* No. 8 leaves Kii-Katsuura at 17:11 and reaches Nagoya at 20:49, daily, according to JR West.
- **From Tokyo.** The tourism bureau suggests the Shinkansen to Nagoya (about 1 hour 40 minutes) then the limited express, or to Shin-Osaka (about 2 hours 30 minutes to 2 hours 50 minutes) then the limited express. It also lists an overnight bus from Ikebukuro, leaving at 21:30 and arriving at 8:30, about 11 hours.
- **By air.** About 70 minutes from Haneda to Nanki-Shirahama airport, a local bus of about 20 minutes to Shirahama station, then about 1 hour 25 minutes by limited express to Kii-Katsuura; about 2 hours 10 minutes from the airport by hire car (tourism bureau).
- **By bus from Osaka.** About 3 hours by Meikō Bus to Kii-Tanabe, then about 1 hour 40 minutes by limited express (tourism bureau).
- **To Nachi.** Local buses from Kii-Katsuura station take about 30 minutes to Nachisan and about 20 minutes to Daimonzaka (tourism bureau).

Check train times on the operators' sites before you travel.

Nearby spring towns are [Yunomine](/en/soak/yunomine/) in the Kumano Hongū area, [Totsukawa](/en/soak/totsukawa/) across the border in Nara, and [Shirahama](/en/soak/shirahama/) on the west coast.

## What people get wrong

- **"Katsuura has one kind of water."** The published types differ by bath, from sodium chloride to hydrogen sulphide to alkaline simple.
- **"The source count is fixed."** The town says 177 in March 2018; the prefecture's Red Data Book says more than 200 for Katsuura Onsen.
- **"The springs are volcanic, like a mountain resort."** A 2003 research abstract calls southern Kii a non-volcanic area. The prefecture says the heat is thought to be related to igneous activity millions of years ago, and the source has not been settled.
- **"Katsuura is not part of the World Heritage site."** Fudarakusan-ji, in the same town, is a listed component. The hot spring itself is not.
- **"Tuna is Wakayama's official local dish."** The town and prefecture tie Katsuura to fresh tuna landings, but tuna is not on the farm ministry's list of local dishes.
- **"The auction runs every day."** The market has days off; check before you go.
- **"Kii-Katsuura is three and a half hours from Shin-Osaka."** On the 13 October 2026 timetable it is closer to four.
