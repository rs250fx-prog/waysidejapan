Nanki-Shirahama Onsen is in the town of Shirahama, Wakayama prefecture, on the Pacific coast. Its tourism association counts 15 sources and 23 places to use the water — baths, footbaths and the like — and the town runs several public baths, one of them a rock pool facing the ocean. This page covers what the sources are, what the town and its bodies say about its age, how the public baths work for a visitor, and how to get there. The ten official spring types and how to read pH and tonicity on the sign are in the separate guide to onsen water types, and what free-flowing water means is in the *kakenagashi* piece; the Honshu overview places Shirahama among the other Kansai springs.

## Where it came from

Every official source agrees on one written record and disagrees on the count of years.

- **Shirahama town**, on its Saki no Yu page (updated 16 July 2026): the spring is said to date from about 1,400 years ago, in the Asuka and Nara period, when it was called the hot spring of Muro (牟婁温湯). The *Nihon Shoki* records that Prince Arima visited in 657 and recommended it to Empress Saimei; later Emperors Jitō and Monmu came too.
- **The tourism association**: Prince Arima’s visit in 657 is in the *Nihon Shoki*, and Empress Saimei came the following year, then Jitō and Monmu. Its Saki no Yu description speaks of "Shirahama Onsen’s 1,350-year history" without saying where it counts from. It calls the Gyōkō source the oldest in Shirahama.
- **The Shirahama Onsen Ryokan Cooperative**, an industry body for the town’s inns: the spring appears in the *Man’yōshū* and the *Nihon Shoki*, and Empress Saimei and Emperor Tenji among others came from the capital to bathe. Its top page says the *Man’yōshū* calls it "the old spring of Muro".
- **JNTO**: "about 1,300 years" on one page and "over 1,350 years" on another.

So the official figures run from about 1,300 to about 1,400 years. This site does not choose.

**"Three ancient springs."** The town says Shirahama "is said to be one of Japan’s three oldest springs, with Dōgo and Arima". The association and the inn cooperative both say it is counted among Japan’s three ancient springs (*Nihon san-kotō*), with the same two. None of these pages says who chose the three, and I could not confirm that it is an official title.

## The water

The tourism association’s page on its sources has a table of spring type, pH and tonicity for each one. I give the table as published; I did not use the explanatory text beneath it, which in the version I read repeated the chloride description for other types.

| Source | Spring type | pH | Tonicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 生絹湯 | sodium chloride | 7.2 | hypertonic |
| 砿湯 | sulphur-bearing sodium chloride | 7.2 | hypertonic |
| 行幸源泉 (Gyōkō) | sodium chloride | 8.2 | hypertonic |
| 垣谷 No. 2 and 3 | sodium chloride-hydrogen carbonate | 7.2 | hypertonic |
| 長生温泉 (Chōsei) | sodium chloride-hydrogen carbonate | 7.7 | hypotonic |
| 小谷湯 | sodium chloride | 7.1 | hypertonic |
| 燈明台 (Tōmyōdai) | sodium chloride | 6.9 | isotonic |
| 甘露の湯 (Kanro no Yu), 藤乃湯, かごめ, 若葉の湯 (Wakaba no Yu), 第三天山 | sodium chloride-hydrogen carbonate | — | hypotonic |
| 蓬莱湯 (Hōrai-yu), えびね温泉 | simple sulphur | 9.9 / 9.7 | hypotonic |
| 渚の湯 (Nagisa no Yu) | simple | 10.1 | hypotonic |

The town’s bath pages add temperatures for the sources they use: Gyōkō, sodium chloride, 78.0°C (Saki no Yu); the 砿湯 source, given there as sulphur-bearing sodium chloride, strong salt, 74.0°C (Muro no Yu, which also uses Gyōkō); and the 生絹湯 source, sodium chloride, 66.8°C (Shirara-yu). The Muro no Yu page gives Gyōkō’s flow as "unmeasurable". The inn cooperative writes the same type slightly differently, as sodium-chloride onsen; it is the same thing.

I could not find a flow rate for the town as a whole, a figure for Shirahama from the environment ministry, or a public source explaining why the water rises here, by the sea.

## How the town works

The town’s onsen pages list its public baths: the open-air Shirasuna, Muro no Yu, Saki no Yu, Shirara-yu, another public bath (Matsu no Yu), footbaths, and springs in the Hiki River area. The tourism association lists 24 baths, footbaths and sources, and names Saki no Yu, Shirara-yu, Muro no Yu, Shirasuna and Matsu no Yu among those open for day bathing. The inn cooperative’s "public baths and footbaths" page (copyright 2019) picks out Saki no Yu, Muro no Yu and Shirara-yu — the last with a bath on its second floor (2F) looking out over Shirarahama beach — and three footbaths, at Yusakihama plaza, Tsukumoto and Mifune. It also says some public baths are free, and advertises a bath stamp rally I could not read.

So the shape is 15 sources spread around the town, used by town-run public baths, inn baths and footbaths. I could not find an official shared pass for walking between baths, as at Kinosaki.

### Saki no Yu

Saki no Yu is the town’s open-air bath on the shore. JNTO describes its rock pools facing the Pacific, where you can feel the waves hitting the rocks. Because the bath water flows into the sea, the town says shampoo, conditioner and soap cannot be used.

## Bathing there

From the town’s bath pages (Saki no Yu updated 16 July 2026; Muro no Yu and Shirara-yu 1 March 2025), checked 10 October 2026:

- **Saki no Yu** — no regular closing day, but it may close temporarily. Hours change with the season; from October to March it is open 8:00 to 17:00. Last entry is 30 minutes before closing.
- **Muro no Yu** — closed every Tuesday.
- **Shirara-yu** — closed every Thursday.
- At **Muro no Yu and Shirara-yu**, 7:00 to 9:00 is for Shirahama residents only. They are open to others from 9:00 to 11:00 and from 14:00 to 21:30.

The town’s pages list admission prices, but none says whether they include tax, so I do not give them. The town’s footbath page calls the footbaths free, but it was last updated in February 2016; check before relying on it. The tourism association’s list of footbaths is Sandanbeki, Yusakihama plaza, Yanagibashi, Tsukumoto, Mifune and Tsubaki Onsen.

**Tattoos.** I found nothing on tattoos from the town, the tourism association, the inn cooperative or JNTO’s Shirahama pages. JNTO’s national onsen page says policies differ by facility and suggests checking in advance. The water types guide covers the national picture, including private baths.

**In English.** I could not find an official English guide to bathing in Shirahama. The association links to a translated version of its site, and the town has a "Foreign Language" page I did not open.

## Food

The inn cooperative — an industry body, not a public one — features *kue*, longtooth grouper, as "the phantom fish": wild fish live on the rocky shores of the Kii Channel and are hard to catch, which makes them costly, while farming in Shirahama has recently made a steady supply possible. It is served as sashimi, in hot pot and in each inn’s own dishes. The page gives no season. The farm ministry’s list of Wakayama’s local dishes has nothing under *kue*, Shirahama or onsen, so I could not find a dish that both the ministry and Shirahama’s official side name.

## Getting there

From the tourism association’s access page (copyright 2023, no update date), checked 10 October 2026:

- **Osaka** — about 2 hours 30 minutes by the JR limited express Kuroshio (about 2 hours 15 minutes from Tennōji), then about 10 to 15 minutes by bus or taxi to the onsen area.
- **Highway bus** — ten round trips a day; about 3 hours 20 minutes from OCAT in Namba, about 3 hours 30 minutes from JR Osaka.
- **Kyoto** — about 3 hours via Shin-Osaka.
- **Tokyo** — about 75 minutes by JAL from Haneda; about 10 hours 30 minutes by highway bus from Busta Shinjuku.

The inn cooperative gives about 2 hours 30 minutes from Shin-Osaka by Kuroshio, about 2 hours from Tennōji, about 2 hours 50 minutes from Kyoto, about 3 hours 30 minutes by highway bus from Osaka, and 75 minutes from Haneda to Nanki-Shirahama Airport. JNTO gives about 2.5 hours from Shin-Osaka, and about an hour from Haneda to the airport, then 10 to 20 minutes by bus. The figures differ slightly between sources — for Tennōji, about 2 hours 15 minutes or about 2 hours — so plan on roughly 2 hours 15 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes from Osaka by Kuroshio. None of these sources gives directions from Kansai Airport. Fares are not on this page.

## What people get wrong

- **"Every public bath is open to visitors all day."** Muro no Yu and Shirara-yu are for residents only from 7 to 9 a.m., close in the late morning, and each has a weekly closing day.
- **"An onsen by the sea is seawater."** The sources here are listed as sodium chloride, chloride-hydrogen carbonate, sulphur and simple springs; I could not find an official explanation of where the water comes from.
- **"Shirahama is exactly 1,300 (or 1,400) years old."** The official sources give anything from about 1,300 to about 1,400 years. The fixed point they share is Prince Arima’s visit in 657.
- **"The three ancient springs is an official ranking."** Three sets of official pages repeat it; none says who chose it.
- **"Saki no Yu is a place to wash."** No soap or shampoo, because the water runs into the sea.
