Togura-Kamiyamada Onsen is a spa town in the city of Chikuma, Nagano, which Nagano's official tourism guide places between Ueda and Nagano city. Chikuma city says it grew into one of the prefecture's leading spa towns as the place where pilgrims to **Zenkōji** temple, in Nagano city, came to unwind after their devotions. Today it has seven public baths open to day visitors and footbaths fed by the same water. This page uses Chikuma city, the city's tourism bureau (the Shinshū Chikuma Kankōkyoku), Nagano's official tourism guide, the Shinano Railway and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, both are given. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring types named below, see [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/) and [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### Two descriptions

Chikuma city's page on the spa (updated 7 September 2026) says **more than 50 sources** have been dug, and that the water is plentiful enough to flow freely even in the public bathhouses. It says most of it is **colourless, clear simple sulphur spring**; some is faintly greenish, and some carries *yu-no-hana* mineral flakes or bubbles. Its summary lists:

- **Spring type**: simple sulphur spring, simple hydrogen sulphide spring; colourless and clear.
- **Temperature**: 25 to 60 degrees.

The city's tourism bureau also gives more than 50 sources, but calls the water an **alkaline simple spring**.

The two do not match. In the environment ministry's table of old and new spring names, "simple hydrogen sulphide spring" is an old name, now written simple sulphur spring (hydrogen sulphide type); "alkaline" describes the water's pH band, not a spring type of its own. The [simple springs section](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/) explains the difference. This page uses the city's wording and records the bureau's as its own description.

Official sources do not give a total flow, a pH figure, a dated analysis or an explanation of where the heat comes from. The city also lists what the water is said to help; this page does not repeat it.

### Not a health resort

Togura-Kamiyamada is not on the environment ministry's list of national health resort hot springs, as of October 2022. [Shirahone](/en/soak/shirahone/), also in Nagano, is.

### How big it is

The sources count differently, and none of them dates its figures:

- **The tourism bureau** (English): about 30 inns, all with hot-spring baths, seven *sotoyu* public baths and three footbaths. Its Japanese page also says about 30 inns, large and small.
- **Nagano's official tourism guide**: seven *sotoyu* and two footbaths, and more than 100 inns, izakaya and snack bars in the bubble years.
- **Chikuma city**: seven *sotoyu*.

## Where it came from

### The Zenkōji pilgrims' spa

Chikuma city calls Togura-Kamiyamada "the spa for *shōjin-otoshi* after a pilgrimage to Zenkōji". The city says the town grew on this role into one of Nagano's leading spa towns, and that from the high-growth years to the bubble era it was packed with large ryokan and hotels. The tourism bureau says it developed in this role from about 100 years ago.

The phrase is the city's and the bureau's description, not an official designation.

### When it opened

- **The tourism bureau**, describing the *sotoyu* in Kamiyamada, says the Kamiyamada spring was opened in **1903** (Meiji 36), after hard work by the people of the time.
- **The Shinano Railway's** page for Togura station speaks of a spa opened more than 120 years ago.

Official sources do not give an opening date for the Togura side, or explain how the two names came to be joined.

### "One of 100 famous springs"

The tourism bureau's Japanese page calls Togura-Kamiyamada one of the "100 famous springs recommended by doctors of balneotherapy". It does not say who made the selection, so treat it as the bureau's own claim.

## How the town works

### Seven sotoyu

Chikuma city says there are **seven day-use public baths**, the *sotoyu*, and that "even the footbaths flow freely". The tourism bureau's page lists the seven with their hours, closing days and prices. Closing days differ from bath to bath; examples include the second Tuesday, the third Tuesday and the fourth Monday of each month. The prices are not given here, because the bureau does not say whether they include tax. The bureau's English page says the footbaths are **free**.

### The street

Chikuma city presents the town through its geisha, *shateki* shooting galleries, a Shōwa-era retro townscape used as a film location, and cycling. Nagano's official tourism guide says it was one of the prefecture's most popular spas in the bubble era. The bureau's English page counts more than 100 places to eat and drink.

### Through the year

- **Summer festival**: on the Saturday and Sunday just before Marine Day in July (Chikuma city).
- **Chikuma river fireworks**: every year on **7 August**, about 10,000 shells (Chikuma city).
- **Morning market**: Sundays from May to October (tourism bureau).
- **Onsen museum**: free, weekdays only, by reservation (tourism bureau).

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The tourism bureau has English pages on the spa and on local food. Neither the city's nor the bureau's Japanese and English pages on the spa give a tattoo rule or a guide to bathing.

Nagano's official tourism guide, writing about the whole prefecture, says tattoos have long been banned, that about 30% of facility and hotel managers do not refuse them according to the Japan Tourism Agency, and suggests covering a small tattoo with tape or using an inn's private bath for a large one. JNTO says public baths decide case by case. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each bath or inn in advance.

### Compared with city baths

No official source here compares the water with baths elsewhere. Chikuma city's point is about the town itself: the water is plentiful enough to flow freely in its bathhouses and footbaths.

What is the same everywhere is the national rule. Since 24 May 2005, any bath in Japan that adds water to its spring water, heats it, recirculates it, puts in bath additives or disinfects it must say so, and why, on its notice. That applies to a *sotoyu* here as much as to a bath in Tokyo. To read the notice, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

**Oshibori soba.** The farm ministry's entry for *oshibori udon* places that dish around Sakaki in northern Nagano, and adds that **Chikuma city (the former Kōshoku city and former Togura town)** has *oshibori soba*. The tourism bureau's English food page lists *oshibori udon* as Chikuma food. Official sources do not call either a speciality of the spa town itself.

**Oyaki.** The farm ministry lists oyaki for northern and central Nagano and the whole prefecture, without mentioning the spa. The tourism bureau's English food page names an oyaki shop in the spa town. So oyaki is a Nagano dish you can find here.

**Not gohei-mochi.** The farm ministry places Nagano's gohei-mochi in the Kiso and Ina areas, and no official source ties it to Togura-Kamiyamada.

Prices are not given. More on these dishes in the [Chūbu local food guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-chubu/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares and current timetables are not on this page.

- **From Tokyo.** Take the Hokuriku shinkansen to **Ueda** and change to the **Shinano Railway** for **Togura** station (Chikuma city). The city's relocation site gives about 1 hour 20 minutes from Tokyo to Ueda by shinkansen, about 30 minutes from Ueda, and **about 2 hours** from Tokyo to Chikuma.
- **From Hokuriku.** The Hokuriku shinkansen to **Nagano**, then the Shinano Railway to Togura (Chikuma city).
- **From Nagoya.** The JR Shinonoi line to **Shinonoi**, then the Shinano Railway to Togura (Chikuma city).
- **From Togura station.** The Shinano Railway lists the Chikuma city loop bus and taxis. Its page for the station also points visitors to the seven *sotoyu* and the free footbaths.

Official sources do not give the walking time or distance from Togura station to the spa, so ask at the station or check the loop bus before you set off.

For other Nagano spas, see [Yudanaka-Shibu](/en/soak/yudanaka-shibu/), [Nozawa](/en/soak/nozawa/) and [Shirahone](/en/soak/shirahone/); across the mountains in Gifu, see [Okuhida](/en/soak/okuhida/) and [Gero](/en/soak/gero/).

## What people get wrong

- **"It is a simple alkaline spring" or "it is a sulphur spring", as if settled.** The city says simple sulphur spring; the tourism bureau says alkaline simple spring. Neither gives a dated analysis.
- **"Zenkōji's own spa."** It is the city's and the bureau's description of its role for pilgrims, not an official title, and Zenkōji is in Nagano city.
- **"It opened in 1903."** That date is the bureau's, for Kamiyamada. Official sources do not give one for Togura.
- **"Oshibori udon is the spa's dish."** The farm ministry places oshibori udon around Sakaki and notes oshibori soba in Chikuma; official sources do not tie either to the spa town.
- **"The footbaths cost money."** The tourism bureau's English page says they are free.
