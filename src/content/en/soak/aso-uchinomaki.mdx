Uchinomaki is the main hot spring town of Aso city, on the floor of the Aso caldera in Kumamoto. Its water does not come from the volcano, by the local geopark’s account; it began with an irrigation well in 1897; and the novelist Natsume Sōseki stayed here in 1899. This page uses Aso city, its tourism association, the Aso hot spring inns’ cooperative (the inns’ own body), the Aso Geopark, the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA), the national roads office, Kumamoto prefecture’s tourism site and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring names below, see [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). For the rest of the region, see the [Kyushu hot springs overview](/en/soak/onsen-kyushu/).

## The volcano and the water

### The caldera

The JMA lists Asosan as a volcano under constant watch. It describes a caldera **17 km east to west and 25 km north to south**, formed by four huge pyroclastic eruptions between about 270,000 and 90,000 years ago, with Takadake at 1,592 m and Nakadake at 1,506 m. Aso city’s tourism association, in English, gives the caldera as 18 km east to west; this page uses the agency’s figure.

### The alert level, as of 13 October 2026

The JMA had Asosan at **alert level 2** (restrictions around the crater). The Fukuoka regional office lowered it from level 3 at 16:00 on **1 September 2026**, and its latest commentary was issued at 16:00 on 9 October. It warns of large volcanic rocks and pyroclastic flows within about 1 km of the Nakadake first crater, says the volcano is releasing a lot of sulphur dioxide and that activity remains raised, and names Aso city and Minamiaso village as the areas concerned. The crater restriction site showed the same on that day, with entry banned within about 1 km of the crater.

Aso city’s tourism association gives the run-up in its notices: the level was raised on 21 June 2026; level 3 was announced at 15:45 on 14 August, when crater viewing stopped but the road to Kusasenri stayed open and there were no road restrictions in the towns at the foot of the mountain; and the lowering to 2 was posted on 3 September.

The agency’s page does not mention Uchinomaki. Levels change; check the JMA and the crater restriction site before you go.

### Water from granite

The Aso Geopark says hot spring water in active volcanic areas often has heated water and gas of magmatic origin mixed in, but that this is a small share, and most of it is groundwater that has soaked in from the surface. It then says something specific about this town: Uchinomaki’s water **rises from granite underground**, and although the town is inside the caldera, it is a **hydrogen-carbonate type** spring. The geopark names Jigoku and Tarutama as the springs thought to be related to the Aso volcano, and links [Kurokawa](/en/soak/kurokawa/) and [Tsuetate](/en/soak/tsuetate/) to the Kujū volcano instead.

### Spring names

The sources do not give one name, and this page does not choose:

- **Kumamoto prefecture’s hot spring site** lists sulphate spring, simple spring, simple spring (weakly alkaline) and hydrogen-carbonate spring. It says the slightly hot water is colourless and clear, and drinkable.
- **The Aso Geopark** says hydrogen-carbonate type.
- **Aso city’s English map** says some of the water that flows straight from the source is colourless, clear and drinkable.

Official sources do not give the number of sources, flow, pH or source temperature for Uchinomaki. The number of inns differs too: about 17 hotels and inns (the prefecture), more than 20 hotels and inns (Aso city’s tourism association) and more than 20 hotels, inns and town baths (the inns’ cooperative, in English). Uchinomaki is not on the environment ministry’s list of national health resort hot springs; that means only that it has no such designation.

## Where it came from

### An irrigation well, 1897

Aso city’s leaflet on Sōseki’s *Nihyaku-tōka* (The 210th Day) says Uchinomaki began in **1897**, when hot water came up while an irrigation well was being dug. Inns and shops followed, and it grew into a spa town.

### Sōseki and The 210th Day

The same leaflet, issued by the Aso caldera tourism council with Aso city’s tourism section as its office, says Sōseki came to Aso in **1899**, while he was teaching at the Fifth Higher School in Kumamoto, with his colleague the English scholar Yamakawa Shinjirō. The trip became the novel *Nihyaku-tōka*. In Uchinomaki he stayed at the Yōseikan, later the Sannōkaku, which has since closed. He is said to have tried to climb Aso and turned back in a storm; in the novel, the hero is caught by a storm on the climb. The leaflet explains the title: the 210th day counted from *risshun*, the start of spring, a time of year with many typhoons.

Aso city’s tourist maps call Uchinomaki a spa town where Sōseki and other writers stayed (March 2026) and one visited by many literary figures (English, February 2022).

### Daikanbō

Aso city’s English map says the viewpoint **Daikanbō**, a peak on the northern outer rim north-east of Uchinomaki, was named by the writer Tokutomi Sohō. Aso city’s materials do not describe any stay by Sohō in Uchinomaki.

## How the town works

### Town baths

Uchinomaki has public baths the locals use, called *machi-yu*, "town baths". The inns’ cooperative’s map sorts places in the Uchinomaki district into town baths, town baths with rooms, and inns; it shows **four** town baths, plus inns that also serve as town baths. Aso city’s tourism association, in English, says **six** town baths are still used as gathering places for local people. Neither explains its count, so this page gives both.

### Day bathing

The inns’ cooperative lists day bathing by facility. Its prices do not say whether tax is included, so none are given here. Hours and closing days vary by facility, and it notes that bathing may not be available depending on bookings. Its page also posts the general contraindications set by the environment ministry, such as acute illness and pregnancy.

### A walk along the Kurokawa

Aso city’s walking map (content as of the 2021 financial year) follows the riverbank of the **Kurokawa**, the river that runs through the middle of the spa town, where stones shaped like hearts and like the prefecture’s mascot Kumamon are hidden. It suggests a drop-in bath afterwards. This river is not [Kurokawa Onsen](/en/soak/kurokawa/), which is a separate town in Minamioguni.

### After the July 2026 earthquake

On 31 July 2026, Aso city’s tourism association said tourist facilities, accommodation, roads and rivers in the city had no major damage and were operating normally, but that some JR Hōhi line trains and Sankō buses were suspended or disrupted and some expressways were closed. On 7 August the inns’ cooperative said no major damage had been confirmed in the Aso and Uchinomaki area and that it was open as usual. From 10 August to 18 October 2026 its member inns opened their baths free to residents of named affected municipalities in the south of the prefecture and to disaster volunteers; family baths were charged.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

Aso city’s tourism association and the inns’ cooperative have English pages, but neither mentions tattoos or how to bathe. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Uchinomaki.

In Japanese, the cooperative’s notice of 7 August 2026 on the free bathing scheme lists who would be turned away, including people with tattoos (body paint and stickers included), along with gang members, anyone with a risk of infection and anyone drunk. That notice is about the scheme; it does not say it is the town’s standing rule. Ask each inn or bath in advance. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

JNTO’s general advice: leave your clothes, swimwear included, in the changing room; wash at the washing area and rinse before getting in; keep your small towel out of the water; tie up long hair; do not swim or splash; leave your phone outside. On tattoos, it says some traditional baths refuse them but this is starting to change, suggests looking for tattoo-friendly places or a private bath (*kashikiri-buro*), and says cover stickers suit only very small tattoos.

### Compared with city baths

No official Uchinomaki source compares its water with baths in the city. For what the notice at the bath tells you about added water, heating and recirculation, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## What it is eaten with

- **Aso takana-zuke.** The farm ministry gives Aso city as the main area for this pickled mustard leaf, and *takana-meshi*, old pickles chopped, fried in oil with chilli and mixed into rice, as a local dish. It says the Aso Takana Festival is held in the **Uchinomaki district** every year in late March, where people harvest takana and try pickling it. The page does not mention the hot spring, but it places the dish in this district.
- **Dagojiru.** The ministry’s page on this dumpling soup covers the whole of Kumamoto and says many people recommend it with takana-meshi. Its Kumamoto overview says that in Aso, flat, long, noodle-like *nobe-dago* are the main kind, and that rice flour is used because the area grows little wheat.
- **Basashi.** The ministry gives Kumamoto city and the Aso area as the main areas for raw horse meat, and says it spread after the war, when people in Aso, a warhorse-breeding area, began eating horse meat in the food shortage, reaching restaurants in the 1955–64 period.
- **Akado-zuke.** The ministry’s overview describes pickled stems of akado taro, grown mainly in Aso city, called "the basashi of the fields".
- **Akaushi.** Red cattle beef is not on the ministry’s list of Kumamoto dishes. *Kumamoto akaushi* is protected as geographical indication No. 67, registered on 27 September 2018, and its entry refers to the cattle’s adaptation to pastures centred on the Aso area. Aso city’s tourism association introduces akaushi raised on the Aso grasslands and lists places serving it on rice.

For Kumamoto’s food as a whole, see [Kyushu local food](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-kyushu/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Times are the sources’ round figures; fares are not on this page.

**Current trains and buses.** Aso city’s tourism association reported some JR Hōhi line and bus suspensions on 31 July 2026. No later official update on the Hōhi line or the Kyushu Ōdan bus could be confirmed, and JR Kyushu’s front page on 13 October did not mention the Hōhi line. Check the operators before you travel.

- **To Aso station** (Aso city’s tourism association):
  - From Aso Kumamoto airport: airport liner to Higo-Ōzu (about 15 minutes), then the JR Hōhi line to Aso (about 50 minutes); or the Yamabiko limited express bus to Aso station (about 1 hour).
  - From Kumamoto station: Hōhi line to Higo-Ōzu (about 35 minutes), then on to Aso (about 50 minutes); or the Kyushu Ōdan bus (about 2 hours).
  - From Ōita station: the Kyushu Ōdan limited express on the Hōhi line (about 2 hours).
- **From Fukuoka.** The prefecture gives Hakata to Kumamoto by shinkansen as about 40 minutes. Official Aso sources do not give a through time from Fukuoka.
- **Aso station to Uchinomaki.** About 15 minutes, by the inns’ cooperative; it does not say by what.
- **By car.** About 50 minutes from Kumamoto interchange to Aso station by Route 57, or about 50 minutes from the airport via Ōzu and the Route 57 northern recovery road (Aso city’s tourism association).

### After the 2016 earthquakes

Aso city’s earthquake record (issued 28 February 2021) says the **JR Hōhi line reopened along its whole length on 8 August 2020**, four years and four months after the earthquakes. The **Route 57 northern recovery road opened on 3 October 2020**, to traffic from 13:00 that day, and the original Route 57 reopened the same day; the national roads office also gives 3 October 2020. For about four and a half years, traffic detoured via the Milk Road. From the Milk Road entrance junction in Ōzu to Akamizu (now the Aso-nishi interchange junction), the record gives 24 minutes before the earthquakes, about 43 minutes by the detour over Futae pass, and about 10 minutes by the new road.

### Roads in 2026

The national roads office’s notices: the northern recovery road between Aso-nishi and Ōzu interchanges was closed on 28 July 2026 and reopened on 29 July. A notice of a full closure of the same road was posted on 24 September; its details could not be read. The Takimurozaka section of Route 57 (Namino to Ichinomiya interchanges) is due to open on 28 November 2026. The JMA’s record of the earthquake: magnitude 7.1, maximum intensity 7, in the Kumamoto region at 16:27 on 28 July 2026.

## What people get wrong

- **"Uchinomaki is volcanic water."** The Aso Geopark says it rises from granite and is a hydrogen-carbonate type, even inside the caldera.
- **"Route 57 and the Hōhi line both reopened in August 2020."** The line reopened on 8 August 2020; the road opened on 3 October 2020.
- **"Kurokawa river means Kurokawa Onsen."** The river through Uchinomaki is also called the Kurokawa. Kurokawa Onsen is a different town in Minamioguni.
- **"Tokutomi Sohō stayed in Uchinomaki."** Aso city credits him with naming Daikanbō, not with a stay here. The writer the city ties to the town is Sōseki.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so. The official indications by spring type are in the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

Other Kumamoto spa towns on this site: [Tsuetate](/en/soak/tsuetate/), [Hinagu](/en/soak/hinagu/) and [Kurokawa](/en/soak/kurokawa/).
