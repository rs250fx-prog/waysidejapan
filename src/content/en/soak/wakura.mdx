Wakura Onsen is in Nanao city, on the Noto peninsula in Ishikawa, at the edge of Nanao Bay. Its tourism association calls it the leading "sea onsen" of Hokuriku, with hot, plentiful water. On 1 January 2024 the Noto Peninsula earthquake did heavy damage here, and the town is still rebuilding. This page puts the current state first, because that is what decides whether and how you can go, and then covers the water, the legend, the public bath, food and getting there. It uses the Wakura Onsen tourism association (which also speaks for the inn cooperative), Ishikawa prefecture, Nanao city and, where nothing official was available, a local newspaper, named each time. It does not rate any inn.

For how to read a spring’s name and the analysis sign, see the guide to [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/); for free-flowing water and what "100% source" means on that sign, see the piece on [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Where things stand (checked 11 October 2026)

Everything in this section has a date and a source, and it will go out of date. **Before you travel, check the tourism association’s site and your inn directly.**

### After the January 2024 earthquake

- **The Sōyu reopened on 26 March 2024.** The public bath closed after the earthquake. Ishikawa prefecture’s official tourism site gives the reopening date in an article dated 13 November 2025, and the association’s lodging page says the Sōyu is open again.
- **11 inns have reopened.** This is from the association’s lodging information page. The page has no update date; its latest entry is an inn reopening on 16 July 2026. It says the inn cooperative went from 20 member inns to 19 on 1 April 2026.
- **Reopenings have been staggered.** The same page notes dates from 9 August 2025 to 16 July 2026, some inns opening at first without dinner and some on a provisional basis. It warns that at some inns the entrance and front desk are under construction and use temporary facilities, and that along the shore seawall work spoils the view of the sea from inside.
- **Some inns are not taking ordinary guests.** The page lists a separate set of 11 places that take in recovery workers and volunteers, not as normal business, and asks those guests to understand that safety cannot be guaranteed because of building damage. It says some places cannot use their large baths, but that the Sōyu is open.
- **The association’s plans.** Its home page offers condolences for the earthquake and says a new town-planning body has been set up to work towards a "creative reconstruction" of the whole town, with industry, academia, government and finance. Its home page also lists a viewing area for the seawall works and a reconstruction tour (from 1 March 2026), and a notice dated 21 January 2026 that the 2026 Wakura fireworks are cancelled.
- **Restaurants.** The association shows which places to eat are open on a map dated April 2026, and says hours and closing days may change without notice, so check with each one.

### The August 2026 heavy rain

- **Nanao city**, at 8:00 on 27 August 2026, warned that heavy rain had flooded roads in Nanao and around Nakanoto and Hakui.
- **Ishikawa prefecture’s** page on the rain from 27 August (updated 18 September 2026; its latest damage report, the 24th, is as of 14:00 on 2 October) says the Disaster Relief Act was applied in four municipalities: Hakui, Shika, Hōdatsushimizu and Nakanoto. Nanao is not one of them, and the page does not mention Wakura, the Nanao line or tourism.
- **According to the Hokkoku Shimbun**, a local newspaper, on 6 September 2026, 96 bookings for 373 people at Wakura were cancelled for the five days from 27 to 31 August, with the suspension of the JR Nanao line given as one reason. The paper does not say which section or for how long.
- **According to another Hokkoku Shimbun report** the same day, JR West’s Kanazawa branch restarted the Nanao line between Hakui and Nanao on 6 September; while it was suspended, trains ran between Tsubata and Hakui. The report does not mention Wakura Onsen station.

The train details above rest on the newspaper; this page has not checked them against JR West’s own service information. The association’s, the prefecture’s and Nanao city’s pages give nothing on how the September 2024 heavy rain affected Wakura, so this page does not describe it.

## The water

The association gives a sodium-calcium chloride spring (*natorium-karushiumu enkabutsu sen*), which it describes further as hypertonic, weakly alkaline and high-temperature. Its English page gives the same type. It describes the water as colourless and odourless.

The association’s figures are a flow of 990 litres a minute, pumped, and a temperature of 82.7°C; elsewhere on the same page it says "about 80 degrees". The English page also gives 82.7°C. The association gives no year of measurement and does not say which source was measured, and official sources do not give the number of sources.

The "sea onsen" is the association’s own phrase. Ishikawa prefecture’s English page says the spring rises under the sea, which it calls "a rarity in Japan". Official sources give no statistics showing how rare that is.

Wakura is not on the environment ministry’s list of *kokumin hoyō onsenchi*, the designated health resort onsen areas; the only Ishikawa entry is the Hakusan onsen district.

## Where it came from

### Age

The association dates the opening of the spring to the Daidō era (806–810), about 1,200 years ago. Ishikawa prefecture’s English page also says 1,200 years.

### The white heron

The association tells it this way. In the Eishō era (1046–1053), movements of the earth shifted the spring’s outlet from the valley to the sea offshore, and the hot spring dried up. Later a fishing couple saw an injured white heron soaking in the bubbling sea, and so found the spring again. The association says this is where the name *Wakuura*, "the bay where water wells up", comes from.

Ishikawa prefecture’s English page tells it differently: a heron healing a wounded leg, found by a farmer and his wife, with no date. So the association has a fishing couple and the prefecture a farmer. This page gives both and does not choose.

### Later history

Ishikawa prefecture’s English page adds that in 1611 Maeda Toshinaga had the water carried to Kanazawa Castle, and that Emperor Taishō visited in 1909 (the page’s wording; in 1909 he was still crown prince); Seirinji, the temple used as a temporary residence for the visit, is open to visitors. These are not confirmed by a second source.

Neither the association’s nor the prefecture’s pages give a title of the "oldest" or "three great" kind with a body named as choosing it.

## How the town works

### The Sōyu

The association describes the Sōyu as a public bath for local people and visitors alike, using 100% source water. Outside are a free footbath and a drinking fountain. A second footbath, Tsumagoibune no Yu, is also free.

Ishikawa prefecture’s article gives the Sōyu’s hours as 7:00 to 21:00, closed on the fourth Thursday of the month. The association’s notice of 13 March 2025 says the closing day changed from the 25th of each month to the fourth Thursday from April 2025. Hours can change; check the association’s site.

The prefecture and the association both list a price, but neither says whether tax is included, so this page does not give it.

### The shore

The association’s lodging page says seawall works along the shore are going on, and its home page offers a place to watch them. Some inns facing the sea have lost their view for now.

## Bathing there

- **English and tattoos.** The association’s English home and about pages say nothing about tattoos or bathing manners. The about page links to a guide called "How to enter Onsen" as a PDF, which this page has not checked. The association’s information about the earthquake is in Japanese only. Official sources give no English tattoo guidance for Wakura. Ask the Sōyu or your inn.
- **Compared with city baths.** No official Wakura source compares its water with baths in the cities. What the association does say is that the Sōyu uses 100% source water. Any bath using hot spring water, in a spa town or a city, has to post whether the water is diluted, heated, recirculated or disinfected.

## What it is eaten with

The association’s food page names *kinshi-uri*, a traditional Noto vegetable it says has long been used in *hōonkō* meals, dishes served at a Buddhist memorial service. For winter it names cold-season yellowtail (*kan-buri*), Kanō crab and Noto sea cucumber, and it calls pufferfish ovaries pickled in rice bran a delicacy made only in Ishikawa. These come from an earlier check of that page and were not rechecked for this update.

The farm ministry’s list of Ishikawa’s local dishes includes *buri daikon*, yellowtail simmered with daikon radish, but the association’s page does not name it as a dish, so this page does not call it Wakura’s. Restaurant hours are on the association’s map dated April 2026; check with each place.

## Getting there

Checked 11 October 2026. Fares are not on this page. After the August rain, check JR West and the association’s notices before you set off.

- **From Kanazawa.** Ishikawa prefecture’s article of 13 November 2025 gives about 52 to 57 minutes by the limited express Noto Kagaribi from Kanazawa to Nanao. That is to Nanao station; the article does not give a time to Wakura Onsen station.
- **From Toyama.** Toyama prefecture’s tourism site gives about 1 hour 30 minutes from Wakura Onsen station to Toyama by the Noto Kagaribi and the shinkansen Kagayaki.
- **The shinkansen.** JR West says the Hokuriku shinkansen opened from Kanazawa to Tsuruga on 16 March 2024.
- **Timetables.** The association’s timetable page (posted 15 March 2026) says JR and Noto Railway timetables changed on 14 March 2026 and Hokutetsu Noto buses on 15 March, and that services may change without notice because of the weather. The timetables themselves are PDFs.
- **Other ways in.** The association’s access page lists local buses (Hokutetsu Noto Bus, Notojima Kōtsū), the Noto Railway, the sightseeing trains *Hanayome Noren* on the Nanao line and *Noto Satoyama Satoumi* on the Noto Railway, and taxis. Highway buses run from Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya and Takaoka. By car, it is about 5 minutes from Wakura interchange on the Noto Satoyama Kaidō to the town. From Noto Satoyama airport there is a shared, reservation-only "furusato taxi". The page gives no journey times for the trains; the rail map is an image, not checked here.

## What people get wrong

- **"Wakura is closed since the earthquake."** It is partly open. As of the association’s latest entry (16 July 2026), 11 inns had reopened, and the Sōyu has been open since 26 March 2024.
- **"Everything is back to normal."** Some inns are still on temporary facilities, seawall works block some sea views, and the cooperative has one fewer member than before.
- **"The August 2026 rain declared a disaster in Nanao."** Ishikawa applied the Disaster Relief Act in four other municipalities. Nanao city did warn of flooded roads, and the newspaper reported cancellations and a train suspension.
- **"The heron was found by a farmer."** That is the prefecture’s English version; the association says a fishing couple.
- **"Wakura is the only onsen that rises from the sea."** The prefecture calls an undersea source a rarity; nobody official counts the others.
- **"The Sōyu closes on the 25th."** That was the old rule. Since April 2025 it closes on the fourth Thursday.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so. The official list of indications for each spring type is in the water types guide.
