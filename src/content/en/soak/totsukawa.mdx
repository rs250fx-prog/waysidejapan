Totsukawa is a mountain village in the far south of Nara prefecture, cut by deep river valleys, with no railway. Its hot springs fall into three areas, **Tōsenji**, **Totsukawa Onsen** and **Kamiyu**, which together form a nationally designated health resort, and in 2004 they pledged to use only free-flowing spring water. This page uses the environment ministry's 2026 plan for the area, the village office and its tourism association, Nara prefecture, the national land survey, Nara Kōtsū, research abstracts, the farm ministry and the Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau. Where they disagree, both are given. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring types named below, see [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/) and [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the analysis sign, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### A designated health resort

The environment ministry's plan for the area (cover dated March 2026) calls the **Totsukawa Onsen-kyō** the collective name for three spring areas, Tōsenji, Totsukawa Onsen and Kamiyu, over 573.32 ha, and describes it as "Nara's only natural high-temperature spring". It was designated a national health resort hot spring in **September 1957**.

### Six sources

The plan says six sources are in use for bathing at inns, day-bathing facilities, medical and care facilities, some also for drinking, and that owners measure temperature and flow every day. Its table gives no year:

| Area | Source | Temperature | Flow | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tōsenji | Tōsenji No. 1 | 59.1°C | 180 L/min | pumped; village-owned; alkaline simple sulphur |
| Tōsenji | Tōsenji No. 2 | 55.8°C | 454 L/min | — |
| Tōsenji | Tōsenji (Yu-no-sato) | 50.7°C | 65.65 L/min | natural flow; private |
| Totsukawa Onsen | Totsukawa No. 2 | 48.0°C | 200 L/min | — |
| Totsukawa Onsen | Totsukawa No. 7 | 71.0°C | 667 L/min | pumped; village-owned; sulphur-bearing sodium hydrogen-carbonate and chloride |
| Kamiyu | Kamiyu | 53.6°C | 124 L/min | pumped; private; sulphur-bearing sodium hydrogen-carbonate |

### The village's own figures

The village's onsen page (no update date, copyright 2018) gives one line for each area:

- **Tōsenji**: alkaline simple sulphur spring, 60°C, pH 9.1.
- **Totsukawa Onsen**: sodium hydrogen-carbonate and chloride spring, 70°C, pH 7.9.
- **Kamiyu**: sulphur-bearing sodium hydrogen-carbonate spring, 85°C, pH 7.7.

These do not line up with the plan. For Kamiyu the village gives 85°C, the plan 53.6°C, and a 2007 research abstract, citing literature, 57.8°C. For Totsukawa Onsen the village's 70°C may or may not refer to one of the plan's two sources at 48.0 and 71.0°C. The pH appears only on the village page. This page keeps the two sets apart and does not settle on one temperature.

### The 2004 free-flowing pledge

On **28 June 2004**, according to the ministry's plan, every hot-spring facility in the area stopped recirculating, heating, adding water and disinfecting with chlorine, offering "only genuine hot spring water" in a *gensen kakenagashi* declaration made "ahead of the rest of the country". The village tourism association says it covered **all 25 facilities** in the three areas, calls it the first in Japan, and says all facilities are tested for legionella regularly. The village says every one of its onsen facilities does not recirculate or reuse, heat, chlorinate or dilute its water.

"First in Japan" is the village side's description; the plan says "ahead of the rest of the country". What free-flowing water means, and what the bath sign tells you, is in the piece on [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

### Where the heat comes from

The plan, the village and the tourism association do not explain the heat. Research on southern Kii does:

- **A 2003 abstract (Tsunoda and others)** notes that high-temperature springs rise at Totsukawa and elsewhere in southern Kii although the area is not volcanic, and says a deep fracture system is likely involved.
- **A 2007 abstract (Hanamuro and others)**, using thermoluminescence on altered rock, estimates hot-water activity above about 100°C around Tōsenji until some tens of thousands of years ago and around Kamiyu until some hundreds of thousands of years ago, and suggests rainwater heated by gas and heat from deep fluids.
- **A 2004 release** by the predecessor of the Japan Atomic Energy Agency puts the heat of southern Kii's high-temperature springs down to deep fluids rather than magma.

The wider research is set out on the [Yunomine](/en/soak/yunomine/) page.

## Where it came from

### How old the springs are

The accounts of **Tōsenji**, the oldest of the three, differ:

- **The ministry's plan**: it is said to have risen in **Hōtoku 2 (1450)**, the oldest in the village, and records survive of visits by Sakuma Nobumori, banished by Oda Nobunaga, and by a son of Hashiba Hidenaga, among others.
- **The village**: it is said to have been brought forth by **En no Gyōja**, founder of mountain asceticism, and is the oldest spring in the village.
- **The village tourism association**: its history is long, and Sakuma Nobumori is said to have visited in **1581**.

This page gives all of them and chooses none. Of the others, the plan says Totsukawa Onsen is said to have been found by a charcoal burner in the Genroku era, and Kamiyu by local people in the Kyōhō era.

### The 1889 flood and Hokkaido

The village's history page says that in 1889 Totsukawa was six villages, with 2,403 households and 12,862 people. A great flood killed 168 people and injured 20, destroyed or swept away 426 houses and half-destroyed 184, and washed out half the paddy fields and a fifth of the other fields. Unable to rebuild, **600 households, 2,489 people,** moved to Hokkaido under the government's policy, founding what is now Shin-Totsukawa town. The next June the six villages merged into Totsukawa village. Another village page speaks of more than 1,000 mm of rain in a day.

Other bodies give other numbers. Nara prefecture says rainfall over 24 hours passed 1,000 mm from Wakayama to southern Nara, with **249 dead and 565 buildings destroyed in Nara prefecture**, and that **2,691** people from Totsukawa moved to Hokkaido. Shin-Totsukawa town says **about 2,500** settled there. The difference may come from counting the village or the whole prefecture; the sources do not say.

### The largest village?

The village calls itself the largest village in Japan. The ministry's plan says the same, giving **672.38 km²**, a fifth of Nara prefecture, 96% of it forest, with a population under 2,900. The national land survey's area table as of 1 July 2026 (published 25 September 2026) gives the same 672.38 km². The survey's own list of the 20 largest municipalities includes Rubetsu, a village in the Northern Territories, at 1,442.82 km² as a reference value, and Totsukawa is not in that top 20. Read against the full table, Totsukawa is the largest village only if the Northern Territories villages are left out; the next is Sarufutsu in Hokkaido at 589.99 km². The survey itself does not use the phrase "largest village".

### World Heritage

The plan says that in July 2004 the **Kohechi** pilgrimage route and the **Ōmine Okugake-michi** ascetic trail through the village were inscribed as part of the Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range. Hatenashi, a hamlet on the Kohechi, has also been chosen for the "100 villages of Japan".

## How the village works

### Three spring areas

The plan describes them this way. **Totsukawa Onsen**, beside the Futsuno dam lake, has the most inns and shops in the village. **Tōsenji** has inns and guesthouses scattered along a gorge. **Kamiyu** is a single inn by a river.

### Four public baths

The village lists four public baths (checked 13 October 2026):

| Bath | Area | Hours | Closed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Izumi-yu (泉湯) | Tōsenji | 10:00–20:00 | Tuesdays |
| Taki-no-yu (滝の湯) | Tōsenji | 8:30–20:00 | Thursdays |
| Hoshi-no-yu (星の湯), Subaru no Sato | Totsukawa Onsen | 12:00–17:00 (to 15:00 at Golden Week, Obon and New Year) | open daily |
| Iori-no-yu (庵の湯) | Totsukawa Onsen | 8:30–20:00 | Tuesdays |

Iori-no-yu also has a footbath and a drinking fountain. Single-entry prices are published without saying whether they include tax, so they are not given. The ministry's plan counts five public baths, two at Tōsenji and three at Totsukawa Onsen; the sources do not explain the difference.

**The three-bath ticket.** The village sells a ticket for any three of its four public baths at **¥1,000, tax included**, from each bath. Its page has no update date, so check the current price.

**Kamiyu's river bath.** A large open-air bath by the river, 9:00 to 17:00, closed on Thursdays except in the summer holidays (village). Its price is not given with tax.

### Footbaths, a drinking fountain and the yaen

- The ministry's plan says Totsukawa Onsen's drinking fountain was **the first in Nara prefecture**.
- The footbath in the Totsukawa Onsen bus stop waiting room is free, 7:00 to 18:30 daily (Nara Kōtsū).
- The Ikora exchange centre at Hiradani has a free drinking fountain and footbath, open every day (village).
- The *yaen*, a hand-pulled rope car across the river, is at Subaru no Sato and free (village).

### The Tanize suspension bridge

The village calls the Tanize bridge, **297 m long and 54 m high**, the longest steel-wire suspension bridge for everyday use in Japan. Crossing is free, and the village car park has room for 60. It is by the "Uenoji" bus stop; the tourism association says the bus from Yamato-Yagi stops there for about 20 minutes, long enough to walk partway across, and that the nearest onsen inn is about 40 minutes south by car, at Tōsenji.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The village's pages for visitors and travel businesses and the tourism association's Q&A say nothing about tattoos or how to bathe, and official sources do not give English guidance from the village. The Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau's English page, "Totsukawa Onsen — Kakenagashi Hot Spring", says each inn has its own onsen bath used without recirculation, and that Hotel Subaru offers day bathing and an onsen pool. The bureau's general guide for the Kumano area says some lodgings may refuse tattoos and suggests covering them, while noting that some places do not allow taped skin in the water. Ask each bath or inn.

### Compared with city baths

The village calls its springs *honmamon*, "the real thing", but no official source compares them with baths elsewhere. The 2004 pledge names the four things to check on any bath sign, recirculation, heating, chlorine and added water; see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

The village tourism association introduces two local foods:

- **Totsukawa *yubeshi***: a yuzu hollowed out, filled with miso, walnut, sesame and other things, wrapped in rice straw, hung and dried in the cold wind, a preserved food it calls unique to Totsukawa.
- ***Mehari-zushi***: a rice ball mixed with bonito flakes and wrapped in pickled *takana*, once a lunch for farm and mountain work wrapped in bamboo sheath, now popular with walkers on the old pilgrim road.

The farm ministry lists *mehari-zushi* among **Wakayama's** local dishes, for the southern area, not Nara's, and does not name Totsukawa. It lists Nara's *kakinoha-zushi* for Gojō and the Yoshino area and *chagayu*, tea gruel, for the whole prefecture, again without naming Totsukawa, and the village's food pages do not mention them. More in the [Kinki local dishes guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-kinki/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are published without saying whether they include tax, so they are not given.

The village says **no railway** runs there; come by car or bus.

- **From Yamato-Yagi** (Kintetsu). The village gives about 4 hours by Nara Kōtsū bus, or 2 hours by hire car, to Tōsenji (village office).
- **From Shingū** (JR). About 2.5 hours by bus or 1.5 hours by car (village).
- **From Tokyo.** The village suggests flying to Shirahama, 2.5 hours by Meikō bus to Hongū Taisha-mae, then 1 hour by Nara Kōtsū.
- **From Gojō** (JR). The ministry's plan gives about 4 hours by bus.

**The Yagi–Shingū bus.** Nara Kōtsū says its route runs 169.3 km with 168 stops, the longest in Japan among routes that do not use expressways. One service leaves Yamato-Yagi station (south) at 9:15, Gojō station at 10:31, stops at Uenoji for the bridge at 12:25, reaches the village office at 13:06 and Totsukawa Onsen at 13:39, then Hongū Taisha-mae at 14:23 and Shingū at 15:47. That is **4 hours 24 minutes from Yamato-Yagi** and **3 hours 8 minutes from Gojō**. On weekends and holidays the sightseeing express *Yamakaze* leaves Gojō at 13:06 and reaches Totsukawa Onsen at 15:39. Northbound, a bus leaves Shingū at 5:53 and reaches Totsukawa Onsen at 8:01.

By car, the tourism association gives about 3 hours from Osaka. From Nagoya, the Kumano Hongū tourism association suggests the Kintetsu limited express to Yagi, about 1 hour 50 minutes, then the bus. Check times with the operators before you travel.

[Yunomine](/en/soak/yunomine/) is about 55 minutes south on the same bus. For the coast, see [Nanki-Katsuura](/en/soak/nanki-katsuura/) and [Shirahama](/en/soak/shirahama/).

## What people get wrong

- **"You can take a train."** There is no railway; it is a bus of three to four hours or more from Yamato-Yagi or Gojō.
- **"Totsukawa is one hot spring."** It is three areas, Tōsenji, Totsukawa Onsen and Kamiyu, with different water.
- **"Kamiyu is 85°C."** That is the village's figure; the ministry's plan gives 53.6°C for its source.
- **"The free-flowing pledge is certified as Japan's first."** That is the village side's description; the ministry's plan says "ahead of the rest of the country".
- **"Totsukawa is the largest village, full stop."** By the national land survey's table, only if the Northern Territories villages are left out.
- **"Mehari-zushi is a Nara dish."** The farm ministry lists it for Wakayama; Totsukawa's tourism association presents it as local.
