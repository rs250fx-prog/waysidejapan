Akiu Onsen is a spa town in Taihaku ward, inside the city of Sendai in Miyagi, on the Natori river. It is close enough to Sendai station to reach by route bus, it carries a legend reaching back to a sixth-century emperor, and it is described by different official bodies as one of three quite different "three" groups of hot springs. This page uses Miyagi prefecture’s hot-spring table, Sendai city and its Akiu branch office, the Sendai tourism foundation (which runs the Sentabi and Discover SENDAI sites), the Akiu Onsen ryokan cooperative, Miyagi’s tourism federation, the Tōhoku tourism promotion organisation and JNTO. Where they disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the chloride spring named below, see [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### The prefecture’s figures

Miyagi prefecture publishes a table of hot-spring use for each spa area. It does not use the name "Akiu Onsen"; the row that fits is **Yumoto** in Taihaku ward, and a Sendai city leaflet describes "the Akiu Onsen town in the Yumoto district". As of **31 March 2024**, that row gives:

- **Sources**: 28 in all. 18 are in use, all pumped (none flowing on their own); 10 are unused, 5 of them flowing naturally and 5 pumped.
- **Temperature**: 2 sources under 25°C, 6 from 25°C to under 42°C, 19 at 42°C or more.
- **Flow**: 2,141.4 litres a minute, pumped.
- **Main spring type**: sodium-calcium chloride spring.

The temperature groups add up to 27, one short of the 28 sources; the table does not explain the gap. It gives no pH. Taihaku ward has other rows, such as Moniwa, Kōnosu and Kamigane, but the table does not say whether any of them belong to Akiu, so only the Yumoto row is used here. Sendai city’s own nature pages describe Kōnosu and Kamigane as separate springs along the Natori river before it reaches Akiu.

### The cooperative’s description

The ryokan cooperative describes the water as *enka dorui burōmu jaku-shokuen-sen*, at **45°C** and plentiful, without a year or a named source. "Weak salt spring" is an old-style name; under the current naming it corresponds to a sodium chloride spring. The cooperative’s name and the prefecture’s current one have different forms, and neither says whether they describe the same water. The cooperative does not explain the word *burōmu*.

Miyagi’s tourism federation, on a page last updated 31 March 2024, calls it a chloride spring that is gentle on the skin.

### Once it flowed on its own

The Sendai city leaflet, issued around 2020, says the original source stood by a wall at the three-way junction of the spa town and flowed on its own until some time between 1965 and 1974, when the number of sources grew and the natural flow stopped. That matches the prefecture’s table, where every source in use is now pumped.

### Where the heat comes from

Official sources do not say where Akiu’s heat comes from, or whether it is volcanic. Sendai city says the area’s representative mountains, Daitōdake (about 1,366 m) and Kamurodake (about 1,253 m), are "old volcanoes of the Tertiary". The national geological survey institute (AIST) lists both as Quaternary volcanoes from the early Pleistocene, around 1.7 million years ago, and gives Kamurodake as 1,356 m. Neither page connects these mountains to the hot water, so this page does not either.

### Not a health resort

Akiu does not have a plan as a national health resort hot spring on the environment ministry’s list. In Miyagi, only [Naruko](/en/soak/naruko/) does.

## Where it came from

### The emperor legend

Every official account begins with Emperor Kinmei, and every one says "it is said":

- **The ryokan cooperative**: around the Kofun period (531–570), the emperor is said to have healed a skin complaint in Akiu’s water and granted it the title *Natori no mi-yu*, the "honourable bath of Natori". It says the name appears in the poetry anthology *Shūishū* and the tale collection *Yamato monogatari*.
- **The Sendai tourism foundation**: when the emperor had a skin complaint, he bathed in water carried from Akiu and was cured; the title *mi-yu* followed. It adds that the warlord Date Masamune visited often in the Warring States period, and says records survive, without naming them.
- **Sendai city (Taihaku ward, page updated 20 September 2016)**: the spa opened around the time of Emperor Kinmei, given as **539–571**.
- **Miyagi’s tourism federation and the Tōhoku tourism organisation** say "1,500 years" and "the sixth century".

The two sets of years are probably two ways of writing the same reign; this page gives both. None of these is a date confirmed from records, and the sources themselves do not claim it is.

### The Date family

The cooperative says the water was guarded for generations as a bathing place of the Date family, lords of Sendai. Miyagi’s tourism federation says the domain kept a bathing place for the Date here, and that it was a busy *tōji-ba*, a place for long curative stays, used by samurai and commoners alike.

### The shrine and the 1855 earthquake

The Sendai city leaflet describes the Yu shrine in the Yumoto district. Its founding date is unknown, but it is said to predate a ridge tag of 1651. A stone in its grounds, the *Natori mi-yu* monument, carries the *Shūishū* poem and records that the source dried up after the great earthquake of **1855**, and that after the *yumori*, the hereditary keeper of the source, made a pilgrimage to the three sacred mountains of Dewa and rebuilt the shrine, the water came back. The leaflet also says one of the four original inns in the district gives its tradition as 1,000 years old and kept the source as *yumori* through the Edo period.

### Four stories for the name

The cooperative gives four explanations for the name Akiu and does not choose: a Heian-period man called Fujiwara no Akiu; a phrase meaning "keeping a hundred autumns", that is, long life; an Ainu word *abo* for a waterfall; and the autumn scenery. This page gives all four.

### Three "three"s

Different official pages place Akiu in different groups, and none says who chose them:

| Title | Listed with | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| *Nihon san-mi-yu*, Japan’s three honourable baths | Bessho (Shinano no mi-yu) and Nozawa (Inukai no mi-yu) | The ryokan cooperative, the Sendai tourism foundation, Miyagi’s tourism federation, the Tōhoku tourism organisation |
| *Ōshū san-kotō*, the three old springs of Ōshū | Naruko and Iizaka | Sendai city (Taihaku ward) |
| *San-meitō*, the three famous springs | Arima and Dōgo, "ranking with" them | Sendai city (Taihaku ward) |
| "Three Great Hot Springs" (English) | Not named | Discover SENDAI |

The cooperative links *san-mi-yu* to the emperor’s title in the legend, which is a story, not a selection by any body. Treat all of these as traditional titles. For the other members, see [Iizaka](/en/soak/iizaka/) and [Naruko](/en/soak/naruko/).

## How the town works

### The old shared bath

Locals have long called the area around the original source the *onsen-ba*, the "spa place", according to the Sendai city leaflet. In the Taishō period an open-air shared bath was made beside the source; it got a roofed building around 1932, and was closed around 1954 because the building was worn out and the flow had dropped. From 1967 the cooperative and the Yumoto source-holders’ association looked for a new site, and the bath moved to where it stands now; the leaflet calls it still a place to relax. Miyagi’s tourism federation’s 2025 brochure carries a photo captioned "Akiu Onsen shared bath". Official sources do not give its current hours or fee.

### How many inns

Miyagi’s tourism federation counts 19 inns. The cooperative’s site lists 14 members, and Discover SENDAI says 14. They may be counting different things; this page gives both.

### Rairaikyō gorge

Miyagi’s tourism federation says a walkway of about 650 m along Rairaikyō runs downstream from Nozoki bridge, and that the gorge, up to about 20 m deep, runs for about 1 km on either side of the bridge. The federation lists it among Sendai’s "100 green beauty spots". The Sendai tourism foundation says it was named in 1931 by Komiya Toyotaka, professor emeritus of Tōhoku University, and that its rock is a dacite tuff also called Akiu stone. The federation’s brochure says a heart-shaped hollow seen from Nozoki bridge has been chosen as a "lovers’ sanctuary".

### Akiu Ōtaki falls

Further up the valley is Akiu Ōtaki, a national Place of Scenic Beauty designated on **7 March 1942** (Sendai city, Cultural Heritage Online, Miyagi prefecture). Miyagi prefecture says the whole Natori river falls over a tuff cliff about 55 m high and about 6 m wide; Sendai city gives the same. The designation text on Cultural Heritage Online, in classical Japanese, gives a width that can be read as about 4.5 m or 45 m, so this page follows the prefecture and the city. Miyagi’s tourism federation says the falls are among "Japan’s 100 waterfalls", without saying who chose them. Above the falls, Ōtaki Fudō is, by the prefecture’s account, the inner temple of Risshakuji (Yamadera) in Yamagata, founded by the monk Jikaku Daishi. The federation’s brochure says the Banjiiwa cliffs are also a national Place of Scenic Beauty.

### Bases and day bathing

- **Akiu Sato Center** houses the Akiu onsen area tourist information office and lends bicycles.
- **Day bathing.** Miyagi’s tourism federation says many inns take day bathers, and lists the day spa Ichitarō no Yu in Tenshukaku nature park. Its prices are not given here because the brochure does not say whether they include tax.
- **Accommodation tax.** A notice on the cooperative’s site says Miyagi prefecture and Sendai city tax overnight stays from 13 January 2026. The notice itself was not readable, so the amount and conditions are not given; ask your inn.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

Discover SENDAI and JNTO have English pages on Akiu, but neither gives a tattoo rule or a guide to bathing, and the cooperative has no English pages. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Akiu. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each inn in advance.

The Sendai tourism foundation’s Japanese page has general bathing advice, credited to the Japan Health Development Foundation:

- Pour water over yourself before getting in, about ten scoops, starting far from the heart: ankles, wrists and so on.
- Get used to the heat with your lower body first.
- Rinsing off afterwards is better skipped, depending on the water.
- Drink water, and rest for at least 30 minutes after bathing.

That is general advice, not an Akiu rule. The same page explains chloride, simple and sulphate springs in general terms, without saying which Akiu sources they apply to. Its notes on benefits are not repeated here.

### Compared with city baths

No official Akiu source compares its water with baths in the city. What the prefecture’s table does show is that all 18 sources in use are pumped. For what the notice at the bath tells you, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

**No local dish tied to the spa.** The farm ministry lists 29 local dishes for Miyagi, such as *zunda-mochi* and *harako-meshi*, but none of its Miyagi pages mentions Akiu, and Akiu’s official pages do not mention zunda-mochi. Miyagi’s tourism federation’s brochure places a short column on the origin of the word *zunda* on its Akiu pages, but does not tie the dish to the spa. Eat zunda-mochi here as a Miyagi dish; more in the [Tōhoku local food guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-tohoku/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

- **From Tokyo.** About 1 hour 30 minutes by Tōhoku shinkansen to Sendai, not counting changes (Miyagi’s tourism federation, data as of December 2023).
- **From Sendai station by bus.** Buses leave from the west exit bus pool. The Miyagi Kōtsū route bus (Akiu line) is given as about 50 minutes by the Sendai tourism foundation and about 55 minutes by the federation. The Takeya Kōtsū **Sendai Seibu Liner** is given as about 40 minutes by both, but the federation’s Rairaikyō page says about 30. In English, JNTO says about 30 minutes by the Miyagi Kōtsū bus, and Discover SENDAI says 40 minutes on one page and 55 on another.
- **From Ayashi.** About 15 minutes by Sendai city bus from JR Ayashi station.
- **By car.** About 30 minutes from Sendai station (the foundation), or about 15 minutes from the Sendai-Minami interchange (the federation).
- **Around Akiu.** The shared taxi Gururin Akiu runs on weekdays only, 6:00 to 18:00, and bicycles can be hired at Akiu Sato Center (the foundation).

The times above are the sources’ round figures, not a timetable. For other Tōhoku spa towns, see [Naruko](/en/soak/naruko/), [Zao](/en/soak/zao/), [Ginzan](/en/soak/ginzan/), [Iizaka](/en/soak/iizaka/) and [Higashiyama](/en/soak/higashiyama/).

## What people get wrong

- **"Akiu is officially one of Japan’s three great springs."** Official pages use three different groupings, and none names who chose them.
- **"The spa dates from 539" (or 531).** Both are ways of placing an emperor legend, not dates from records.
- **"The water flows up on its own."** It did until some time between 1965 and 1974; every source in use in the prefecture’s 2024 table is pumped.
- **"Zunda-mochi is Akiu’s dish."** The farm ministry ties it to Miyagi, not to the spa.
- **"Akiu’s water is volcanic."** Official sources do not say where its heat comes from.
- **"The water cures things."** The legends say so; this page does not.
