Unazuki Onsen is a spa town in Kurobe, Toyama, at the mouth of the Kurobe gorge. Its hot water was brought down from further up the gorge, it opened in 1923, and it is where the Kurobe Gorge Railway’s trolley train starts. This page explains how it works using Toyama prefecture’s survey of its hot springs (fiscal 2026 edition, with the list of sources as of the end of March 2026), the Kurobe-Unazuki tourism bureau, Toyama’s official tourism site (Toyama Kankō Navi), the railway company and Kurobe city. Where sources disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any inn or bath.

For what the spring names mean, see the guide to [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/); for the notices about added water and heating, see the piece on [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/). The [Honshu hot springs guide](/en/soak/onsen-honshu/) covers the region as a whole, and [Wakura Onsen](/en/soak/wakura/) is another Hokuriku spa town in this series.

## Where it came from

### A plateau of peaches, and water from upstream

Toyama’s tourism site says the place was once an empty plateau covered with peach trees and called by a name written with the characters for peach and plain. Hot water was successfully piped there from Kuronagi Onsen, further up the Kurobe river, and Unazuki opened in **1923**. The same site’s feature on the spa marked its hundredth year in 2023.

Official sources give no date for when people began to use the water at Kuronagi itself. Toyama prefecture’s list of sources names Kuronagi Onsen Ryokan as the user of the Kuronagi sources, and that is all this page gives.

### The railway and the power stations

The Kurobe Gorge Railway’s own history of the line gives these points:

- **1919.** Tōyō Aluminium, set up on 7 December 1919 by Dr Takamine Jōkichi, began serious hydroelectric development of the Kurobe river.
- **1922–23.** Nihon Denryoku took over the work in 1922, and in 1923 began laying a light railway from Unazuki to Nekomata, 11.8 km.
- **1926 to 1937.** Trains ran from Unazuki to Nekomata from October 1926, on to Koyadaira from August 1930, and to Keyakidaira from July 1937.
- **Riding along.** It was a private line for carrying materials, and local people rode it free. From 1929 tourists were also let on for a fee; the history says this was prohibited in October 1951.
- **1953.** On 5 November 1953 Kansai Electric Power, with a licence under the local railways law, began running it as a public railway. In 1971 it set up a subsidiary, the Kurobe Gorge Railway Company.

The spa opened in 1923, the same year the line to Nekomata was begun. The two sources simply give the same year. No official source says one caused the other, so this page does not.

## The water

### What the prefecture lists

Toyama prefecture’s list of sources (as of the end of March 2026) gives two entries that matter here:

| | Unazuki Onsen | Kuronagi Onsen |
|---|---|---|
| Wells | Nos 3, 4, 6 and 9 | Nos 1, 5 and 7 |
| Where | Left bank of the Kuronagi river, in the Kurobe Okuyama national forest | Right bank of the Kuronagi river |
| Temperature | 70.3°C | 92.5°C |
| pH | 7.93 | 7.81 |
| Dissolved solids | 477.0 mg/kg | 615.6 mg/kg |
| Spring name | Simple spring | Simple spring |
| Last analysed | 7 November 2024 | 7 November 2024 |
| Flow column | 1,927 | 954 |
| Users | 18 permitted facilities, among them inns, the public bath and footbaths | Kuronagi Onsen Ryokan |

The Unazuki wells are listed as flowing by their own pressure and as drilled to 100 m. The flow figures are in the column for litres a minute, but the table does not make clear whether they count natural flow or water rising up a drilled well, so they are given only as the table’s figures.

The prefecture’s text says most of the springs along the Kurobe gorge are above 70°C, and that the hottest source in the prefecture is Kuronagi, at 92.5°C.

### Two ways of describing the source

- **The Kurobe-Unazuki tourism bureau** calls Unazuki the spa "whose source is Kuronagi Onsen".
- **Toyama’s tourism site** says the water was piped from Kuronagi, which its feature places about **6 km** further up the Kurobe river. It describes the water as a weakly alkaline simple spring, colourless and clear, with a source temperature above 90°C, and says it is said to be among the clearest in Japan. That is the site’s wording; official sources give no measure behind it.
- **Toyama prefecture’s list** gives Unazuki its own wells on the left bank of the Kuronagi river, as a separate entry from the Kuronagi sources on the right bank, at 70.3°C.

The official pages cited here do not explain how these fit together. This page gives all three and does not reconcile them.

### Where the heat comes from

Toyama prefecture sorts its hot springs into six types. It places Unazuki and Kuronagi in the type where **young granite and rhyolite are combined with a geothermal area**. Of that type, it says the water is mostly simple spring, hotter than 60°C, plentiful and flowing naturally, and that it is plentiful probably because rain and snow keep feeding it. The prefecture has a separate type for springs caused by volcanic activity, around the explosion craters of Tateyama at Tateyama Jigokudani, and it does not put Unazuki or Kuronagi in it.

The railway company describes the river: the Kurobe rises at Mount Washiba in the Northern Alps and runs 86 km, falling 3,000 m, through a V-shaped gorge.

Unazuki is not on the environment ministry’s list of *kokumin hoyō onsenchi*, the designated health resort onsen areas; the list has no entry for Toyama.

## How the town works

### The street and the footbaths

Toyama’s tourism site calls Unazuki the largest spa town in Toyama, with room for 3,500 guests, and the gateway to the Kurobe gorge. It says footbaths are dotted all over the town, naming Omokage and Momohara and one on the platform of Unazuki-Onsen station, and that there is a hot-water fountain in front of the station.

### The public bath

The public bath, the *sōyu*, is **Yumedokoro Unazuki**. Toyama’s tourism site says the footbath and the drinking fountain there are free. Kurobe city’s page and the bath’s own site both give a bathing fee, but neither says whether it includes tax, so this page does not give it. They also give different closing days: the bath’s own site says the fourth Tuesday, and the city’s page, which uses an older name for the bath, says something else. Check the bath’s own site for the current closing day.

### The trolley train in 2026

The Kurobe Gorge Railway is still dealing with the effects of the 2024 Noto Peninsula earthquake. What its own pages said, as checked on 13 October 2026:

- **Spring and summer.** From 24 April to 30 September 2026 the trains ran only between Unazuki and Nekomata and back.
- **Full line from 1 October.** The whole line from Unazuki to Keyakidaira reopened with the first train from Unazuki on Thursday 1 October 2026, according to a notice of 3 July 2026. From 1 October trains stop at Unazuki, Kuronagi, Kanetsuri and Keyakidaira; you cannot get on or off at Nekomata.
- **Keyakidaira.** Because of recovery work around the station, you can go no further than the station building and the visitor centre.
- **Booking.** The number of passengers to Keyakidaira is limited, and bookings open three months before the day of travel. The notice says Unazuki to Keyakidaira (from 1 October) is booked by telephone, and some journeys to Kanetsuri and Kuronagi online; but the fares page says Keyakidaira can now be booked on the web. The operator’s pages are not consistent, so check which applies when you book.
- **Times.** The timetable for 1 October to 23 November 2026 includes, for example, a train leaving Unazuki at 8:17 and reaching Keyakidaira at 9:33. If you turn straight round at Kanetsuri or Keyakidaira, allow at least 20 minutes there.
- **Changes.** The operator says the period and the stretch of line it runs can change with snow or snowmelt, and that it will announce the next season in late March.

Fares are not given here, because the fares page does not say whether they include tax. Check the operator’s site before you travel; the line has changed more than once this year.

## Bathing there

Official sources from the tourism bureau and Toyama’s tourism site give no guidance on how to bathe and nothing on tattoos, and no official Unazuki source compares its water with baths in the cities.

- **Language.** The Kurobe-Unazuki tourism bureau has English and other language menus. Toyama’s tourism site has an English version, whose Unazuki page this page has not checked.
- **Swimwear at Kuronagi.** Toyama’s tourism feature says swimwear or a bath towel may be worn in the mixed outdoor bath at Kuronagi Onsen. That is from a description of one inn, and it is given only as a fact, not a view of the inn.
- **Tattoos.** The general picture is in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each inn or bath in advance.
- **Compared with city baths.** What the prefecture does give is the figures above: a simple spring at 70.3°C from wells in the gorge, last analysed in November 2024.

## What it is eaten with

**Toyama Bay at the inns.** The guide to [Hokuriku local food](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-hokuriku/) quotes Toyama’s tourism site on the kaiseki at an inn in Unazuki, with white shrimp, firefly squid, *bai* whelk and yellowtail from Toyama Bay. The site’s Unazuki page also says that although the spa lies in a mountain gorge, the Japan Sea is close and the seafood is fresh, and mentions local sake and beer brewed with the gorge’s water. Its centenary feature, describing an inn, says firefly squid is served in spring and white shrimp in summer.

**What is not linked.** The farm ministry’s Toyama list includes [masu-zushi](/en/eat/sushi-masu/) and white shrimp *kakiage*. No official page ties masu-zushi to Unazuki, so this page does not pair them. White shrimp appears only in the tourism site’s descriptions of inns.

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page, because the official pages do not say whether they include tax.

- **From Tokyo by rail.** The railway company gives about 3 hours from Tokyo to its Unazuki station: the Hokuriku shinkansen to Kurobe-Unazuki-Onsen, the Toyama Chihō Railway from Shin-Kurobe to Unazuki-Onsen, then about 5 minutes on foot. It gives about 3 hours 10 minutes from Nagoya and about 3 hours 30 minutes from Osaka.
- **From the shinkansen station.** Toyama’s tourism site gives about 25 minutes by train from Kurobe-Unazuki-Onsen station.
- **By car.** The railway company gives 361 km from Tokyo to the Kurobe interchange and about 20 minutes from there to its Unazuki station. Toyama’s tourism site gives about 15 minutes from the Kurobe interchange on the Hokuriku expressway. The two figures differ; both are given.
- **In autumn 2026.** Plan around the limits above: a capped number of seats to Keyakidaira, and little to see there beyond the station and the visitor centre while the work goes on.

## What people get wrong

- **"The trolley runs to Keyakidaira all year."** In 2026 it ran only to Nekomata until 30 September, reopened to Keyakidaira on 1 October, and the operator says snow can change things.
- **"You can walk around at Keyakidaira as before."** For now, only the station building and the visitor centre.
- **"Unazuki’s water is volcanic."** Toyama prefecture places it in a granite-and-geothermal type and keeps the volcanic type for Tateyama.
- **"Unazuki and Kuronagi are the same source."** The tourism bureau says Kuronagi is the source; the prefecture lists them as separate wells on opposite banks.
- **"The spa was built for the railway."** The two began in the same year, 1923, but no official source checked for this page links them.
- **"It is the clearest water in Japan."** Toyama’s tourism site says it is said to be among the clearest. No official measurement is given.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so. The official list of indications for each spring type is in the water types guide, along with the environment ministry’s note that spa therapy is best done under a doctor’s guidance.
