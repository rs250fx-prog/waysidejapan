Toyako Onsen is on Lake Toya, in the town of Tōyako in Hokkaido. Its tourism association says the spring has a known birthday: hot water appeared after an eruption of Mt Usu in 1910. The water is pumped from twelve wells and named a sodium-calcium chloride spring. The lake is a caldera that the association calls the northernmost lake in Japan that does not freeze, and from spring to autumn fireworks go up over it every night. This page sets out what the association, the Japan Meteorological Agency, the Tōya-Usu geopark and JNTO say, keeping the association’s account of the spring apart from the agency’s account of the volcano. It does not rate any inn or bath.

For how Hokkaido’s onsen towns compare, see the [Hokkaido overview](/en/soak/onsen-hokkaido/). For what a chloride spring is, see [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/), and for the names in general, the guide to [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## Where it came from

### The lake

The association says Lake Toya was born in a huge eruption about 110,000 years ago, and calls it Japan’s third-largest caldera lake and its northernmost lake that does not freeze. The Tōya-Usu geopark says water filled a caldera about 10 km across to make the lake, and that an eruption of that size has happened only nine times in the Japanese islands in the past 120,000 years.

### Two starts for the spring

The association’s page on knowing the town gives two dates, and keeps them apart.

- **1910, the water.** During the Meiji 43 (1910) eruption known as the Shijūsan-zan eruption, the ground swelled, and in one place the lake water near the shore showed 24°C. After the activity ended, water at 42°C was found coming up in the lake at the northern foot of Nishi-Maruyama. The association says this is said to be the start of Toyako Onsen, and the town marked 100 years of the spring in 2010.
- **1917, the first inn.** The association quotes the history of the old town of Abuta: in the Toyako Onsen area, an inn opened in Taishō 6, 1917, seven years after the eruption, and that was when the spring was opened.

So the water came first and the inn seven years later. The association gives both and does not pick one as "the" opening, and neither do I.

On the same page the association says the hot spring resources of Toyako Onsen and Sōbetsu Onsen are closely tied to the activity of Mt Usu, and calls Toyako an extremely rare natural spring whose birth is clearly known. Its geopark page adds that both towns were born when hot water came up in the 1910 eruption, and that because a seismometer was taken onto an erupting volcano for the first time in the world during that eruption, the area is called "the birthplace of modern volcanology". That is the association’s wording. I could not find a sentence about the birth of the spring on the Tōya-Usu geopark’s own site.

### What the weather agency says about Usu

The Japan Meteorological Agency describes Mt Usu as a stratovolcano with a group of lava domes that formed about 10,000 to 20,000 years ago on the southern rim of the Tōya caldera. It became active again in 1663. The eruptions of 1910, 1943 to 1945 and 2000 were phreatomagmatic or phreatic (driven by water meeting magma or by steam), and since 1910 such eruptions have also happened at the foot of the mountain. The agency keeps Usu under constant observation.

Its account of the 2000 eruption, published by the Sapporo regional office:

- A phreatomagmatic eruption began on the north-west foot at 13:07 on 31 March 2000, and eruptions came on and off until September 2001.
- Because people were evacuated quickly before the eruption, there were no casualties.
- Volcanic bombs, mudflows and ground deformation damaged schools and other buildings, and electricity, water and other infrastructure.

Neither the agency’s Usu page nor its 2000 account mentions the hot springs. The link between Usu and the water is the association’s, and I give it as the association’s.

**Shōwa-shinzan**, the new mountain, was born in the 1943 to 1945 eruption, says the geopark, which also notes the Mimatsu Diagram, the record of its growth kept by a local man, Mimatsu Masao.

### The Ainu and the lake

The association tells an Ainu legend of the lake. A winged snake, Rapushi Oyau, lived in Lake Toya. Kotan-kor-kamuy sent two young men to the island in the lake to punish it; the foul smell went away, the snake took the form of a dragon, and since then it has been revered as the lake’s guardian, the dragon god. The association does not give the original source. The geopark says the area holds many traces of Ainu life, among them *chashi*, thought to be forts or places of ritual.

I could not find an official sentence giving the meaning of the Ainu name behind "Tōya", or any official account of the Ainu and the spring itself.

## The water

The association says Toyako Onsen has twelve source wells, drawing water from depths of 60 to 150 m, and names it a sodium-calcium-chloride spring. It does not give a year, temperatures, flow or pH.

The association lists what the water is said to be good for, and adds one sentence suggesting that the 2000 eruption may have made it more effective. It gives no basis, and nothing from the agency, the town or the geopark that I read says how the springs changed after 2000. I do not repeat either.

Hot spring heat is also put to work. The association says the CO2 from the fireworks is offset with J-credits from geothermal power generated by the Toyako Onsen hot spring cooperative.

## How the town works

### The fireworks

The association’s page for the 45th Lake Toya Long-Run Fireworks says:

- They run from 28 April (Tuesday) to 31 October (Saturday) 2026.
- They go up every night from 20:45 for about 20 minutes, until 21:05, from the lakeshore at Toyako Onsen.
- They go ahead in rain, but may be cancelled if the wind direction or speed is unsettled.

The association’s top page speaks of 450 fireworks every day; the fireworks page itself does not give a number.

**Winter fireworks.** The association’s page for the Toyako Onsen Winter Fireworks 2026 describes a ten-day run, with fireworks at 20:30 for about 8 minutes, cancelled in bad weather, free to watch, set off from the pier in front of the Tōyako Kisen offices, ten minutes on foot from the bus terminal. A special viewing booth needs a ticket bought in advance. The page does not give the dates of the run, and I found nothing yet for winter 2027. The association’s top page also lists a winter illumination tunnel.

### Footbaths and hand baths

The association says there are hand baths in ten places, put in in 2003, and footbaths in two:

- **Dōryū no Yu**, on the lakeside promenade. From 28 April to 31 October it is open 9:00 to 22:00, and after that 10:00 to 16:00. In the fireworks season you can watch them close up from here. **It is closed from 13 to 30 October 2026 for preservative coating work**, by the association’s notice dated 8 October 2026. That covers almost all of the last weeks of this year’s fireworks.
- **Yakushi no Yu**, in a small footbath park. From 28 April to 31 October it is open 9:00 to 22:00, and after that 10:00 to 20:00.

## Bathing there

**Day bathing.** The association’s stay page lists ten places offering day bathing with prices marked as including tax. For adults they run from 500 to 1,500 yen including tax, and one charges 2,000 yen including tax only on weekends, public holidays, school holidays and Golden Week. I do not name the places or give each price. The page has no update date. A separate day-bathing list was posted on 25 December 2025 as a PDF, which I did not open. The English Stay & Spa page gives no prices.

**Tattoos and manners.** I found nothing on tattoos or bathing manners on the association’s Japanese or English pages, including its knowledge, access and stay pages. Ask each facility before you go; the general picture is in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

**Compared with city baths.** I found no official source for Toya that compares its water with baths in the cities. What the association does say is that the water is pumped from twelve wells 60 to 150 m deep.

## Getting there

Checked on 12 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

**By train.** The association gives these limited express times to JR Tōya station:

- From New Chitose Airport, via Minami-Chitose, about 1 hour 30 minutes.
- From Higashi-Muroran, about 30 minutes.
- From Hakodate, about 1 hour 30 minutes in one section of the page, and about 2 hours in another. The English page also says about 2 hours. I do not choose.

JNTO gives 1 hour 40 minutes from Sapporo to Tōya station by the limited express bound for Hakodate. From Tōya station to the onsen, the association says, take a Dōnan bus or a taxi, about 20 minutes.

**By bus.** The association gives about 2 hours 45 minutes from JR Sapporo station on Dōnan Bus, reservation only, and about 2 hours 12 minutes from Muroran port via Date to Tōya station.

**By car.** The association gives about 2 hours 10 minutes from the Sapporo area by Route 230, about 3 hours 20 minutes from Hakodate by national roads, and about 2 hours 40 minutes from Tomakomai or Muroran. From New Chitose Airport it gives the expressway distance, 109.1 km from Chitose interchange to Abuta-Tōyako interchange, without a time; the English top page says about 1 hour 30 minutes from the airport.

**In winter.** The association’s access page says nothing about winter services, and bus timetables are in separate PDFs. It suggests asking the tourist information centre (0142-75-2446, 9:00 to 17:00, Japanese and English).

For the other towns in this series, see [Jozankei Onsen](/en/soak/jozankei/), [Sounkyo Onsen](/en/soak/sounkyo/) and [Noboribetsu Onsen](/en/soak/noboribetsu/).

## What people get wrong

- **"Toyako Onsen opened in 1910."** The water appeared after the 1910 eruption; the first inn, by the old Abuta town history the association quotes, opened in 1917.
- **"The weather agency says the springs come from Usu."** The agency’s pages on Usu do not mention the springs. The link is the tourism association’s.
- **"The 2000 eruption improved the water."** The association mentions that idea in one line with no basis. I found no official account of how the springs changed after 2000.
- **"The 2000 eruption killed people."** The agency says there were no casualties, because people were evacuated before it began.
- **"Usu is quiet now, so it is not watched."** The agency keeps it under constant observation.
- **"The lakeside footbath is always open in fireworks season."** Dōryū no Yu is closed from 13 to 30 October 2026.
- **"The water treats a condition."** The association lists indications. This page makes no health claims.
