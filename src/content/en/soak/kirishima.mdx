Kirishima Onsen is not one town but a scatter of hot springs on the south-eastern slopes of Kirishimayama, a group of volcanoes above Kirishima city in Kagoshima; the warning area for one of them also covers part of Miyazaki. The inns and baths sit in highland country between about 400 and 900 m. One of the volcanoes, Shinmoedake, has been active in recent years. This page explains the area using the environment ministry’s 2017 plan for Kirishima as a national health resort onsen, the ministry’s national park pages, the Japan Meteorological Agency, Kagoshima prefecture, the Kirishima tourism association, the farm ministry and JNTO. Where sources disagree, I give each. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For how Kirishima compares with the rest of Kyushu, start from the [Kyushu hot springs overview](/en/soak/onsen-kyushu/). For what its many spring names mean, see the pages on [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/), [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/) and [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/). [Ibusuki](/en/soak/ibusuki/), on the coast to the south, has its own guide.

## Where it is

### Two districts, or nine springs

The ministry’s plan places the facilities on the south-eastern slopes of Kirishimayama, at **400 to 910 m**, and divides them into two areas, each about 10 square kilometres:

- **The Kirishima onsen district**, including Shinyu, Hayashida, Iōdani, Maruo, Yunotani and Sekihira.
- **The Kirishima Jingū onsen district**, near the shrine.

Part of the area lies in Kirishima-Kinkōwan National Park. To the north, the plan says, runs the line of the Kirishima volcanoes: Karakunidake, the Ōnami pond, Shinmoedake, Nakadake and Takachihonomine.

The Kirishima tourism association’s English page counts it differently: **nine hot springs**, all at **600 to 850 m**. Neither source explains the other’s count, so I give each.

## Where it came from

### Ryōma’s honeymoon

The ministry’s plan says that in **1866** Sakamoto Ryōma of Tosa and his wife Oryō came here on what it calls **Japan’s first honeymoon**. It says the route they walked has been laid out as the Honeymoon Road, about **45 km** from Hamanoichi in Hayato to the tourist information centre near Kirishima Jingū, marked by 57 signposts, and that a "Ryōma Honeymoon Walk in Kirishima" is held every March.

Kagoshima prefecture’s tourism site, describing Shiohitashi Onsen Ryōma Park, says Ryōma **is said to** have stayed there on his honeymoon. The park has a small Ryōma museum, a footbath and a monument to the couple’s stay. The prefecture’s page gives no year.

"Japan’s first honeymoon" is the plan’s wording. No other official source backs it, so it is given here as the plan’s.

### One of the first national parks

The ministry’s plan calls Kirishima-Kinkōwan "the national park first designated in Japan, in 1934". The ministry’s own history page says that on **16 March 1934**, Setonaikai, Unzen and Kirishima were designated together as Japan’s first national parks. Its page for the park calls it one of the country’s first. So I say "one of the first three", as on the [Unzen](/en/soak/unzen/) guide. The farm ministry’s Kagoshima page also calls Kirishima the first national park designated in Japan.

## The volcano

### What the ministry says

The ministry’s park page says the Kirishima area has more than 20 volcanoes, large and small, and that crater lakes, fumaroles, hot springs and highlands came with the volcanic activity. The plan describes where the springs are, at the foot of the volcanoes, but does not explain how the water forms. No official source explains that, so this page goes no further.

### Shinmoedake, as checked on 12 October 2026

The Japan Meteorological Agency’s page for Shinmoedake showed:

- **Eruption alert level 2** (restrictions around the crater), with a crater-area warning in force.
- The level was **lowered from 3** (no entry to the mountain) **to 2 at 11:00 on 17 October 2025**.
- The latest commentary, **No. 41, issued at 16:00 on 9 October 2026**, kept the warning in place.
- **No eruption had been observed since 8 September 2025.**
- Sulphur dioxide was being released at several hundred tonnes a day, and the agency said activity remained elevated and **an eruption was still possible**.
- **The warning range**: large ballistic rocks within about **2 km** of the crater, and pyroclastic flows within about **1 km**. The areas concerned are Kobayashi city in Miyazaki and Kirishima city in Kagoshima.

Official sources do not show where the onsen area sits relative to that range, so this page does not say whether any spring is inside or outside it. Alert levels can change at any time, so check the agency before you go.

### The wider mountain

The agency’s page for Kirishimayama as a whole still carried an older announcement, from 6 December 2016, of an eruption forecast with no particular change apart from the areas around Ohachi, Shinmoedake and Ebino Kōgen (Iōyama). Its latest commentary for the mountain as a whole was No. 1 of 17 January 2026, and its monthly report for September 2026 was issued on 8 October.

## The water

### Sources, as of 2017

The ministry’s plan says **49 sources are used by 41 facilities**. Its table gives temperature, flow, spring type and how each source rises: naturally, by its own pressure from a bore, or by pump. Not every figure in the table is legible. Of those that are:

- **Temperature**: from **36.5°C** (Sekihira No. 1) to **94.8°C** (Maruo No. 43).
- **Flow**: from **28 litres a minute** (Sekihira No. 1) to **1,900 litres a minute** (Iōdani No. 13). Many sources are marked "not surveyed".
- **In the Jingū district**, Yunono No. 1 is a simple sulphur spring at 59.1°C and 1,700 litres a minute.

These are the plan’s figures from 2017. Official sources give no current count.

### Many kinds

The plan’s table lists, among others:

- simple springs
- simple sulphur springs (hydrogen sulphide type)
- simple acidic springs
- magnesium·calcium·sodium-hydrogen carbonate springs
- sulphur-containing sodium-chloride springs
- sodium·calcium·magnesium-hydrogen carbonate·sulphate springs

Some of these are older forms of the names. The deep-dive pages linked above explain how to read them. Unlike towns with one main water, Kirishima’s plan gives no single type for the whole area.

## How the area works

### Footbaths and baths

The plan says the area has many footbaths. It also sets out policies for multilingual signs and for health promotion, and notes a forest therapy base certified in 2007.

The tourism association’s English page says opening hours can change and suggests asking each facility. Its English home page leads to a list of the springs, without separate notes on spring types, Ryōma or tattoos.

### Shiohitashi Onsen Ryōma Park

As given on Kagoshima prefecture’s tourism site, checked on 12 October 2026:

- **Hours:** 9:00 to 18:00, last bath entry 17:30.
- **Closed:** Mondays, or the next day if Monday is a public holiday.
- **Prices:** the page lists bath and museum fees but does not state whether they include tax, so none are given here.

## Bathing there

Official sources give no bath prices in Kirishima with a stated tax status (so none are given), no tattoo guidance beyond JNTO’s general advice, no Kirishima-specific guidance on sulphur waters, and no comparison of the water with baths in the cities.

- **Tattoos.** The tourism association’s English pages do not mention tattoos. JNTO’s general guide (undated) says some onsen refuse people with tattoos, that you should check in advance, that a private bath, *kashikiri-buro*, is an option, and that small cover sheets only suit very small tattoos. The wider picture is in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).
- **Manners.** JNTO’s guide says to leave your clothes, swimwear included, in the changing room, wash before you get in, keep the small towel out of the water, tie up long hair, not swim or splash, and not take phones or cameras in.
- **Sulphur.** Several of the plan’s sources are sulphur or acidic springs. The ministry’s general rules on these waters, including on rinsing and hydrogen sulphide, are on the [acidic and sulphur springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/) page.

## What it is eaten with

Official sources tie no dish to the Kirishima springs, and give nothing on Kirishima tea or any link between it and the springs.

- **Sweet potato.** The farm ministry’s Kagoshima page mentions Kirishima’s scenery and many hot springs in one paragraph and the area’s sweet-potato dishes, *karaimo ryōri*, in another. It does not connect them.
- **Black pork and wagyu.** The same page treats these as the prefecture’s livestock as a whole, not as Kirishima’s.
- **Hiyajiru.** Miyazaki’s cold miso soup is placed by the farm ministry in the centre of the prefecture. Its page does not mention Kirishima or hot springs, so this page does not link them.

More of Kagoshima’s and Miyazaki’s dishes are on the [Kyushu local food](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-kyushu/) page.

## Getting there

As given by Kagoshima prefecture’s tourism site and the Kirishima tourism association, checked on 12 October 2026. Neither shows an update date. Timetables change, so check the operator before you travel. Fares are not on this page.

- **From Kagoshima Airport.** Airport bus to Kirishima Onsen (Maruo), about **34 minutes**. About **30 minutes** by car via prefectural road 56 and national route 223; the tourism association also gives about 30 minutes by car.
- **From Hakata.** Kyushu shinkansen to Kagoshima-Chūō, about **1 hour 16 minutes** at the fastest.
- **From Fukuoka Airport.** About 50 minutes by air to Kagoshima Airport.

Official sources give no journey times to the onsen area from Kagoshima-Chūō, Miyazaki, Kumamoto or Oita.

## What people get wrong

- **"Kirishima Onsen is one town."** The ministry’s plan describes two districts of scattered springs; the tourism association counts nine.
- **"The onsen area is inside the Shinmoedake danger zone,"** or **"is outside it."** Official material confirms neither. Check the Japan Meteorological Agency before you go.
- **"Kirishima was Japan’s first national park."** It was one of three designated on the same day in 1934.
- **"Ryōma’s honeymoon was officially Japan’s first."** That is the ministry plan’s wording; no other official source gives it.
- **"Kirishima has one kind of water."** The plan lists simple, sulphur, acidic, hydrogen-carbonate and chloride waters, among others.
- **"The volcano heats the springs,"** as a confirmed mechanism. The ministry says the springs came with the volcanic activity but no official source explains how.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so. The official list of indications by spring type is in the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).
