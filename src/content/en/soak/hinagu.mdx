Hinagu is a hot spring town in Yatsushiro city, on the coast of the Yatsushiro Sea (also called the Shiranui Sea) in southern Kumamoto. It has a founding legend dated to 1409, a street of grilled-chikuwa shops, and a public bath that began as a domain bath. It is also close to the area hit by the earthquake of 28 July 2026, and in October 2026 the town and its railway were still recovering. This page uses the Hinagu Onsen inns’ association, Yatsushiro city and its tourism portal (Kinasse Yatsushiro), the bath centre’s own site, Kumamoto prefecture’s hot spring site, Hisatsu Orange Railway, the national earthquake research body and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the simple spring named below, see [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## After the July 2026 earthquake

The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) records a magnitude 7.1 earthquake, maximum intensity 7, in the Kumamoto region at 16:27 on **28 July 2026**, followed at 17:08 by a magnitude 6.1 in the Amakusa–Ashikita area and on 29 July by a magnitude 5.8 there, both intensity 5-lower. It set up a temporary tsunami gauge at Yatsushiro port on 8 August, and on 10 August warned of flooding along the Yatsushiro Sea coast.

As of mid-October 2026:

- **The railway.** Hisatsu Orange Railway, in a notice last updated on 6 October, said its suspended section from Yatsushiro to Higo-Taura would reopen along its whole length **from the first train on 13 October 2026**, on the normal timetable, subject to aftershocks and weather. Replacement buses ran until the last service on 12 October. It lists track deformation between Higo-Kōda and Hinagu Onsen, among other places, and a broken feeder line between Hinagu Onsen and Higo-Futami. **Hinagu Onsen station is inside this section.** Whether trains ran on 13 October could not be confirmed.
- **The public bath.** The bath centre’s site says the Higashi-yu is not operating and the main building is on reduced hours, and that water may run short (details below).
- **The city.** Yatsushiro city’s disaster site was still posting recovery notices in October, such as a third round of applications for temporary housing (8 October) and a disaster-waste collection site (6 October). Its tourism portal carries notices of city events cancelled because of the earthquake.

Check the railway and the bath before you go.

## The water

### What the sources say

- **The inns’ association**: a weakly alkaline simple spring, soft, "said to be" a *bijin-yu* (a "beauty bath"), and drinkable. The opening of the same page says alkaline simple spring. It describes soft water, a faint smell of sulphur and a spout stained yellow.
- **Kumamoto prefecture’s hot spring site**: simple spring (weakly alkaline).

In the environment ministry’s table of old and new names, the old "simple spring" becomes "simple spring" or "alkaline simple spring" today. The "beauty bath" label is the association’s phrase, not a finding.

Official sources do not give the number of sources, flow, pH or source temperature. Yatsushiro’s tourism portal says 16 baths, at inns and public bathhouses, are popular for drop-in bathing; that is a count of places to bathe, not of sources. Hinagu is not on the environment ministry’s list of national health resort hot springs.

### The sea and the fault

The inns’ association says that at the Shimo-yu, just below the Onsen shrine, where the spring was first found, "the sea once came right up to there". The farm ministry’s Kumamoto overview describes the Yatsushiro Sea as calm, a fine fishing ground.

The national earthquake research body (Headquarters for Earthquake Research Promotion) describes the **Hinagu fault zone**, running from near Kiyama in Mashiki town through Ashikita town to the southern Yatsushiro Sea, possibly about 81 km long, joined at its northern end to the Futagawa fault zone and divided into three sections: Takano–Shirahata, Hinagu and Yatsushiro Sea. It says the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake is thought to have come from the Futagawa section, and that the Yatsushiro Sea section’s latest activity may have been the Higo earthquake of 744. The page does not discuss hot springs. Official sources do not explain how Hinagu’s water rises, so this page does not tie the spring to the fault.

## Where it came from

### Finding the spring: four tellings

Every source dates the discovery to **1409** (Ōei 16), about 600 years ago, and tells it as a legend. The details differ, and this page does not choose:

- **The inns’ association.** In the Nanboku-chō wars, Kai Shigemura, on the Ashikaga side, fought Kikuchi Takeshige of the Southern Court and lost. His man Hamada Ukon, wounded, reached the Hinagu area, married a village woman and had a son, Rokurōzaemon. Rokurō prayed for his father’s sword wound to the Itsukushima deity of Aki, and on the last day of his vow a message told him to dig in the shallows of the sea. Hot water came up.
- **The bath centre’s site.** Hamada Ukon, a man of Kikuchi Takeshige, governor of Higo, was wounded on **4 September 1338** (Engen 3) in fighting around Kuradake, on the border of the Aso and Kōshi districts, and reached a small island at Yatsushiro. His son prayed to the deity Ichikishima-hime of Aki, found an odd stone on the tidal flat as a dream told him, and dug. The spring came up in 1409 and was praised as *Kōkan-sen*, the "spring of filial piety".
- **Kumamoto prefecture’s site.** Hamada Rokurō prayed to heal his father’s sword wound, was told in a dream where to go, and found hot water. It says the place is today’s Hon-yu, which was then in the sea.
- **Yatsushiro city.** The Ushi-no-yu festival began because the spring was found on the *doyō no ushi* day in high summer, and it is said that bathing on that day is worth a thousand days. In 2026 the festival was held in the spa town on **26 July**, with free bathing that day (page updated 28 July 2026).

The inns’ association’s front page also has a phrase giving 610 years since opening; it may not have been updated, but the page does not say.

### The domain bath

The bath centre’s site gives a separate history of its building: it began about 350 years ago, in the Edo period, as **Gozen-yu**, a bath run by the Hosokawa domain of Higo; from Meiji to Shōwa it was the Hinagu Onsen Hon-yu; from 1967 it was the Onsen Center; and on **21 July 2009** it reopened as Banpei-yu.

## How the town works

### The street

Yatsushiro’s tourism portal calls Hinagu a spa town with a Shōwa-era feel, where workshops of bamboo craftsmen and shops grilling chikuwa line the main street and back lanes. The tourist information office is **Hinagu Yume Sōko**, about 700 m from Hinagu Onsen station, according to the railway.

The inns’ association describes some of its member inns as old wooden buildings: one as a three-storey wooden building registered as a national tangible cultural property, another as a three-storey wooden inn founded in 1925 whose tubs and washing-area tiles are still from the Taishō period. Those are the association’s descriptions; this page does not name the inns.

### Banpeiyu baths in winter

Yatsushiro’s tourism portal says that in winter especially, baths have *banpeiyu*, a very large citrus, floating in them. The inns’ association posted "banpeiyu baths have started" on 25 December 2024. *Yatsushiro tokusan banpeiyu* is protected as geographical indication No. 94, registered on 30 March 2020, grown in Yatsushiro city and Hikawa town; the ministry’s entry does not mention Hinagu.

### The public bath

The **Hinagu Onsen Center (Banpei-yu)** is at 316 Hinagu-Nakamachi, Yatsushiro, run under its own site with, it says, Yatsushiro city’s agreement. 1F is the public bath **Hon-yu**; 2F is a large bath with sauna and outdoor bath, where the men’s and women’s sides are swapped; 3F has family baths. Its standard hours are 10:00 to 22:00, temporarily 10:00 to 20:00, closed on the third Tuesday of each month and on 1 January; in October 2026 the closing day is 20 October. It publishes a price table but does not say whether tax is included, so no prices are given here.

Its notices after the earthquake: the 2F large bath was opened free to people affected by the earthquake, and the bath may run out of water and close early. From **16 September 2026**, the Hon-yu and the large bath (without sauna or cold bath) are open 10:00 to 20:00, and some family baths 10:00 to 18:00, without phone booking. The **Higashi-yu is not operating**.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The inns’ association, the bath centre’s site and Yatsushiro’s tourism portal do not mention tattoos or how to bathe, and the portal’s English is a machine-translation link. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Hinagu. That is not the same as saying tattoos are allowed; ask each inn or bath in advance. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

JNTO’s general advice: leave your clothes, swimwear included, in the changing room; wash and rinse before getting in; keep your small towel out of the water; tie up long hair; do not swim or splash; leave your phone outside. It says some traditional baths refuse tattoos but this is starting to change, and suggests tattoo-friendly places or a private bath.

### Compared with city baths

No official Hinagu source compares its water with baths in the city. For what the notice at the bath tells you, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## What it is eaten with

- **Hinagu chikuwa.** The inns’ association says Hinagu’s chikuwa began in **1883** (Meiji 16), when a man named Iwasaki, thinking it a waste to throw away fish caught in excess, wrapped it round bamboo and grilled it. Yatsushiro city calls chikuwa "Hinagu’s speciality", and in 2026 sold a *Chikuwan Dog*, a hot dog named after the city mascot Chikuwan, at 12 shops in the city (page updated 28 July 2026). It is **not** among the 29 Kumamoto dishes on the farm ministry’s list, and the ministry’s Kumamoto pages do not mention Hinagu. It is a speciality of the town by the city’s and the inns’ account, not a ministry-listed local dish.
- **Banpeiyu.** Above, in the baths and as a protected product of Yatsushiro.
- **Shaku tempura.** The ministry gives the Yatsushiro, Arao and Tamana areas as the main areas for *shaku* tempura. It does not mention Hinagu or the spring.

For Kumamoto’s food as a whole, see [Kyushu local food](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-kyushu/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Official Hinagu sources do not give journey times from Yatsushiro, Kumamoto or Fukuoka; fares are not on this page.

- **By train.** Hinagu Onsen station is on Hisatsu Orange Railway, within its Yatsushiro to Higo-Taura section. The station is staffed from 7:55 to 16:00. The railway says some trains have been cancelled since 1 August 2025 because of a shortage of drivers, apart from the earthquake.
- **To Yatsushiro.** JR Kyushu said the Kyushu shinkansen was running along its whole length from 18 September 2026, on a changed timetable, and the Kagoshima main line on its normal timetable from 10 October. The transport ministry’s report as of 5 October, before that, recorded replacement buses between Uto and Yatsushiro and transfers onto the shinkansen between Kumamoto and Shin-Yatsushiro.
- **A precedent.** After heavy rain on 11 August 2025, the Yatsushiro to Hinagu Onsen section was suspended and reopened on 27 September 2025, according to Yatsushiro city.

## What people get wrong

- **"The trains are back to normal."** The railway planned to reopen on 13 October 2026, subject to aftershocks and weather, and has run a reduced service since 2025 for lack of drivers. Check on the day.
- **"Hinagu’s water comes from the Hinagu fault."** The fault zone shares the name; no official source links it to the spring.
- **"Chikuwa is a Kumamoto local dish."** Yatsushiro and the inns call it Hinagu’s speciality; the farm ministry’s Kumamoto list does not include it.
- **"The spring was found in 1338."** 1338 is the battle in the bath centre’s telling; every source gives 1409 for the spring, as a legend.
- **"A bath on the festival day is worth a thousand."** That is what the city says "is said". This page makes no health claims; the official indications by spring type are in the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).
