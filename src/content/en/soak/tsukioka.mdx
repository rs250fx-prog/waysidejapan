Tsukioka Onsen is a hot spring town in Shibata city, Niigata prefecture. It is young by Japanese standards: the inns’ association dates it to 1915, when hot water came up during a search for oil. It is known for sulphur water that the association describes as emerald green, and it has built itself a walkable centre with a free footbath, a free hand bath and shops selling Niigata food and sake. This page uses the Tsukioka Onsen inns’ association (in Japanese and English), Niigata prefecture’s tourism association and its site *Niigata Kankō Navi*, Shibata city, the environment ministry and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For how to read a spring’s name and the analysis sign, see the guide to [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## Where it came from

### Oil, not legend

Both the inns’ association (in Japanese and English) and Niigata prefecture’s tourism association say Tsukioka opened in **1915** (Taishō 4). The inns’ association says the water came up by chance while people were drilling for oil, and that the place began as a *tōjiba*, a spa for long stays to take the waters.

There is no founding monk or healing animal here. Official sources give only this one account, and Shibata city does not publish a history of the town.

### Geisha

The inns’ association says Tsukioka is an unusual spa town in still having close to 30 *geigi* (geisha) registered.

## The water

### What the sources say

- **The inns’ association**: 51°C at the source, a "sulphur-bearing sodium chloride spring" (*gan-iō–natorium–enkabutsu sen*), weakly alkaline, hypotonic and high-temperature.
- **Niigata Kankō Navi**: a "*gan-shokuen ryūka-suiso sen*", that is, a salt-containing hydrogen sulphide spring, at 51°C.

The second is an old name. In the environment ministry’s table of old and new names, the old "salt hydrogen sulphide spring" becomes today’s "sulphur-bearing sodium chloride spring (hydrogen sulphide type)". The inns’ association’s current name, though, does not add "hydrogen sulphide type". Read through the ministry’s table, the prefectural name corresponds to the hydrogen sulphide type; without the analysis sheet, this page does not state that the water is that type. For what the type label means, and the environment ministry’s cautions about hydrogen sulphide in bathrooms, see [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/); for the chloride side, see [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/).

Official sources do not give the pH, the number of sources, the flow or the year of measurement.

### The colour

The inns’ association calls the water emerald green, and says it is said to have "seven faces": it can turn milky white, and shifts slightly with the season and the weather. It also says the water is strong enough to blacken precious metals.

### "Top class" and "number one"

The inns’ association calls Tsukioka a sulphur spring with "the highest content in Japan", "top class in Japan as a *bijin no yu*" (a "beauty bath"), and "among the highest sulphur content in the country". Niigata Kankō Navi also says "among the highest sulphur content in the country". Shibata city calls it "the onsen that makes you even more beautiful". None of them says who made the comparison, and no environment ministry or prefectural document comparing sulphur content between spa towns was found. These are the town’s and city’s own descriptions, not findings, and this page makes no beauty or health claims.

### Volcanic or not

The inns’ association’s account is of water struck during oil drilling. Official sources do not explain the geology, so this page does not call Tsukioka a volcanic spring. Tsukioka is not on the environment ministry’s list of national health resort hot springs; the Niigata entries are Iwamuro, Muikamachi, Seki and Tsubame, and the Gozu onsen district.

## How the town works

The inns’ association calls the centre an *aruki-taku naru onsengai*, "a spa town that makes you want to walk". From its page, as checked on 13 October 2026:

- **Yutabi footbath** (湯足美). Open 8:00 to 21:00, no closing day, free. Towels are sold at nearby inns.
- **Gensen no Mori**, "the source grove", which the association calls the birthplace of Tsukioka Onsen. It has a hand bath, a statue you pour water over, and a drinking fountain the association itself calls "self-proclaimed the worst-tasting hot spring in Japan". Open 8:00 to 22:00, free.
- **Tsukiakari no Niwa**, "the moonlight garden", a free garden.
- **Shops** themed on Niigata: one where you can taste the brands of every sake brewery in the prefecture, a rice-cracker shop where you grill your own, and shops of Niigata food. Niigata Kankō Navi describes the street in the same way.

Shibata city’s page on walking the town (updated 24 June 2021) puts the footbath at the centre of the spa town and lists two free car parks for visitors: the Ashiyu Yutabi car park, for about 20 cars, and the Iyashi no Sato car park, for about 60.

The inns’ association’s hotel pages do not list day-bathing prices, and no official page gives a bathing price with tax stated, so none is given here.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The inns’ association’s English pages (top, about, walking, hotels, access) do not mention tattoos or how to bathe, and official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Tsukioka. That is not the same as saying tattoos are allowed; ask each inn in advance.

JNTO’s general advice: leave your clothes, swimwear included, in the changing room; wash and rinse before getting in; keep your small towel out of the water; tie up long hair; do not swim or splash; leave your phone outside. It says some traditional baths refuse tattoos but this is starting to change, and suggests tattoo-friendly places or a private bath.

### Compared with city baths

No official Tsukioka source compares its water with baths in the city. For what the notice at any bath tells you about added water, heating and recirculation, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## What it is eaten with

- **Shibata city’s specialities** (page updated 12 April 2024): local sake from four breweries, Japanese sweets, ***noppe*** ("one of Niigata’s local dishes"; as part of food education, the city aims for every third-year junior high school pupil to be able to cook a dinner with noppe on their own), ***kara-zushi***, a pressed sushi made with okara (soy pulp) and made in Shibata since the middle of the Edo period, Shiunji Pioneer Pork, and *jōki pan*, steamed bread also called *poppo-yaki*.
- **The farm ministry.** Its list of 30 Niigata dishes includes *noppe*, eaten across the prefecture, with the story of keeping the whole pot in the snow, and *hegi soba*, said to have begun in the Uonuma area. Neither the list nor the ministry’s Niigata overview mentions Shibata or Tsukioka.

So *noppe* appears both on Shibata city’s list and the ministry’s, but neither ties it to Tsukioka Onsen. For the region’s food, see [Hokuriku local food](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-hokuriku/); for the sake, see [Niigata sake](/en/eat/sake-niigata/).

## Getting there

From the inns’ association’s access page, which carries no update date but is marked 2026 (checked 13 October 2026). Bus fares are given there without a tax note, so they are not given here.

**Direct buses, run by Niigata Kōtsū.** There are very few. Check the times before you travel.

| Route | Time | Departs | Returns from Tsukioka |
|---|---|---|---|
| Niigata station ⇄ Tsukioka | about 60 min | 13:00, 15:30 | 10:15, 14:10 |
| Shibata station ⇄ Tsukioka | about 25 min | 10:50, 15:30 | 10:20, 11:15 |

The Niigata bus takes no reservations: it is first come, first served, up to 50 passengers, paid in cash or PayPay. If it is full, the association suggests a bus bound for Murakami to Shibata or Toyosaka station instead. At Shibata the stop is the station-front rotary; at Tsukioka it is beside "Imai iriguchi".

- **To Niigata.** About 2 hours 10 minutes from Tokyo by the Jōetsu shinkansen. From Shin-Osaka or Nagoya, the association routes you via Tokyo; it also gives a route via the Hokuriku shinkansen and Jōetsumyōkō. Official sources do not give the JR time from Niigata to Shibata.
- **By air.** From Niigata airport, the association gives about 25 minutes by limousine bus to Niigata station and then the direct bus, or **about 40 minutes by car** straight to Tsukioka.
- **By car.** From Tokyo by the Kan’etsu, Hokuriku and Nihonkai-Tōhoku expressways to Toyosaka Niigata-Higashikō interchange, then 15 km; the association gives times for each section, not for the whole trip. Niigata Kankō Navi gives about 20 minutes from Toyosaka Niigata-Higashikō interchange and about 30 minutes from Yasuda interchange on the Ban’etsu expressway.
- **From Tsukioka station.** Niigata Kankō Navi says it is 5 minutes by bus from Tsukioka station on the JR Hakushin line. The inns’ association does not mention this station, and official sources do not give the bus times from it.

## What people get wrong

- **"Tsukioka is an ancient spa."** Both official accounts give 1915 and oil drilling.
- **"It is the best sulphur spring on the Sea of Japan coast."** That phrase was not found on the official pages. The association’s "highest in Japan" and "top class" name no one who judged them.
- **"The sign says hydrogen sulphide type."** The inns’ association’s spring name does not. The old prefectural name converts to it in the ministry’s table, but the analysis sheet has not been checked.
- **"Echigo Kōtsū runs the bus."** The inns’ association says the direct buses are run by Niigata Kōtsū.
- **"It is a volcanic spring."** No official source says so.
- **"Noppe is Tsukioka’s dish."** Shibata city and the farm ministry list it; neither links it to the spa.
- **"The water cures things."** This page makes no health claims. The official indications by spring type are in the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

Other Niigata and Hokuriku spa towns on this site: [Echigo-Yuzawa](/en/soak/echigo-yuzawa/) in the south of Niigata, and in Ishikawa, [Yamanaka](/en/soak/yamanaka/), [Yamashiro](/en/soak/yamashiro/) and [Wakura](/en/soak/wakura/).
