Tamatsukuri Onsen lies in Matsue city, Shimane, 8 minutes by train from Matsue station by the city’s English tourism site. Its inns line both banks of a small river, the Tamayu, and it rests its reputation on two things: a mention in the *Izumo fudoki*, a provincial record compiled in 733, and the label *bihada no yu*, "the bath for beautiful skin". This page explains what each of those rests on, what is known about the water, how the riverside town works and how to get there. It uses the Tamatsukuri Onsen official site (tamayado.com, which does not say on the pages I read who runs it), the Matsue tourism association, Matsue’s English tourism site, Shimane’s tourism federation and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, I give each. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For how to read a spring’s name and the analysis sign, see the guide to [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/); for what the posted notes on added water and recirculation mean, see the piece on [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Where it came from

### The fudoki

The official site says the *Izumo fudoki*, compiled in 733, records hot water rising by the river, with men and women, young and old, crowding to it. It says the exact date the spring opened is unknown and that Tamatsukuri is counted as "one of Japan’s oldest springs". It also says the same record describes bathing as making people look young again, and that the spring is said to appear in *The Pillow Book* (*Makura no sōshi*).

The Matsue tourism association says the water has been rising for 1,300 years and quotes the *fudoki* to the effect that bathing once makes one’s appearance very beautiful. Matsue’s English tourism site says the spring first appears in the chronicles of Izumo province in 733, that gods are said to have bathed in it, and calls it "one of the oldest hot springs in Japan" and "baths of the gods". Shimane’s tourism federation says it opened in the Nara period and is one of the oldest in Japan.

### What "oldest" rests on

None of these pages names a body that chose Tamatsukuri as Japan’s oldest, or one of the oldest, spring. The phrase rests on the *fudoki* text. I treat it as the town’s own description and a customary way of speaking, not an official ranking. None of the pages gives a competing account either.

Tamatsukuri is not on the environment ministry’s list of *kokumin hoyō onsenchi*, the designated health resort onsen areas; the Shimane entries are Sanbe and Sagi no Yu.

## The water

### The spring type, three ways

- **The official site** gives a sodium-calcium sulphate-chloride spring (*natorium-karushiumu ryūsan’en-enkabutsu sen*), weakly alkaline.
- **Shimane’s tourism federation** gives "sodium, calcium, sulphate, chloride spring" in its basic data, but calls it a sulphate spring in its description on the same page. It gives the temperature as 72°C, without saying which source or when.
- **The Matsue tourism association** says only that the water is weakly alkaline and colourless.

These are not in conflict on the main point; the federation’s page is simply inconsistent with itself. The 72°C is the only official temperature I found.

I could not find the number of sources or the flow on the official site or the Matsue association’s pages.

### "Bihada no yu"

The official site explains the "beautiful skin" label through the water’s components: sulphate, chloride, weak alkalinity and metasilicic acid, with figures for how much metasilicic acid it says makes a bath a "bihada no yu". The Matsue tourism association bases it on the *fudoki* text and the water’s high metasilicic acid content. Those are effect claims, and this page does not repeat them.

What I can say is this. The environment ministry’s ten official spring types have no "beautiful skin" category and no category for metasilicic acid, and I found no public body that defines or awards the title. It is the town’s own description. The official site also carries a heading calling Tamatsukuri Japan’s most moisturising onsen, with a note that the claim comes from a survey by a private cosmetics company; the survey is not described, and I do not use it.

## How the town works

### The river and the magatama island

The Tamayu river runs through the town, and Matsue’s English site says the inns stand along both banks. The Matsue tourism association says there is a small island shaped like a *magatama*, the comma-shaped bead, in the river, with a rough blue agate stone at its centre that people say brings happiness if you touch it. The association also points to shops built around the "beautiful skin" theme and to spots for *en-musubi*, prayers for good relationships.

### Footbaths

Shimane’s tourism federation says there are three free footbaths. Matsue’s English site puts two of them along the river and the third at Himegami Square, and the Matsue association says the one at Himegami Square has a roof. The official site mentions two footbaths along the Tamayu, which fits the two riverside ones.

### Day bathing

The official site’s day-bathing page lists five places where you can bathe without staying. Four of them give prices marked as including tax, but individual facilities are outside what this page covers, so I do not list them. None of the five entries mentions tattoos.

I found no public bath of the *sōyu* kind, run by the town for everyone, on the official site or the Matsue association’s pages.

### Cars

The official site’s access page has a note about vehicles, reading roughly "ordinary cars from 15:00, route and sightseeing buses all day". The note is incomplete as it stands and I could not tell what it restricts. If you are driving in, check with the town first.

## Bathing there

- **English and tattoos.** The official site has no link to English pages. Matsue’s English tourism site and Shimane’s tourism federation say nothing about tattoos or bathing manners. I found no official guidance. Ask each bath or inn. The general steps for a Japanese bath are in the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).
- **Compared with city baths.** I found no official Tamatsukuri source that compares its water with baths in the cities. Any bath using hot spring water has to post whether it is diluted, heated, recirculated or disinfected, which is the fair way to compare.

## What it is eaten with

The farm ministry lists 28 local dishes for Shimane. Two of them belong to this part of the prefecture:

- **Izumo soba.** The ministry hands it down in the Izumo region and says it is counted among Japan’s three great soba, without saying who counts it. It says the dish is thought to have spread after Matsudaira Naomasa, the first lord of the Matsue domain, brought soba makers with him from Shinshū in the early Edo period. It names two styles, *warigo soba*, which began in Matsue, and *kamaage soba*, said to come from around Izumo Taisha. More in the [Izumo soba guide](/en/eat/soba-izumo/).
- **Shijimi-jiru.** Clam soup made with the brackish-water *Yamato shijimi* of Lake Shinji, an everyday dish in the eastern, Izumo, part of the prefecture. The ministry says Lake Shinji is Japan’s seventh-largest brackish lake, that its *shijimi* catch is among the largest in the country, that the clam is one of the lake’s "seven delicacies", and that around 300 people fish for it.

Neither ministry page mentions Tamatsukuri, and the official site and the Matsue association’s Tamatsukuri pages do not mention either dish. So I do not call them Tamatsukuri dishes; they are dishes of the area around it.

## Getting there

Checked 11 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

- **From Matsue.** Matsue’s English tourism site gives 8 minutes by train from JR Matsue to Tamatsukuri-Onsen station, or 30 minutes by bus.
- **From Tamatsukuri-Onsen station to the town.** Shimane’s tourism federation gives about 10 minutes by Ichibata bus or about 5 minutes by taxi. Matsue’s English site gives about 20 minutes on foot.
- **By air.** About 30 minutes by taxi from Izumo Enmusubi airport, says the federation. The official site also lists Yonago Kitarō airport, without a time.
- **By car.** About 7 minutes from Matsue-Tamatsukuri interchange, says the federation.
- **Highway buses.** The official site names Ichibata, Chūgoku JR Bus and Hiroshima Electric Railway, without times.

I could not find official times for limited expresses from further afield, such as from Izumo-shi, Kurayoshi or Tottori. For Misasa, the radon spring further east in Tottori, see the [Misasa guide](/en/soak/misasa/).

## What people get wrong

- **"Tamatsukuri is officially Japan’s oldest onsen."** No page names a body that ranked it. The claim rests on the *fudoki* of 733, and the official site itself says the opening date is unknown.
- **"Bihada no yu is a certified category."** The environment ministry’s spring types have no such class, and I found no public body that awards it.
- **"The spring type is just sulphate."** Shimane’s tourism federation says so in one sentence, but its own basic data and the official site give a sodium-calcium sulphate-chloride spring.
- **"There is a town public bath like Kinosaki’s."** I found no *sōyu*-style public bath on the official pages; day bathing is at the five places on the official list.
- **"Izumo soba and shijimi are Tamatsukuri’s dishes."** The farm ministry places them in the Izumo region and Lake Shinji without naming the town.
- **"The water will make your skin younger."** This page does not say so. The official indications for each spring type are in the water types guide.
