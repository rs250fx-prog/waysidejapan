Yufuin sits in a basin at the foot of Mount Yufu, about 30 km by road from Beppu according to JNTO. It is the best known of five hot spring towns in Yufu city, and its story is less about the water than about what the town decided to do with it. This page explains how the area works using the environment ministry’s 2026 plan for the Yufuin onsen district, the Yufu city tourism bureau and JNTO. Where they give different figures, I give both. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For how Yufuin compares with the rest of Kyushu, start from the Kyushu hot springs overview. For what the spring names and pH mean, see the guide to onsen water types; for the posted notices about added water, heating and disinfection, see the piece on *kakenagashi*.

## Where it came from

### Five towns, one district

The ministry’s plan, dated April 2026, treats five hot spring towns in Yufu city as the "Yufuin onsen district": Yufuin, Yunohira, Tsukahara, Shōnai and Hasama. Yufu city itself was formed in October 2005 by the merger of the towns of Hasama, Shōnai and Yufuin.

### Early records

The tourism bureau says the name appears in the *Bungo Fudoki* of the early Nara period, and that Yufuin became known across Japan as a hot spring town after residents began planning the town themselves from the late 1950s. I have not seen the *Fudoki* text. I found no official founding year or founder for Yufuin’s springs, so I do not give one.

For Yunohira, the plan says its founding is "said" to go back about 800 years to the Kamakura period, that its present shape as a bath town dates from the late Edo period, and that it is recorded in the Edo-period *Bungo Kokushi*. The stone-paved slope along the river was laid about 300 years ago. A fire in 1912 burned almost every inn and house, and the town was rebuilt.

### The town that said no to resort development

The plan tells the modern story like this. When Beppu prospered first, Yufuin was a farming area with hot spring inns, called *Oku-Beppu*, "the back of Beppu". From the late 1950s the town began planning itself, from the ground up. It was designated a national health resort onsen (*kokumin hoyō onsenchi*) in 1959, which the plan calls the second such effort in Kyushu. Under a slogan of "a hundred years of town-making", it took a line opposed to resort development. After the 1975 central Oita earthquake damaged the area, events followed: horse-drawn carriages, the Yufuin film festival and a beef-eating "shouting contest".

Beppu city adds a detail from the other side: in the Taishō era, Beppu’s group of baths was called the "ten baths of Beppu", with Yufuin and Tsukahara included.

In 2004 a Japanese academic society for hot spring regions chose four items in Yufu city as "hot spring heritage", the plan says: Lake Kinrin and the Shitan-yu communal bath in Yufuin, the stone-paved streets of Yunohira, Yunohira’s communal bath culture, and the source area at Tsukahara.

## The water

### How many springs

Two figures, from two kinds of source.

- **The ministry’s plan** gives 1,055 springs for Yufu city as a whole, as of the end of March 2023, from the Oita prefectural report on hot spring use. It lists 378 as the main ones.
- **The tourism bureau** says Yufuin Onsen has about 850 springs, with no year or method.

One may be the whole city and the other one town, but I could not confirm that, so I do not treat them as the same count. The plan also says Yufuin was second in Japan for number of springs (in 2009) and for flow (in 2014), citing two issues of the Japan Spa Association’s journal. Those are old figures from an association, not the ministry’s current statistics.

### What kind

- **Yufuin** — the plan says mostly alkaline or weakly alkaline simple springs, with chloride and hydrogen carbonate springs in some parts. The tourism bureau describes three broad groups: alkaline simple springs, sodium chloride springs, and sodium chloride-sulphate-hydrogen carbonate springs.
- **Yunohira** — mainly chloride springs.
- **Tsukahara** — strongly acidic. JNTO gives a pH of 1.4. The plan, quoting a local pamphlet, says it sits by a spring on the side of the active volcano Garandake, with steam vents above 120°C and a mud volcano, and that its acidity and its iron and aluminium content are rare in Japan.
- **Shōnai** — the plan says wells of about 500 m tend to give simple springs, and 600 to 800 m tend to give sulphate springs.

### Why here

The plan calls Yufuin’s water a typical volcanic spring: rain falling on the mountains around the basin soaks in and is heated by geothermal heat from the Kujū volcanoes and the Yufu and Tsurumi volcanoes, then rises. It says the flow is plentiful and many facilities use *gensen kakenagashi*, water flowing straight from the source. The tourism bureau also calls the water volcanic.

The Japan Meteorological Agency gives Yufudake as 1,583 m, last erupting about 2,000 years ago, and not under constant watch. Tsurumidake and Garandake are watched around the clock.

## How the town works

### A district, not a single street

The ministry, JNTO and the tourism bureau describe the five towns differently from one another: Yufuin in the basin at the foot of Mount Yufu, Yunohira with a stone-paved street from the 17th century, Tsukahara on a strongly acidic plateau, with Shōnai and Hasama besides. They are spread out rather than joined into one bath town.

### Yufuin itself

The plan says the main tourist area runs from JR Yufuin station to Lake Kinrin at the foot of the mountain. A tourist information centre opened in front of the station in April 2018, and shops and restaurants line the Yu-no-tsubo street. JNTO suggests walking or cycling around Lake Kinrin, mentions a footbath at the station, and says the information office next to the station takes bookings for rental bicycles and the horse-drawn carriage.

### Yunohira’s shared bath

The plan says Yunohira opened its communal baths free of charge in the Shōwa era, which it calls rare in Japan. Today, according to the local tourism association as quoted in the plan, one communal bath is open, with 18 inns and 9 restaurants and shops. Many of the inns’ baths are for staying guests only.

### Crowds

The plan quotes Yufu city’s visitor survey for 2024: 4,297,903 visitors, 1,444,387 of them from abroad, with 2,902,445 day-trippers and 1,395,458 staying overnight. It says concentration in one area has brought traffic jams and problems with toilets and rubbish that also affect residents.

## Bathing there

I found no official prices for baths in the area whose tax status I could confirm, so none are given.

Nor did I find official guidance on bathing manners or tattoos for Yufuin. JNTO’s Yufuin page does not mention them, and I could not find them on the tourism bureau’s site. The general manners and the national picture on tattoos are in the water types guide. Ask each inn or bath in advance, and consider a private bath where there is one.

I could not find a pairing of Yufuin, Yunohira, Tsukahara or Shōnai with a local dish that both the farm ministry’s list of Oita dishes and the tourism bureau name, so I do not give one.

## Getting there

As checked on 10 October 2026. Timetables change, so check the operator before you travel.

- **By train.** JNTO gives about 1 hour from Oita station on the JR Kyūdai line, and about 1 hour 15 minutes from Beppu, changing at Oita to the Kyūdai line.
- **By road from Beppu.** JNTO gives about 40 minutes by car or 50 minutes by bus; the distance is about 30 km.
- **Open-top bus.** The tourism bureau says an open-top double-decker bus runs between Beppu and Yufuin from 7 October to 13 December 2026, about 65 minutes one way, booking required.

I could not find official journey times to Yufuin from Hakata, Fukuoka Airport or Oita Airport on JNTO’s page or the tourism bureau’s access pages. Fares are not on this page.

## What people get wrong

- **"Yufuin has about 850 springs" and "Yufuin has 1,055 springs" are the same claim.** The 1,055 is Yufu city as a whole; the 850 has no year or method. I could not reconcile them.
- **"Yufuin is a suburb of Beppu."** It is in a different city. It was once called the back of Beppu, and once counted among Beppu’s ten baths, but its modern identity came from deciding not to follow Beppu’s path.
- **"All the water is the same."** Yufuin is mostly simple alkaline, Yunohira mostly chloride, Tsukahara strongly acidic, and at Shōnai the type depends on how deep the well goes.
- **"Kakenagashi means a better bath."** The plan says many facilities use it; it is a description of how the water is handled, not a grade. The *kakenagashi* guide explains the notices.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so. The plan quotes a pamphlet’s claim about Tsukahara and the skin, which I have not repeated. The official list of indications by spring type is in the water types guide, along with the environment ministry’s note that spa therapy is best done under a doctor’s guidance.
