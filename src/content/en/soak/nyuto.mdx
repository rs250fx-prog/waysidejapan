Nyūtō, in Akita, is not a single town. It is seven inns spread around the foot of a mountain, each drawing its own water, joined by a bus that runs between them. Most of what a visitor needs to know — who can buy the bath-hopping book, what to pay in, what closes in winter — follows from that. This page uses the inns’ own cooperative, Semboku city, the Tohoku tourism promotion body and JNTO. Where they disagree, I give each with its source. It does not rate any inn.

For what the spring types and the long chemical names on the sign mean, see the separate guide to onsen water types; for the posted notices about added water, heating and disinfection, see the piece on *kakenagashi*. The Honshu hot springs guide covers the region as a whole.

## Where it came from

The Nyūtō Onsen cooperative calls the area "a hidden spring of the Akita domain, continuing since the Edo period", and says it was once loved as a *tōji* spot, a place to stay for days or weeks for the sake of the water. Its history page is mostly photographs with captions. Among them:

- At Tsuru no Yu, the thatched row house called the *honjin* is "said to be" where guarding samurai waited.
- At Kuroyu, people stayed for long *tōji* in the farming off-season.
- Magoroku was busy with *tōji* guests and mountain climbers.
- The thatched hut at Ganiba dates from the Meiji era.
- In Shōwa 40 (1965) the Tazawako Kōgen National Vacation Village, now Kyūkamura Nyūtō Onsenkyō, opened, and a bus route opened with it.
- The cooperative itself was formed in the Shōwa 50s (1975–84), and the first version of the bath-hopping book appeared in the same decade.

Semboku city says Tsuru no Yu has the oldest history in Nyūtō, is said to have been where the lord of the Akita domain came for *tōji*, and that its *honjin* still stands. It gives no founding year, origin story or lord’s name. JNTO says Tsuru no Yu has more than 300 years of history and was favoured by travelling *daimyō* in the Edo period. I found no founding year on any official page I read, nor whether there are competing accounts.

### A designation I could not confirm

The cooperative’s site says Nyūtō is a national health resort onsen (*kokumin hoyō onsenchi*) designated by the environment ministry. The ministry’s list of designated areas has an entry for the Tazawako Kōgen onsen district in Akita, but not under the name Nyūtō. I could not find out whether that is a difference of name, an overlap of area, or something else, so this page does not call Nyūtō a designated area.

## The water

The cooperative says the seven inns each have their own source, and that there are more than ten kinds of spring in all. I found no official total for the number of sources or for the flow.

Its "spring types and effects" page lists the springs at each of the seven. Since this page does not review individual inns, here they are without inn names, as the page gives them (one entry lists three more alongside the first):

- Sulphur-bearing sodium-calcium-chloride-hydrogen-carbonate spring
- Calcium-magnesium sulphate spring; simple spring
- Simple hydrogen-sulphide spring; acidic sulphur spring
- Simple sulphur spring
- Simple spring
- Acidic, arsenic-bearing sodium chloride sulphate spring
- Simple sulphur spring; sodium hydrogen-carbonate spring

The list mixes current spring names with older ones such as "simple hydrogen-sulphide spring". The water types guide explains how the old and new names relate. The page also lists effects for each inn; I have not copied them.

Semboku city’s Nyūtō page and the Tohoku tourism body’s page give no spring types.

**Why the water is here.** I could not find a public source that explains the heat or origin of Nyūtō’s water.

## How the area works

### Seven inns, not one town

The cooperative has seven members: Tsuru no Yu, Taenoyu, Ganiba, Ōgama, Magoroku, Kuroyu and Kyūkamura. Each is an inn with its own source. A visitor moves between them rather than walking a street of baths.

### The bath-hopping book and the bus

There are two products, and they are easy to confuse.

- **Yumeguri-chō**, the bath-hopping book. Semboku city’s English leaflet says it covers rides on the Yumeguri-gō bus and one bath at each of the seven inns. It is for overnight guests only, sold at each inn’s front desk, valid for one year from purchase, and covers day bathing only, not a stay. Extra baths are paid in cash. The cooperative also says guests only, cash only.
- **Yumeguri map**, a one-day pass for unlimited rides on the Yumeguri-gō. The city’s leaflet says any guest or visitor can buy it; the cooperative mentions it in the context of sales to guests, so the two read slightly differently. It does not include baths, which are paid in cash at each inn.

The prices do not agree. The city’s leaflet and the Tohoku tourism body give one pair of figures; the cooperative gives a higher pair. None says whether tax is included, and I could not tell which is current, so I have not given any.

The leaflet says the bus leaves on time and does not wait for latecomers, and that times can change with the weather and road conditions. There is a winter timetable; the one in the city’s leaflet is a revision dated 14 November 2020, and the cooperative’s home page links to timetables for the regular and winter seasons. I could not confirm the current operating period.

### Fees

I have not given the inns’ bath fees. They belong to individual inns, and I could not confirm whether they include tax.

## Bathing there

- **Cash.** The bath-hopping book, the bus pass and extra baths are paid in cash, according to the cooperative and the city.
- **Winter.** Semboku city says some facilities close or change their hours in winter, and marks Kuroyu as closed for winter. It warns that phones may not be answered in winter and suggests asking the Tazawako tourist information centre "Folake", which is open all year. The cooperative’s spring page also notes, for one inn’s day bathing, a winter closure from mid-November to early April; I could not tell from the text which inn it refers to.
- **Feeling unwell from bathing.** The cooperative asks bathers to take care against *yu-atari*, feeling ill from too much bathing, and says its member inns employ staff qualified as onsen bathing instructors.
- **Tattoos and English.** I could not find official guidance on tattoos or on bathing in English from Semboku city, the Tohoku tourism body or JNTO. The cooperative links to an English site, "RYOKAN STORIES", which I did not read. Ask each inn in advance about tattoos, and consider a private bath where there is one.

## What it is eaten with

I could not find a dish that both the inns’ side and the farm ministry tie to Nyūtō. The ministry’s list of Akita’s local dishes includes *kiritanpo* hotpot but nothing naming Tazawako, Semboku, Nyūtō or onsen. The cooperative mentions mountain vegetables and mushrooms as gifts of the land, without naming a dish.

## Getting there

As checked on 10 October 2026. Fares are not on this page. The figures do not quite agree, so I give each.

- **Rail, by the cooperative.** Tokyo to Tazawako station 2 hours 55 minutes by shinkansen, then about 50 minutes by the local bus bound for "Nyūtō Onsen", or about 30 minutes by taxi. The page has no update date.
- **Bus, by others.** The Tohoku tourism body gives about 40 minutes from Tazawako station by bus. JNTO gives about 45 minutes, and another JNTO article 30.
- **Car.** The cooperative gives about an hour from Morioka interchange on the Tohoku expressway; the Tohoku tourism body gives about 70 minutes via Routes 46 and 341.

So plan on roughly 40 to 50 minutes by bus from Tazawako, and check the current timetable with the cooperative or the city, especially in winter.

## What people get wrong

- **"Nyūtō is an onsen town with a main street."** It is seven separate inns, each with its own source, linked by a bus.
- **"Anyone can buy the bath-hopping book."** The city and the cooperative say it is for overnight guests. Day visitors can buy the bus pass and pay at each bath.
- **"Cards are fine."** The bath-hopping book, bus pass and extra baths are cash.
- **"It is a national health resort onsen."** The cooperative says so, but I could not match that to the ministry’s list.
- **"Nyūtō opened in a known year."** I found no founding year on the official pages I read.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so. The official list of indications for each spring type is in the water types guide, along with the environment ministry’s note that spa therapy is best done under a doctor’s guidance.
