Isawa Onsen is a spa town in the city of Fuefuki, in the Kōfu basin of Yamanashi. It is young by Japanese standards: the spa as it is known today dates from 1961, when hot water poured into the rivers and fields around a vineyard district. Much of its water is pumped from wells owned by the prefecture and piped to the inns. This page uses Yamanashi prefecture's enterprise bureau and a prefectural research institute, Fuefuki city and its tourism portal (Fuefuki Kankō Navi, which calls itself the city's official information portal), the Isawa Onsen ryokan cooperative, the prefectural tourism organisation's site and JNTO. Where they disagree, both are given. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring types named below, see [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### The prefecture's wells

Isawa is unusual in that the prefecture itself supplies hot water. Yamanashi's enterprise bureau says the prefecture drilled five sources to protect the resource and support tourism, and began supplying water in **1963**. In 1972 it switched from letting the water run constantly to a metered system with circulation. Its supply network has 1,489 m of main pipe, 10,540.7 m of distribution pipe, **27 distribution taps** and heating equipment, and it delivered 671,995 cubic metres in the 2024 financial year.

The bureau's table, as of 1 April 2025, lists five working sources:

| Source | Depth | Flow (litres a minute) | Temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| No. 1 | 185 m | 316 | 26.0°C |
| No. 2 | 168 m | 82 | 53.3°C |
| No. 3 | 175 m | 774 | 36.8°C |
| No. 5 | 190 m | 388 | 60.6°C |
| No. 6 | 800 m | 200 | 71.7°C |

Together that is 1,760 litres a minute (this page's sum). No. 4 was closed in September 2018 because its temperature had fallen. The bureau says No. 6 was drilled in 2002 at 67°C and 340 litres a minute; the 2025 table gives different figures, and the bureau does not explain the change.

### What the analysis says

The prefecture's water, taken from its **collection tank**, is an **alkaline simple spring** (hypotonic, alkaline, high temperature). Its analysis, sampled on **15 May 2025**, gives:

- **Temperature**: 46.9°C
- **pH**: 9.1 (9.15 in the laboratory)
- **Evaporation residue**: 352 mg/kg
- **Dissolved substances**: 406.2 mg
- **Radon**: not measured

The tank collects water from all five prefectural sources, so these are figures for that mixed supply, not for every bath in Isawa.

### The whole spa

Official sources do not give the total number of sources or the total flow for Isawa as a whole, since privately owned sources are not counted in the prefecture's table. The prefectural tourism site's English page gives **2,000 litres a minute** and says the baths along Isawa Onsen street draw on the same source; it may count differently or from a different year than the bureau's 1,760. Fuefuki city's portal says Isawa's sources run above 60°C.

### Where the heat comes from

The prefecture's public health and environment research institute studied the Isawa and Kasugai area in its 2018 annual report. It found:

- Most of the 44 sources it grouped are rich in sodium, potassium and chloride; hot chloride-rich water and cooler hydrogen-carbonate-rich water are **mixed underground**.
- Water rising from **below the andesite layer** is marked at certain points, which the institute takes to be the area's heat source, citing a 1982 study.
- The resource fell sharply after development and more slowly until 1992; the prefecture made the area a **protected zone** in the 1965 financial year, limiting new wells and pumping. The institute estimates the heat resource has been stable since 1990.

The report does not say what lies beneath the andesite, such as magma, or how the basin was formed, so this page does not either.

### Not a health resort

Isawa is not on the environment ministry's list of national health resort hot springs, as of October 2022. [Masutomi](/en/soak/masutomi/), further north in Yamanashi, is.

## Where it came from

### Three accounts of 1961

The sources tell the start differently:

- **Fuefuki city's portal**: in January 1961 hot water welled up from a vineyard in Isawa and ran into a nearby river; the resulting *aozora onsen*, an open-air "blue-sky spa", became famous. The ryokan cooperative says the same: so much hot water came from a vineyard in 1961 that it flowed into rivers and fields, and the makeshift baths made in the fields were reported on television and in newspapers across Japan.
- **The prefecture's enterprise bureau**: in 1961, while a source was being **drilled** at an inn called Izumisō, hot water came out in quantity, ran into the rivers and fields nearby and was taken up by the press as a natural bathing place.
- **The prefectural research institute**: the spa began with a source **drilled in 1956**, and in 1961 water came out at 1,200 litres a minute.

The prefectural tourism site's English pages say both "discovered by chance in 1961 when a well was being dug" and that it "flowed forth from a vineyard suddenly". The other sources do not say "by chance". This page does not choose between the vineyard and the drilling; what they share is 1961, hot water in the rivers and fields, and the name *aozora onsen*.

### Growth

The ryokan cooperative says the Chūō line limited expresses and the Chūō expressway brought Isawa within easy reach of Tokyo, that large hotels and a nightlife district grew quickly, and that it came to be called the *Keihin no okuzashiki*, Tokyo and Yokohama's secluded retreat. That is the cooperative's phrase.

## How the town works

### Size

Fuefuki city's portal counts the cooperative's members at a little over 30 inns, with room for about 7,000 guests, and calls Isawa the largest spa town in the prefecture. The cooperative says more than 30. Neither gives a date for the count.

### The station footbath

Fuefuki city's portal lists the **Isawa-Onsen Ekimae Kōen Ashiyu**, the footbath in the park by the station:

- **Free.**
- **Hours**: 10:00 to 18:00 from April to September, 10:00 to 17:00 from October to March.
- **Closed**: no fixed closing day.
- **Towels**: on sale at the information office by the station.

### Day bathing at the inns

The ryokan cooperative says many of its inns take day bathers, and its day-bathing page lists them with a filter for multilingual service. Fuefuki city's portal counts 14 hotels and inns where you can spend a day bathing. Prices are not given here, because neither says whether they include tax.

### Sakura Onsen-dōri

The prefectural tourism site's English page describes a waterway of about 900 m fed from the Fuefuki river, with dozens of baths and hotels along both banks, lit from above the water in cherry-blossom season. This page has not checked how it looks today.

### Fruit country

Fuefuki city calls itself *Nihon-ichi tōgenkyō*, "Japan's number-one peach-blossom paradise", and says its peaches and grapes lead Japan in production. These are the city's own claims; it does not give the statistics or the year. The city's portal lists 12 wineries in Fuefuki.

The portal's list of fruit farms belonging to the city's tourism federation (as of February to March 2026) gives:

- **Seasons**: peach picking from June and grape picking from July; grape seasons run, farm by farm, from early July to late November.
- **A caution**: the periods are a guide, change with the weather, and you should ask each farm directly.
- **Where**: one farm is in Isawa itself (early August to early November); most are in Ichinomiya, Misaka, Yatsushiro and Kasugai.

The list gives no prices. The ryokan cooperative's fruit page points visitors to the same federation.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The prefectural tourism site has English pages on Isawa, but they give no tattoo rule. Fuefuki city's portal and the cooperative's site do not have English pages on the spa; the cooperative's day-bathing list lets you filter for multilingual service. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule or a guide to bathing for Isawa.

JNTO says public baths decide case by case. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each inn in advance.

### Compared with city baths

No Isawa source compares its water with baths elsewhere. What applies everywhere is the national posting rule: since 24 May 2005, any bath that adds water, heats, recirculates, uses bath additives or disinfects must say so, and why, on its notice. At Isawa, the prefecture's own supply is circulated and has heating equipment, so read the notice at the bath you use. See [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

The labels themselves are policed. An environment ministry committee paper from November 2004 records that on 24 August 2004 one bathing business in Isawa received a written caution under the law on misleading representations, for advertising heated groundwater, not onsen water, as if it were a hot spring. It concerns one business, not the spa.

## Food

**Hōtō.** The farm ministry describes hōtō as a dish of the whole prefecture, an everyday food all year that is eaten more often in winter. Fuefuki city's portal publishes a food map for the area around Isawa Onsen. So you can eat hōtō around Isawa, but no official source calls it Isawa's own dish. More in [udon and hōtō](/en/eat/udon-hoto/).

**Rahō.** Fuefuki city has developed *rahō*, a cross of ramen and hōtō, as a new local speciality and registered it as a trademark. It is the city's creation, not one of the farm ministry's local dishes.

**Not tied to the spa.** The farm ministry lists *usuyaki* for the whole prefecture and notes a "usuyaki project" event in Fuefuki, without mentioning the spa. It places *torimotsu-ni*, simmered chicken giblets, in Kōfu city. Neither peaches nor grapes appear as dishes among its 30 for Yamanashi.

Prices are not given. More in the [Chūbu local food guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-chubu/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares and current timetables are not on this page.

- **From Shinjuku by train.** About **1 hour 30 minutes** by JR Chūō line limited express to **Isawa-Onsen** station (Fuefuki city, the ryokan cooperative). The prefectural tourism site gives 87 minutes from Shinjuku to Kōfu by the Azusa or Kaiji expresses; JNTO says about 90 minutes to Kōfu. Fuefuki city's portal sums it up as an hour and a half from central Tokyo.
- **By highway bus.** About 1 hour 50 minutes from Shinjuku (Fuefuki city, the cooperative). JNTO gives a little over two hours from Shinjuku to Kōfu.
- **By car.** About 1 hour 20 minutes from Shinjuku on the Chūō expressway to the **Ichinomiya-Misaka** interchange, then about 5 minutes on route 20 (the cooperative).
- **From Nagoya.** Limited express to Shiojiri (about 2 hours), then limited express to Isawa-Onsen (about 1 hour), per Fuefuki city.
- **From Osaka.** Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka to Shizuoka (about 2 hours), the Minobu line limited express to Kōfu (about 2 hours 10 minutes), then the Chūō line to Isawa-Onsen (about 5 minutes), per Fuefuki city.

The times above are the sources' round figures, not a timetable.

For another Yamanashi spa, see [Masutomi](/en/soak/masutomi/); for spas over the border in Nagano, see [Shirahone](/en/soak/shirahone/) and [Togura-Kamiyamada](/en/soak/togura-kamiyamada/).

## What people get wrong

- **"It was found by chance in a vineyard."** "By chance" is only in the prefectural tourism site's English. The city's portal and the cooperative say a vineyard; the enterprise bureau says a drilled well; the research institute traces it to a 1956 well.
- **"pH 9.1 is the water of Isawa."** It is the prefecture's mixed collection tank, sampled in May 2025, not every bath.
- **"Hōtō is Isawa's local dish."** The farm ministry says it belongs to the whole prefecture. *Rahō* is a recent city creation.
- **"Every bath runs free-flowing from the ground."** The prefecture's supply has been metered and circulated since 1972, with heating equipment. Check the notice.
- **"It is a national health resort."** It is not on the environment ministry's list.
