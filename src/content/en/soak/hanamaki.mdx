Hanamaki Onsen-kyō is the name for the group of hot springs scattered around the city of Hanamaki in Iwate. It is not one spa town but a spread of separate springs, from a planned resort with four hotels and a rose garden to a narrow street of small inns and riverside and mountain springs further west. This page uses Hanamaki city, the Hanamaki tourism association, Iwate prefecture’s tourism site, the Dai Onsen ryokan cooperative and the website of Hanamaki Onsen Co., the company that runs Hanamaki Onsen itself. A company’s figures describe its own hotels, not the whole area, and this page says so each time. Where sources disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the sulphur springs named below, see [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/); for the simple and sulphate springs, [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The springs

### Two lists of twelve

Hanamaki city (page updated 21 October 2025) and the tourism association both count twelve springs, but not the same twelve:

| Both lists | City only | Association only |
|---|---|---|
| Hanamaki, Dai, Kanaya, Matsukura, Shidotaira, Ōsawa, Yamanokami, Namari, Shin-Namari, Hanamaki-kita, Tōwa | Ōedo Onsen Monogatari Premium Iwate Hanamaki (the city gives its opening as 19 December 2025) | Watari Onsen |

The association does list the Ōedo Onsen Monogatari hotel, but under "other springs" in its accommodation pages. Official sources do not explain the difference, so this page gives both lists.

The city gives a line on each. Hanamaki Onsen has four hotels and inns, with the rose garden and monuments in its grounds. Dai Onsen is a row of small inns. Shidotaira stands facing the Toyosawa river. The city says Namari Onsen is known for an indoor bath you stand in, and Ōsawa for its open-air bath. It describes Hanamaki-kita as pale amber, slippery and alkaline, and calls Tōwa the first simple spring in the Kitakami mountains.

Official sources do not give a source count, total flow or pH for the area as a whole. The figures below belong to two places, Dai and Hanamaki Onsen, and come from bodies with different standing.

### Dai Onsen’s three waters

The Dai Onsen ryokan cooperative says the water rises from more than ten sources and differs slightly from inn to inn. Its table gives three representative types, with no year and no source names:

| Spring type | Temperature | pH |
|---|---|---|
| Sulphur-bearing sodium sulphate-sulphide spring | 96°C | 8.2 |
| Simple sulphur spring | 88.4°C | 8.4 |
| Sodium sulphate-chloride spring | 76°C | 8.0 |

Iwate prefecture says only that the type and claimed benefits vary by inn. Without a year, none of these figures can be called current; read the analysis at the inn where you bathe.

### Hanamaki Onsen’s analysis

The operator posts an analysis for the mixed source "Hanamaki Onsen (mixed spring)", which supplies Hotel Senshūkaku, Hotel Kōyōkan and Hotel Hanamaki. It was sampled on **6 August 2025** and completed on 29 August 2025 by the Iwate pharmaceutical association:

- **Spring type**: simple spring (hypotonic, weakly alkaline, high temperature).
- **Temperature**: 61.6°C. **pH**: 7.7.
- **Flow**: 177 litres a minute, read from the meter on the mixing tank.
- **Radon**: 1.92×10⁻¹⁰ Ci/kg (0.53 Mache units/kg). **Evaporation residue**: 0.0955 g/kg.

The overview on the same page gives the flow as 251 litres a minute, also as a meter reading for the mixed spring. The page does not explain why the two differ. The operator says its baths combine free-flowing water with filtered recirculation and use a chlorine-based disinfectant.

### Where the heat comes from

Official sources do not link Hanamaki’s springs to Iwate-san or any other volcano. The Japan Meteorological Agency lists Iwate-san (2,038 m) as a continuously monitored volcano, and the national geological survey institute (AIST) places Matsukura-yama (968 m) on a map sheet called Namari, the same name as Namari Onsen. Neither page mentions hot springs, so this page does not connect them. The tourism association’s English site says only that Hanamaki lies on the Kitakami plain, between the Ōu mountains to the west and the Kitakami highlands to the east.

### Not a health resort

None of the Hanamaki springs has a plan as a national health resort hot spring on the environment ministry’s list. In Iwate, only Kindaichi Onsen and Hachimantai Onsen-kyō do.

## Where it came from

### Dai Onsen’s two stories

The Dai Onsen cooperative gives two accounts and labels them itself:

- **The legend.** About 1,200 years ago, in the Daidō era (around 806), the spring was found in connection with the general Sakanoue no Tamuramaro.
- **The historical account.** About 600 years ago, in Genchū 4 (1387), a hunter chasing a pheasant deep into the hills saw smoke, and a man named Shōgen found the spring. The cooperative names Ōyu, also called Kiji-no-yu, the "pheasant bath", as the birthplace of the spa.

It gives two explanations for the name Dai: from Tamuramaro’s words *tai iyu*, "the body heals", or from an Ainu word *tai*, meaning forest. It also says every bathhouse burned down in a fire in Tenpō 10 (1839). Iwate prefecture says Dai opened in the fourteenth century and that the Nanbu lords are said to have bathed there. This page gives every version and chooses none.

### Hanamaki Onsen, 1923

Iwate prefecture says Hanamaki Onsen was developed in **1923** by piping water from Dai Onsen. Today it is a single site run by one company, with four inns and the rose garden. Iwate prefecture puts Dai about 1 km to the west, along the Dai river.

### The rose garden and Miyazawa Kenji

The operator says its rose garden was begun in 1958 and opened in 1960 on the site of a former south-facing flower bed. It covers about 5,000 *tsubo* (roughly 1.6 hectares) and holds about 450 varieties and more than 6,000 plants.

On **Miyazawa Kenji**, the operator says the garden includes a sundial flower bed that is famous as his design, and that it was first in the courtyard of the Kōyōkan before being moved into the garden. Iwate prefecture also describes a sundial flower bed designed by Miyazawa Kenji. That is what the operator and the prefecture say; this page has not seen plans or other primary records.

Official sources do not give the history of Shidotaira, Ōsawa, Namari or the other springs.

## How the area works

### A resort and a street

The two best-documented places work differently. Hanamaki Onsen is an enclosed resort: four inns, a rose garden and shared bathing facilities on one company’s grounds. Dai Onsen, by the city’s description, is a spa street of small inns. The others are spread along rivers and into the hills, and the tourism association lists a sightseeing taxi, the *Donguri to Yamaneko-gō*, that picks up from the area’s hotels and inns. It does not give the fare.

### Day bathing at Hanamaki Onsen

The operator’s day-bathing page states prices including tax. As checked on 13 October 2026:

| Bath | Weekdays (adult / child) | Weekends, holidays and set days (adult / child) |
|---|---|---|
| Large bath at one of the three hotels (Senshū-no-yu, Hanamaki-no-yu, Kōyō-no-yu) | ¥1,200 / ¥600 | ¥1,500 / ¥750 |
| Garden open-air bath *Hanamaki Manten-no-yu* (opened 26 November 2024) | ¥1,500 / ¥750 | ¥1,800 / ¥900 |

Children here are primary school age. The hotel ticket covers one hotel’s bath only.

The smaller **Hōrai-yu** carries a notice that it has been closed "for the time being" from Friday 10 April, because supplies of heavy fuel oil are uncertain owing to the situation in the Middle East. The notice gives no year, but 10 April falls on a Friday in 2026. On 13 October 2026 the page gave no reopening date.

A **footbath and hand bath** are free, from 6:00 to 18:00 (to 21:00 in summer), and closed in winter.

### Rose garden entry

The operator’s prices include tax and "may change with the season". For adults (junior high school age and over), as checked on 13 October 2026:

- **Late May to early July** (peak season): ¥1,000.
- **Early July to early October**: ¥500.
- **Early to mid October**: ¥800.
- **Other times of year**: free.

Children of primary school age and under enter free. An annual pass costs ¥3,000, limited to 150. A night rose garden runs from late May to mid October at ¥600, ¥400 or ¥500 depending on the period.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The tourism association has an English site with pages on hot springs, accommodation and travel tips, and the operator has a FAQ, but none of them says how to bathe or mentions tattoos. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Hanamaki. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each inn in advance.

### Compared with city baths

No official Hanamaki source compares its water with baths elsewhere. What the operator does say is that Hanamaki Onsen’s baths mix free-flowing and recirculated water and are chlorinated. For what that means at the bath, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

**Wanko soba, but not tied to the spa.** Iwate prefecture’s page on wanko soba in Hanamaki tells the tradition that some 400 years ago the Nanbu lord Toshinao, the 27th head of the family, stopped in Hanamaki on his way to Edo and was served soba. It says Hanamaki’s wanko soba is served without the chanting, that a national wanko soba contest is held every year on 11 February, and lists shops in the city. The tourism association’s food page also lists wanko soba shops. Neither mentions Hanamaki Onsen or Dai Onsen, and the operator’s and cooperative’s pages do not mention wanko soba. The farm ministry’s list of 30 Iwate dishes does not include it. So it is a Hanamaki city dish, not the spa’s. See [wanko soba](/en/eat/soba-wanko/) and the [Tōhoku local food guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-tohoku/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

- **From Tokyo.** Tōhoku shinkansen to **Shin-Hanamaki**: about 180 minutes by the operator’s page, about 190 by the tourism association’s English site. Iwate prefecture gives Tokyo to Morioka as 2 hours 10 minutes.
- **From Sendai.** About 60 minutes to Shin-Hanamaki (both sources).
- **Two stations.** Shin-Hanamaki, on the shinkansen, and Hanamaki, on the conventional line, are separate stations about 7 km apart (the tourism association).
- **To Hanamaki Onsen.** From Shin-Hanamaki, about 20 to 30 minutes by free shuttle bus or taxi. From JR Hanamaki, about 20 minutes by free shuttle, paid route bus or taxi (the operator; Iwate prefecture also mentions the free shuttle). Official sources do not give who may ride the shuttle.
- **To Dai Onsen.** About 25 minutes by route bus from Hanamaki station, then 2 minutes on foot from the Dai Onsen stop (Iwate prefecture).
- **By air and car.** About 15 minutes by car from Iwate Hanamaki airport, and about 5 minutes from the Hanamaki interchange to Hanamaki Onsen, or 10 to Dai Onsen.

The times above are the sources’ round figures, not a timetable. For other Tōhoku spa towns, see [Naruko](/en/soak/naruko/), [Akiu](/en/soak/akiu/), [Iizaka](/en/soak/iizaka/), [Higashiyama](/en/soak/higashiyama/) and, further north, [Asamushi](/en/soak/asamushi/) and [Sukayu](/en/soak/sukayu/).

## What people get wrong

- **"Hanamaki Onsen-kyō is one spa town."** It is a group of separate springs, and the city and the tourism association do not list quite the same twelve.
- **"Hanamaki Onsen’s figures are the area’s figures."** The analysis is the operating company’s, for one mixed source feeding three hotels.
- **"Dai Onsen dates from 806."** That is the legend; the cooperative gives 1387 as its historical account, and the prefecture says the fourteenth century.
- **"The springs are heated by Iwate-san."** No official source says so.
- **"Wanko soba is the spa’s dish."** Official pages tie it to Hanamaki city, not to its springs.
- **"The water cures things."** This page does not say so.
