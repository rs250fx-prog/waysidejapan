Ogoto Onsen is a small spring resort on the western shore of **Lake Biwa**, at the foot of **Mt Hiei**, in the city of Ōtsu, Shiga. The resort writes its name in hiragana, おごと温泉, and the station took that name in 2008; the district itself is written 雄琴. Its water comes from deep wells numbered as the city's, and an Ōtsu city ordinance supplies it to the inns. This page uses Ōtsu city, the Ogoto Onsen tourism association and the inns' cooperative (which share a website), the 2019 spring analysis, Shiga's official tourism site, the farm ministry and JR West. Where they disagree, both are given. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring types named below, see [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/) and [acidic, sulphur and radioactive springs](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/). For reading the analysis sign, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### Where it is

The inns' cooperative, in its report revised on 25 June 2025, describes Ogoto as a spring resort of **nine ryokan** on the western shore of Lake Biwa at the foot of Mt Hiei. Shiga's official tourism site, run by the Biwako Visitors Bureau, says it is backed by Mt Hiei and next to **Sakamoto**, the temple town below the mountain. Ogoto is not on the environment ministry's list of national health resort hot springs; no area of Shiga prefecture is.

### The 2019 analysis

The cooperative's site posts a spring analysis. The applicant is the **mayor of Ōtsu**, and the source is named as a **mix of the city's sources No. 4, No. 5 and No. 6** at Ogoto 1-chōme. The Kyoto Microbiological Research Institute carried out the survey on **6 November 2019** and dated its report 26 November 2019. It gives:

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Temperature at source | 27.0°C (air 18.2°C) |
| Flow | 168 m³ a day (amount used, submersible pump) |
| pH | 9.04 on site (8.89 in the laboratory) |
| Radon | under 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ curie per kg |
| Dissolved solids | 238 mg per kg (dried at 110°C) |
| Spring type | **alkaline simple spring** (hypotonic, alkaline, low-temperature) |

Ōtsu city's page for the Ogoto Onsen Kankō Kōen park (updated 1 April 2024) gives the same type, a simple spring, hypotonic, alkaline and low-temperature, colourless and clear.

### The association's older figures

The tourism association's own spring table, which gives no year, says alkaline simple spring, pH 9.0, **36 degrees**, hypotonic, radon 2.36 Mache units per kg, and a flow of **393 m³ a day** (about 400 kilolitres). The temperature and flow differ from the 2019 analysis. This page uses the dated 2019 figures and gives the association's table as undated.

Both radon figures are below the 8.25 Mache units (30 × 10⁻¹⁰ curie) at which a spring is named radioactive, which fits the type name having no radioactive label. The association says a 1914 analysis called the water a "radium mineral spring"; see the [radioactive springs guide](/en/soak/acidic-sulphur-radioactive-springs/) for how the official names work.

### Six wells, three in use

The cooperative's report lists the sources by number:

| Source | Drilled | Depth | Temperature | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ōtsu city No. 1 | listed under "1951–" | 31.6 m | 19.5°C | not in use |
| No. 2 | 1958 | — | 19.5°C | not in use |
| No. 3 | 1959 | 1,000 m | 26°C | not in use |
| No. 4 | 1987 | 1,000 m | 35.9°C | in use |
| No. 5 | 1997 | 1,800 m | 35.9°C | in use |
| No. 6 | 2006 | 1,600 m | 44.2°C | in use |

The association's English page says all hotels and inns in Ogoto Onsen use hot-spring water for their baths. Official sources do not explain where the heat or the water comes from; the only fact given is that the wells in use are 1,000 to 1,800 m deep.

### How the city supplies it

Ōtsu city's screening criteria for permits under the **Ōtsu City Ogoto Onsen Supply Ordinance** (1959, Ordinance No. 26) set out the system:

- The city supplies spring water **only for the in-house baths** of ryokan and other facilities the mayor considers suitable (Article 7).
- On an application, the mayor surveys the flow and other conditions before granting a permit, and may limit, withhold or refuse supply when there is not enough water (Article 9).
- The criteria include that there is no risk of the water being **resold or given free to a third party**, and that the amount does not exceed what the city can supply.
- The standard processing time is 14 days, handled by the city's tourism promotion division.

The city's published standards for administrative penalties (updated 15 October 2021) also list suspension of supply and fines under the same ordinance. Read together with the cooperative's list of sources, these describe water from the numbered city sources going to the inns.

## Where it came from

### Saichō and Hōkō-ji

The accounts agree that the spring is tied to the temple of **Hōkō-ji**, but differ on who founded the temple and how far they credit Saichō, founder of the Tendai school on Mt Hiei:

- **The tourism association**: the spring was opened **about 1,200 years ago by Saichō** (Dengyō Daishi). Hōkō-ji, a Tendai temple south of Ogoto, has a pond in its grounds, the Nenbutsu-ike (also called Ja-ga-ike). Saichō enshrined the Nōka Jizō, one of the Six Jizō of Sakamoto, at the pond, and the water was treasured as sacred. In the **Taishō era** it was analysed, found to be a "radium mineral spring", and development as a hot spring began.
- **Shiga's tourism site** (updated 30 June 2016): the spring **is said to have been** opened by Saichō; Hōkō-ji was built **in the mid-9th century by Imao no Sukune** of the Otsuki clan, who opened up Ogoto. Travellers on the western Ōmi road visited the sacred water in its grounds, the Kaji-i or Nenbutsu-ike.
- **Ōtsu city**: Hōkō-ji is an old Tendai temple **said to have been founded by Saichō** (767–822), with traditions about the pond's water; in modern times the water was analysed and found to be onsen. The city does not itself say Saichō opened the spring.

So Hōkō-ji's founder is Saichō according to the city and Imao no Sukune according to Shiga's tourism site, and "opened by Saichō" is the association's and the tourism site's tradition. "About 1,200 years" is the association's and the cooperative's way of counting. This page gives all of them and chooses none.

### From analysis to resort

The cooperative's report, citing a document on the spring's origins, says the temple's water was formally analysed at the **Osaka Sanitary Testing Laboratory in June 1914** and recognised as onsen. It then lists:

- **1923**: the first onsen ryokan opens.
- **1974**: the Kosei line opens, with Ogoto station.
- **2008**: the station is renamed **Ogoto-onsen**.

### The name

The two accounts are close:

- **The tourism association**: the sound of a *koto* was heard from the house of Imao no Sukune, and the name is said to have come from the "O" (雄) of his name and *koto* (琴).
- **Ōtsu city**: in the Heian period Imao no Sukune of the Otsuki clan received the land as an estate, giving "O"; the sound of a *koto* often drifted from the noble's residence, giving "koto".

### Mt Hiei

The association's English page says Enryaku-ji on Mt Hiei was founded by Saichō (767–822) in the 8th century and is a World Heritage site. Its Japanese page on Ogoto and Mt Hiei quotes a late-Heian text that called Lake Biwa "the pond of Tendai and Yakushi".

## How the resort works

### By the lake

The tourism association calls Ogoto a hot spring where, unusually in Japan, you can watch the **sunrise** from open-air baths overlooking Lake Biwa, and a spring resort with a harbour. It also lists facilities open for day bathing and a free footbath. Day-bathing prices are on individual inns' pages, which this page does not cover.

### The footbath park

The **Ogoto Onsen Kankō Kōen** is an Ōtsu city facility, managed for the city by the inns' cooperative (city page, updated 1 April 2024). It has a tourist exchange centre, a park and a **covered footbath, free, 72 m² with 22 seats**.

- **Hours.** 9:00 to 18:00.
- **Closed.** 30 December to 1 January, with occasional other closures.
- **Access.** About 20 minutes on foot or 5 minutes by taxi from JR Ogoto-onsen station; about 5 minutes by car from the Ōgi-Ogoto interchange.

The association's footbath page also says it is free for everyone. As of 13 October 2026 its home page carried a notice that the hexagonal footbath in front of the JR station was closed.

### From the station

Shiga's tourism site says the inns are about 20 minutes' walk from JR Ogoto-onsen station, and that **each ryokan runs a shuttle bus**.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The tourism association's English page covers the spring, its water, sights, food and access, but not tattoos or how to bathe. Shiga's tourism site and the city's page say nothing on them either. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Ogoto. Ask each inn before you book.

### Compared with city baths

No official source compares Ogoto's water with baths elsewhere. The city's supply ordinance does limit where the water goes: it is supplied for the in-house baths of inns and similar facilities, on condition that it is not resold or given to third parties. That is a supply rule, not a statement about Ogoto water used away from Ogoto. For what to read on any bath's sign, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

The tourism association's **English page** names dishes served at Ogoto Onsen: **Ōmi beef** as steak or shabu-shabu; **Biwa trout, ayu and funa-zushi**, which it calls traditional dishes of long standing; duck in winter; Ōmi rice and local sake. Its Japanese home page mentions only local dishes nurtured by Lake Biwa, without names, and Shiga's tourism site speaks of "varied flavours".

The farm ministry's list of Shiga's local dishes includes:

- **Funa-zushi**, for the whole prefecture, often made with roe-bearing nigoro-buna from Lake Biwa, selected in **1998** as one of Shiga's intangible folk cultural properties, the "food cultural assets of Shiga".
- **Ōmi beef in miso** (*Ōmi-gyū no misozuke*), for the whole prefecture, with a record of the Hikone domain presenting beef in miso in 1781.

So funa-zushi appears both on the association's English page and in the ministry's list, although the ministry does not name Ogoto. For beef, the association names steak and shabu-shabu and the ministry names beef in miso, a different dish. The cooperative's report says it was registered as an Ōmi beef designated shop in 2010; that is the cooperative's own account. More in [funa-zushi](/en/eat/sushi-funa/) and the [Kinki local dishes guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-kinki/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

- **By train from Kyoto.** The tourism association says to take a local train on the **JR Kosei line**, not the Biwako line, to **Ogoto-onsen station**, the fifth stop from Kyoto. On JR West's line timetable for 13 October 2026, departures from Kyoto and from Ogoto-onsen are **19 to 28 minutes apart**, mostly 19 to 25, so the ride itself is a minute or so shorter.
- **From the station.** About 20 minutes on foot; each ryokan runs a shuttle bus (Shiga's tourism site).
- **By car.** The association gives about 20 minutes from Kyoto, by the Meishin expressway's Kyoto-Higashi interchange, the Nishi-Ōtsu bypass and the Kosei road to the Ōgi-Ogoto interchange. Shiga's tourism site gives about 10 minutes from that interchange to each inn.
- **From Osaka.** Shiga's tourism site, last updated in June 2016, says "under an hour from Kyoto and Osaka". Official sources do not give a more recent time from Osaka.

Check times with the operators before you travel.

For other springs in the region, see [Arima](/en/soak/arima/), [Kinosaki](/en/soak/kinosaki/) and [Yunoyama](/en/soak/yunoyama/) in Mie.

## What people get wrong

- **"The water is 36 degrees."** That is the association's undated table. The 2019 analysis gives 27.0°C, from a mix of three sources.
- **"It is a radium spring."** A 1914 analysis used that phrase, but the 2019 analysis names an alkaline simple spring, with radon well below the radioactive threshold.
- **"Saichō founded the spring; it is recorded history."** It is the association's and Shiga's tourism site's tradition. The city credits Saichō with Hōkō-ji, Shiga's site credits Imao no Sukune, and the water was recognised as onsen after analysis in 1914.
- **"Each inn has its own well."** The ordinance and the cooperative's list describe city-numbered sources supplied to the inns' in-house baths.
- **"Take the Biwako line."** Ogoto-onsen is on the Kosei line.
