Asamushi Onsen is a spa town by the sea in the city of Aomori, at the base of the Natsudomari peninsula. Its tourism association says it has more than 1,200 years of history and that it has been called "the Atami of Tōhoku" and "Aomori’s *okuzashiki*", its secluded retreat. It lies within the Asamushi-Natsudomari prefectural natural park. This page uses Aomori city, Aomori prefecture and its tourism site (Amazing AOMORI), the Asamushi Onsen tourism association and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, each is given with its source. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the sulphate-chloride spring named below, see [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/) and [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### What the official pages say

Aomori prefecture’s tourism site describes the water two ways:

- **Its feature on the spa town**: colourless and clear, fairly hot but not harsh, and rich in sodium and calcium ions.
- **Its spot page**: colourless, clear, tasteless and odourless, with a gentle feel.

Aomori city, describing the public drinking fountain, calls it an alkaline water containing sodium and calcium. The prefecture’s page on one public bath, Matsu-no-yu, gives a **sodium-calcium sulphate-chloride spring** for its family bath. That is one bathhouse’s label, not an analysis of the whole town.

Official sources do not give a source count, total flow or pH for Asamushi. The tourism association’s map points out the large tanks of the hot-spring cooperative that supply water to the inns around town.

### Temperatures you can see

Aomori city gives two temperatures at **Asamushi Gensen Kōen**, the source park (page updated 23 December 2024):

- The **drinking fountain** runs water from a source at **67°C to 68°C**, free-flowing.
- The **egg-boiling spring** uses water at **72°C to 73°C**, and takes up to about 100 eggs at a time.

The city says eggs take about 15 minutes; the tourism association’s map says 18 to 20. Bring your own eggs.

### The prefecture’s figures, for scale

Aomori prefecture’s page on hot springs gives figures for the whole prefecture, not for Asamushi: as of the end of March 2025, 1,089 sources, 670 of them at 42°C or more, 120 spa areas and a total flow of 139,808 litres a minute.

### An area "requiring consideration"

Aomori prefecture’s guidelines on protecting hot springs list **Asamushi**, part of Aomori city, among the "areas requiring consideration", alongside Sukayu and Tashirotai among others. The guidelines define these as areas where sources interfere noticeably with one another, where the hot-spring content is declining, or where the water rises in an unusual way. They do not say which of these applies to Asamushi, and the document’s date could not be read.

### Where the heat comes from

Official sources do not say where Asamushi’s heat comes from, or whether it is volcanic. The tourism association describes the setting, not the cause: Hadakajima, a 33 m rhyolite rock, is said to have once been joined to the land.

### Not a health resort

Asamushi does not have a plan as a national health resort hot spring on the environment ministry’s list. In Aomori, the prefecture says, only [Sukayu](/en/soak/sukayu/) is designated.

## Where it came from

### Hemp, a monk and a deer

The tourism association gives one account. In the Heian period, the spring found by the monk Jikaku Daishi (Ennin) was used only to steam hemp for weaving. In **1190**, Enkō Daishi (Hōnen), visiting the area, saw a wounded deer bathing in it and urged the villagers to bathe, and from then on people used it. The spa’s name comes from the steaming of hemp: *asa-mushi*, "hemp steaming", is said to have become Asamushi. The prefecture’s tourism site gives "more than 1,200 years of history". Official sources do not give any other account of the name.

### Yunoshima, the island of hot water

The tourism association’s map describes **Yunoshima** as a conical island 132 m high, about 800 m offshore to the north-west. It says the name, "hot-water island", comes from hot water rising from the sea just in front of it. That is how the association explains the name, not a geological account. Halfway up the island stands the Yunoshima Benzaiten shrine (Yunoshima jinja), reached by 96 steps, with its main festival in April. The association calls the red torii "the symbol of Asamushi".

### Munakata Shikō

The tourism association’s map introduces one inn, Tsubakikan, as an inn connected with the woodblock artist Munakata Shikō, with a gallery of his paintings. Aomori city’s page on Munakata does not mention Asamushi, and official sources do not say when or why he stayed. This page goes no further than the association’s description.

## How the town works

### Two sides of the station

Aomori prefecture’s tourism site divides the town at the Aoimori Railway’s Asamushi-onsen station. To the east, narrow lanes hold old inns and diners, with the egg-boiling spring, the drinking fountain, Asamushi Onsen forest park and the aquarium. To the west is the seaside: Sunset Beach Asamushi and the roadside station Yu-sa Asamushi. The prefecture’s feature, undated, counts ten inns and *minshuku* in the Asamushi district.

### Free footbaths, fountain and eggs

Aomori city lists two **footbaths**, one in the station plaza and one in Asamushi Gensen Kōen. As checked on 13 October 2026:

- **Footbaths**: April to October 7:00 to 19:30, November to March 8:00 to 19:30, open every day. Bring a towel.
- **Drinking fountain**: open 24 hours.
- **Egg-boiling spring**: 6:00 to 18:00.

All three are free.

### Bathing

- **Matsu-no-yu.** A public bath listed on the prefecture’s tourism site, open 8:00 to 20:00 (last entry 19:30), closed Mondays and New Year’s Day. Its fee is not given here because the page does not say whether it includes tax.
- **Yu-sa Asamushi.** Aomori city says the observation bath on the fifth floor of the roadside station has been suspended because of an equipment failure (page updated 21 August 2026). On 13 October 2026 the page gave no reopening date.

Official sources checked for this page do not give any bathing fee marked as including tax, so none is given.

### The aquarium

The prefectural **Asamushi Aquarium** shows about 300 species. The prefecture’s tourism site says 8,000 animals and the tourism association’s map 10,000; the two do not agree. A 15 m tunnel tank recreates Mutsu Bay, including the farming of scallops and sea squirts. It is open 9:00 to 17:00 (last entry 16:30) every day, with longer hours possible in Golden Week and Obon. Aomori prefecture announced that on **3 April 2026** it reopened two tanks, a squid tank and a jellyfish photo-spot tank, paid for by donations. The admission fee is not given here because the page does not say whether it includes tax.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The tourism association’s site has a language switch but no separate English text. Amazing AOMORI has an English page on Asamushi Onsen, but it says nothing about how to bathe or about tattoos, and neither do Aomori city’s pages. Official sources do not give a tattoo rule for Asamushi. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each inn in advance.

### Compared with city baths

No official Asamushi source compares its water with baths elsewhere. The one thing the city does say is that the drinking fountain runs free-flowing from the source. For what the notice at an inn’s bath tells you, see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

**Kujira-mochi.** Aomori prefecture’s tourism feature names *kujira-mochi* (久慈良餅) as Asamushi’s speciality, made by two long-established shops: a steamed sweet of rice flour mixed with strained bean paste and sugar, with the flavour of walnuts. The tourism association’s map adds, of one shop, that the name carries a wish for "a good rice cake cherished for a long time", and that it is made from milled Tsugaru rice. It is not in the farm ministry’s list of 29 Aomori dishes, and it is a different sweet from Yamagata’s *kujira-mochi*. So it is Asamushi’s sweet, not a regional dish.

**Scallops from Mutsu Bay.** The association’s map lists places serving scallop set meals and scallop bowls, scallop fishing as an activity, and the aquarium shows scallop farming. The farm ministry’s Aomori story tells of Mutsu Bay scallops, first farmed successfully between 1955 and 1964, but does not mention Asamushi. It gives *kaiyaki miso*, scallop-shell miso, to the Shimokita and Tsugaru areas. So the link is Mutsu Bay scallops, not a dish of the spa.

**Ginger miso oden.** The farm ministry gives Aomori city as the home of *shōga miso oden*, said to have begun at post-war black-market stalls to warm passengers of the Seikan ferry. One shop on the association’s map has it on the menu. Asamushi is in Aomori city, but the ministry does not mention Asamushi, so treat it as an Aomori city dish you may find here. More in the [Tōhoku local food guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-tohoku/).

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page.

- **From Tokyo.** About 1 hour 20 minutes by air to Aomori (the prefecture’s tourism site), then about 35 minutes by limousine bus from Aomori airport to Aomori station (the tourism association). The Tōhoku shinkansen runs direct to Hachinohe and Shin-Aomori; the official pages do not give a time.
- **From Shin-Aomori.** About 6 minutes on the Ōu line to Aomori station (the association).
- **From Aomori station by train.** By the Aoimori Railway to Asamushi-onsen station: about 25 minutes by the tourism association, 30 minutes in the prefecture’s feature, about 20 minutes on its spot page.
- **By city bus.** About 36 minutes from Aomori station to the Asamushi-onsen-eki stop on the Aomori city bus (the prefecture’s aquarium page).
- **From Sendai.** The association’s table gives about 1 hour 20 minutes by shinkansen to Hachinohe, then about 70 minutes by the Aoimori Railway.
- **By car.** About 15 minutes from the Aomori-higashi interchange by Route 4 (the association).

The times above are the sources’ round figures, not a timetable. For other spa towns in Tōhoku, see [Sukayu](/en/soak/sukayu/), [Hanamaki](/en/soak/hanamaki/), [Naruko](/en/soak/naruko/), [Akiu](/en/soak/akiu/), [Iizaka](/en/soak/iizaka/) and [Higashiyama](/en/soak/higashiyama/).

## What people get wrong

- **"Asamushi’s water comes from the sea, so it is not volcanic."** Official sources do not say where its heat comes from. Yunoshima’s name refers to water rising offshore, not to how it is heated.
- **"It is 30 minutes from Aomori by train."** Official pages give 20, 25 and 30 minutes.
- **"Kujira-mochi has whale in it, or is the same as Yamagata’s."** Asamushi’s is a rice-flour sweet with bean paste and walnuts, written with different characters.
- **"Munakata Shikō lived at Asamushi."** Official sources say only that an inn is connected with him.
- **"Ginger miso oden is Asamushi’s dish."** The farm ministry ties it to Aomori city.
- **"The water cures things."** The founding story has a wounded deer; this page does not make the claim.
