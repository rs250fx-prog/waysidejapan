Noboribetsu Onsen is in Hokkaido, and its tourism association describes it as a town where hot water rises all over, much of it from Jigokudani, "Hell Valley". It is known for having many kinds of water in one place. This page covers what the association and Hokkaido’s tourism organisation say about the water, why their counts differ, what the town is like to walk around, and how to get there. The geology of Kuttara volcano and the town’s founding dates are set out in the Hokkaido overview; the ten official spring types and how to read the sign by the bath are in the guide to onsen water types.

## Where it came from

The association’s Japanese and English pages say the springs have been used from the Edo period to the present. On the founding there are two accounts, which the Hokkaido overview sets out: Okada Hanbei in 1857 and Takimoto Kinzō in 1858. This site does not choose a starting point.

## The water

### Hell Valley

The association says that much of Noboribetsu’s water wells up from Jigokudani, which it describes as created by volcanic activity about 10,000 years ago. Its English page says the same: "Many of these ONSEN come from Jigokudani (Hell Valley)". The 10,000 years is the association’s figure. The weather agency’s description of Kuttara volcano and the Jigokudani craters, which does not give that date, is in the Hokkaido overview.

Hokkaido’s tourism organisation describes Jigokudani as a place where boiling water and steam come out from between the rocks, and names Oyunuma as a nearby lake.

I could not find the number of sources or the flow. Neither the association’s Japanese or English pages, nor Noboribetsu city’s site, nor the environment ministry’s page for the Shikotsu-Tōya national park gives them.

### Nine or ten

The town is famous for its variety of water, but the count depends on who is counting, and this site does not choose.

- **Ten.** The association’s page on spring types says Noboribetsu has ten kinds.
- **Nine names on the English page.** The association’s English page lists sulphur, salt (chloride), alum (aluminium-bearing), Glauber’s salt (sulphate), green vitriol (aluminium-bearing), iron, acidic, bicarbonate of soda and radium springs. I could only count nine in what I read, so I could not match it to the ten.
- **Nine.** Hokkaido’s tourism organisation, in English, says "nine mineral-rich waters" and names sulphur, salt, iron, acidic iron, alum, Glauber’s salt and bicarbonate of soda.

None of these pages explains how it counts. Several of the names are older ones, and "radium spring" is not one of the official spring types; the Hokkaido overview explains how the association’s list lines up with the official ten.

### What you notice in the bath

The association’s English page on its waters gives practical notes for bathers. These are notes about bathing, not claims about health.

- In sulphur springs and salt springs, soap does not lather well.
- People with sensitive skin should avoid long soaks in salt, alum and acidic springs.
- Iron springs can turn a white towel reddish.
- After washing your hair in a bicarbonate of soda spring and rinsing with the water, hair feels glossy, the page says.

The same page describes nearby **Karurusu Onsen** as a Glauber’s salt simple spring containing radium, at 48 to 74°C where it emerges. Those figures are for Karurusu, not Noboribetsu Onsen.

## How the town works

Hokkaido’s tourism organisation describes hot springs all over the town, the smell of sulphur, white steam rising from the ground, and statues of *oni* (ogres) around the streets. It suggests going round the baths of different inns and hotels as one way to enjoy the town.

The association’s list of sights names Noboribetsu Jigokudani, Oyunuma, the Oyunuma river, Tessen-ike pond, Okunoyu, and a geyser in Sengen Park. Its one footbath on that list is the natural footbath on the Oyunuma river, which Hokkaido’s tourism organisation describes as a footbath in the forest.

The association lists day bathing in three areas — Noboribetsu Onsen, Karurusu Onsen and the wider Noboribetsu area — at inns and at dedicated day facilities.

## Bathing there

The association’s day-bathing pages give adult prices across a range, but do not say whether they include tax, so I do not give them. Some facilities list a reception time separate from their opening hours; the pages do not explain the difference, so check the last entry time. The pages do not explain how to buy a ticket, bathing manners or tattoos.

**Tattoos.** I could not find a policy on the association’s Japanese or English pages, on its day-bathing pages, or on Hokkaido’s tourism site. That site suggests checking the facility’s own website or the association (0143-84-3311). I do not say whether you can bathe; ask in advance, and a private bath is one option. The water types guide has what the tourism agency and JNTO say in general.

**Food.** The association’s list of sights has categories for meals, cafés and sweets, bars, food and drink shops and souvenirs, but no page that ties a particular dish to Noboribetsu Onsen. I could not find an official source linking a local dish to the town, so there is none here.

## Getting there

From the association’s English access page (checked 10 October 2026). It has no update date, and some air and ferry details on it are marked as of November 2021, so check with the operator before you travel. Fares are not on this page.

- **From New Chitose Airport** — the airport train to Minami-Chitose, then a limited express (*Hokuto* or *Suzuran*) to Noboribetsu station; the page gives no total time. Or the Kōsoku Airport-gō bus (reservation required), about 1 hour, or the Kōsoku Hayabusa-gō bus (reservation required), about 1 hour 15 minutes with a change.
- **From Sapporo** — limited express about 1 hour 10 minutes; intercity bus (several services, one requiring reservation) about 1 hour 20 minutes.
- **From Noboribetsu station** — Hokkaido’s tourism organisation gives about 15 minutes by bus.
- **By car** — Hokkaido’s tourism organisation gives about 10 minutes from the Noboribetsu-Higashi interchange on the Dōō Expressway and about 60 minutes from New Chitose Airport. The association’s Japanese top page gives about 1 hour 40 minutes from Sapporo and about 1 hour from New Chitose Airport.

The JNTO page I tried for Noboribetsu did not open, so I have not used it.

## What people get wrong

- **"Noboribetsu has ten official spring types."** It has ten on the association’s count and nine on the regional tourism organisation’s; neither is the official classification.
- **"Hell Valley is 10,000 years old, officially."** That is the association’s figure.
- **"Tattoos are banned in Noboribetsu."** I could not find an official policy either way.
- **"The water treats skin conditions."** The association’s notes are about bathing, such as soap and towels. This page makes no health claims.
