Nagato-Yumoto Onsen is in Nagato city, Yamaguchi, along two rivers, the Otozure and the Taineiji. It is an alkaline spring with a long temple story behind it, a public bath built directly over its source, and, since 2019, terraces set out over the river. This page covers the water and the different figures given for it, the legend of its discovery, how the river terraces came about, how the town works, food and the way in. It uses the Nagato-Yumoto inns' cooperative, the town's official tourism site (run by Nagato Yumoto Onsen Machi Co.), Nagato city, Yamaguchi's tourism site, the prefecture's environmental health research centre, the farm ministry and the environment ministry. Where they disagree, I give each. It does not rate any bath or inn.

What "alkaline simple spring" means is in the guide to [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/). For reading the analysis sign, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### What the cooperative says

The inns' cooperative says the water is an onsen as defined in the Hot Springs Act, of the **alkaline simple** type, and that Yamaguchi prefecture published survey results on 5 August 2004 stating that **no bath additives were mixed in**.

It then gives two sets of figures, from different sources and different years:

- **Onto, the public bath.** Its own source, the Onto spring, is **39.0°C with 36.5 litres a minute (2020 survey)**, an alkaline simple spring of **pH 9.62**. Both the men's and women's baths are flow-through, *kakenagashi*.
- **The inns.** Water goes to 12 inns and 15 facilities in all, delivered at about 52°C. The cooperative lists **city-owned source No. 1 at 34.2°C and 675.0 litres a minute, and No. 3 at 39.0°C and 234.4 litres a minute (both February 2004 surveys)**. The mixed water of the two is an alkaline simple spring of pH 9.67, from an analysis dated 9 February 2000. Some inns use flow-through and some recirculate and filter, and the cooperative says the recirculating ones nearly all combine it with flow-through.

So the figures come from 2000, 2004 and 2020. I give each with its source and year rather than a single number for "the" spring. The cooperative also says the spring source in the centre of the town is still owned by Taineiji, the temple in the legend below.

The English pages of the official tourism site give a "simple alkaline hot spring (pH 9.62)" and describe Onto's water, at 39 degrees, as "slightly lukewarm".

### A bath over its source

According to the official tourism site, Onto stands **directly above its spring source**. Its water is an *ashimoto yūshutsu-sen*, a spring that rises from under your feet, and you can see the hot water coming up from the bedrock. The bath is **1 metre deep**, which the site compares with 50 to 60 cm for an ordinary public bath.

### Where the heat comes from

I could not find an answer. I found no public or academic source that explains how Nagato-Yumoto's spring forms, and I do not guess.

For background, a paper by the prefecture's environmental health research centre, covering the springs it analysed from 1951 to 2004, counts **39 sources in Nagato city**. The environment ministry's onsen use table (end of March 2025) gives Yamaguchi as a whole 404 sources, of which 276 are below 25°C and only 20 are 42°C or above. That is the prefecture, not Nagato-Yumoto.

Nagato-Yumoto is not on the environment ministry's list of *kokumin hoyō onsenchi*, the designated health resort onsen areas; Yamaguchi's entries are Tawarayama and Mitsuo.

## Where it came from

### The 1427 legend

The official tourism site says that about 600 years ago, in **1427** (Ōei 34, in the Muromachi period), the Zen priest **Jōan of Taineiji** found the spring after a revelation from the deity **Sumiyoshi Daimyōjin**, and that it is known as the spring with the longest history in Yamaguchi. The inns' cooperative gives the same year and the same story, adding that the revelation came during *zazen*.

The cooperative also tells the legend in outline. On the 23rd day of the third month of Ōei 34, Jōan gave a robe to an old man. The old man was Sumiyoshi Daimyōjin, the deity of Nagato's first shrine, who told him that he had brought out a hot spring deep in the hills for him to use, turned into a dragon and vanished above the clouds.

Taineiji, the official tourism site says, was once a great Sōtō Zen temple, prosperous enough to be called "the Kōya of the west". In the Edo period, the domain's lords came often to take the waters.

Yamaguchi's tourism site calls Nagato-Yumoto **the prefecture's oldest onsen area**, said to have opened in the Muromachi period, and speaks of 600 years since its opening. I found no other account of its beginning on the official pages, so this legend is the only one I give.

### Two baths, by rank

The cooperative says that in the past the upper bath, the **Reitō**, was used by samurai and monks, and the lower one, **Onto**, by ordinary people. In the Edo period the baths were kept by the domain, with a reception house, the Seionte, nearby. A photo caption on its page says Onto was **renewed in 2020**.

### The river terraces, step by step

Nagato city's own pages give a clear timeline:

- **16 September to 9 October 2017.** A social experiment under the "Nagato-Yumoto Mirai Project" set up river terraces, *kawadoko*, on the Otozure and Taineiji rivers, with lighting at night (page updated 6 October 2017).
- **23 October 2018.** The stretch of both rivers was designated a riverside "urban and regional regeneration use area", **the first in Yamaguchi**. A council of local residents and businesses, the Nagato-Yumoto Osoto Katsuyō Kyōgikai, became the body using the river space, to set up and run terraces and events, from November (page updated 23 October 2018).
- **29 March 2019.** The terraces were opened, with **four river terraces** and benches now in place permanently (page updated 29 March 2019). The city's 2025 article still speaks of four terraces.

The official tourism site adds that in **2020** the town held a renewal opening, with the opening of a new inn and the rebuilding of Onto. Yamaguchi's tourism site also refers to the town's regeneration in 2020. So 2019 is the year of the terraces, and 2020 the year of the wider renewal.

The plan behind all this, the Nagato-Yumoto Onsen tourism town-planning plan, is dated differently. **The official tourism site says Heisei 29 (2017). The cover of Nagato city's summary of the plan says August Heisei 28 (2016).** I have not found which one marks its adoption, so I give both.

## How the town works

### "Osoto tengoku"

The official tourism site calls the town an *osoto tengoku*, an "outdoor heaven", with six kinds of attraction scattered along the Otozure river: outdoor baths, eating while walking, cultural activities, strolling, places that make a picture, and spaces to rest and linger. Yamaguchi's tourism site mentions the stone-paved riverside path, stepping stones and the terraces.

### Onto

Onto is the town's walk-in public bath, open **10:00 to 22:00 with irregular closing days**, according to the Japanese pages of the official tourism site. Its fees for adults, children and a one-day ticket are listed without a tax note, so I do not give them.

There is one exception. The **first bath of the day can be booked privately for ¥30,000 per group, tax included** (checked 13 October 2026): every day from 7:40 to 8:40, two groups a day, up to six people each, booked at least three days ahead.

After bathing there is a café, and a free reading corner and an *engawa* Othello board, according to the same site.

### Inns and footbaths

The official tourism site's bathing list includes day bathing and saunas at inns, with hours and prices that vary by place. The inns' cooperative lists two footbaths: one in the Otozure riverside park, laid out as a garden with an arbour, and the Otozure footbath on the river path. **Neither page gives hours or fees.**

## Bathing there

- **English and tattoos.** The English pages of the official tourism site, "Visiting Hot Springs" and "Transport", describe Onto but say nothing about tattoos or bathing manners. I found no official rule. Ask the bath or the inn.
- **Check the Japanese pages.** The English pages (marked ©2024) differ from the current Japanese ones on bus times, Onto's one-day ticket and Onto's closing day: the English page says the third Tuesday of each month, the Japanese page says irregular. I follow the Japanese.
- **Compared with city baths.** I found no official comparison. The only thing on sale that touches on it is the business's own: Onto's front desk sells a bath liquid, "Craft Onsen Onto", made from Onto's water combined with minerals from the area, which Onto's page says lets you enjoy the spring at home. That is the seller's description. What you can compare anywhere is the posted sign, which says whether the water is diluted, heated, recirculated or disinfected; see the piece on [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## What it is eaten with

Yamaguchi's tourism site suggests eating your way around the town with kawara soba and yakitori. The official tourism site's food page lists **two kawara soba specialists**, yakitori and set-meal places, pizza and cafés, and inn restaurants serving Chōshū chicken and seafood. I do not name restaurants or inns.

- **Kawara soba.** It was born at Kawatana Onsen, which is in Shimonoseki, not Nagato. Nagato-Yumoto is a place where you can eat it. More in the [Setouchi local dishes guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-setouchi/).
- **Whale.** The farm ministry's Yamaguchi story says Nagato city has a whaling history, recording that a whaling group from Senzaki bay was taken into the service of the Chōshū domain in 1672 (Kanbun 12), and that whale in *nanban* sauce and whale *tatsuta-age* are still made. That is the ministry's link to Nagato city; whale does not appear on Nagato-Yumoto's official food page.
- **Fugu and tilefish.** I found neither on Nagato-Yumoto's official food page. The farm ministry places fried fugu in the Shimonoseki and Hagi areas. So I do not present them as the town's food.

## Getting there

From the Japanese access page of the official tourism site (checked 13 October 2026). Fares are listed without a tax note, so they are not on this page.

- **From Shin-Yamaguchi, direct.** A shared taxi service runs to the town in **about 1 hour**, four a day: leaving Shin-Yamaguchi at 11:15, 13:45, 15:25 and 16:55, arriving at 12:20, 14:50, 16:30 and 18:00. Booking is recommended.
- **By rail and bus.** **The JR Mine line has been suspended since heavy rain in June 2023**, with replacement buses. Shin-Yamaguchi to Asa on the Sanyō line takes about 30 minutes, then about an hour by replacement bus. **From 1 October 2026 the replacement bus stops at "Nagato-Yumoto Onsen"**, inside the onsen car park, not at Nagato-Yumoto station.
- **From the airport.** A shared taxi, booked in advance, runs from Yamaguchi Ube airport in about 1 hour 20 minutes, on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and Mondays from 3 April 2026 to 29 March 2027.
- **From Fukuoka.** The highway bus "Hagi-Nagato Otozure-gō" runs direct from Hakata and Tenjin, twice a day each way, for example Hakata 8:06 to Nagato-Yumoto Onsen 11:11, and 16:30 to 19:36.
- **By car.** From the car park to the Onto square is about 3 minutes on foot.

The English transport page gives one bus a day from Hakata at 9:01 and a direct bus of about 1 hour 8 minutes from Shin-Yamaguchi; the Japanese page is the current one. I found no official times from Hagi, Izumo-shi or Yonago. For other spring towns in the region, see [Yuda](/en/soak/yuda/) in Yamaguchi city, [Yunotsu](/en/soak/yunotsu/) on the Shimane coast, [Kaike](/en/soak/kaike/) and [Misasa](/en/soak/misasa/) in Tottori, [Tamatsukuri](/en/soak/tamatsukuri/) near Matsue and [Yubara](/en/soak/yubara/) in Okayama.

## What people get wrong

- **"The terraces were built in 2020."** Nagato city's pages give a trial in 2017, designation of the river space in 2018 and the terrace opening in March 2019. 2020 was the town's renewal opening.
- **"The river is the Fukawa."** The rivers are the Otozure and the Taineiji. Fukawa is part of the address, Fukawa-Yumoto.
- **"Nagato-Yumoto is 39°C."** That is the Onto source in 2020. The city-owned sources were 34.2°C and 39.0°C in 2004.
- **"Kawara soba comes from here."** It comes from Kawatana Onsen in Shimonoseki; you can eat it here.
- **"Fugu is the town's dish."** It is not on the official food page; the farm ministry ties fried fugu to Shimonoseki and Hagi.
- **"You can take the Mine line to the town."** It has been suspended since June 2023; replacement buses run from Asa.
- **"The terraces are free public seating."** Nagato city describes them used for booked plans. I found nothing on free seating.
