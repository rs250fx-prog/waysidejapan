Okuhida Onsen-gō is not one town but five spa areas in the north-east of Takayama city, Gifu: **Hirayu, Fukuji, Shin-Hirayu, Tochio and Shin-Hotaka**. They lie between 800 and 1,300 m up, spread over about 18 km along rivers that rise on Mt Norikura and in the Yarigatake and Hotaka ranges of the Northern Alps. This page uses the environment ministry's plan for the area, the Japan Meteorological Agency, the Okuhida Onsen-gō tourism association, the Hida Takayama tourism site, Nōhi Bus and the farm ministry. Where they disagree, both are given. It does not rate any bath or inn.

For the spring types named below, see [hydrogen-carbonate, sulphate, iodine and simple springs](/en/soak/hydrogen-carbonate-sulphate-iodine-simple-springs/) and [carbon dioxide, chloride and iron springs](/en/soak/carbon-dioxide-chloride-iron-springs/). For reading the sign at any bath, see [onsen water types](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/).

## The water

### Five areas, one health resort

Okuhida is one of the environment ministry's **national health resort hot springs**. The ministry's list, as of October 2022, gives the date as 19 November 1968. Its plan for the area, dated April 2024, explains why: **Hirayu** was designated in 1964 and the other four areas in 1968, and in 2017 they were merged as Okuhida Onsen-gō.

### More than a hundred sources

The plan says there are **more than 100 sources**, of which **96** are used for bathing:

| Area | Sources used for bathing |
|---|---|
| Hirayu | 28 |
| Fukuji | 9 |
| Shin-Hirayu | 19 |
| Tochio | 8 |
| Shin-Hotaka | 32 |

Its table of 12 sample sources gives temperatures of **60.0 to 92.0°C** and flows of 190 to 500 litres a minute, some flowing naturally and some pumped. The types include simple springs, sodium hydrogen-carbonate springs and chloride springs. The table gives no pH and no year. Because so many sources are hot, the plan says, **stream water and the like is added** before the water is used for bathing.

The plan also lists other uses of the heat: fish farming, greenhouses, melting snow on roads, heating, a heated pool, and **five geothermal power stations** in operation.

### The association's figures

The tourism association gives more than 100 sources, an average temperature above 60°C and **37,000 litres a minute**, which it calls **third in Japan**, citing the Japan Hot Spring Association's statistics without a year. The ministry's plan gives no total flow.

### Yakedake and Norikura

The Meteorological Agency watches two volcanoes here around the clock:

- **Yakedake** (2,455 m): a stratovolcano of andesite and dacite with a lava dome at the summit. Almost all its eruptions in recorded history have been steam-driven, it steams actively even in quiet times, and its last magmatic eruption was about 2,300 years ago. On 13 October 2026 the agency showed it at **alert level 1**. It lowered the level from 2 (restrictions near the crater) to 1 at 14:00 on **4 March 2026**, after an increase in volcanic earthquakes from 25 January.
- **Mt Norikura** (3,026 m): alert level 1 on 13 October 2026; see [Shirahone](/en/soak/shirahone/).

Hotaka, Hotakadake and Kasagatake are not on the agency's list of volcano names.

The ministry's plan says the area has volcanoes of the Norikura volcanic belt, that Yakedake is an active volcano that steams constantly, and that the last eruption to cause harm was in June 1962, when two people were injured. The association says the springs are a gift of "the active volcano Yakedake". No official source connects Yakedake's or Norikura's activity with the water at any of the five areas, so this page gives these facts side by side and does not link them.

## Where it came from

### The legends

The ministry's plan, and the association for Hirayu, give one legend for each of three areas. All are told as legends:

- **Hirayu**: when Takeda Shingen's army invaded Hida, the soldiers saw an old white monkey healing its wounds in a pool of hot water, bathed there and recovered their strength. The association says this is said to be how the spring was found. In the Edo period, the plan adds, lords from Hokuriku rested here on their journeys to and from Edo.
- **Fukuji**: Emperor Murakami is said to have come here in secret in the Heian period to bathe; the plan says the name of the **Tennō-sen** ("emperor's spring") is the trace.
- **Shin-Hirayu**: the monk **Enkū** is said to have come in 1690 and stayed about a year, bathing.

### Mountains and floods

The plan describes the area as the foot of the Northern Alps and the Gifu-side gateway for climbers, with Shin-Hotaka the base for Yarigatake and the Hotaka range. The association says Kasagatake can be seen from Hirayu; its front page gives the peak as 2,897 m and its Northern Alps page as 2,898 m.

The plan also says that the history of Okuhida is a history of river floods and landslides, citing a debris flow in 1920 that swept away 13 buildings in the Gamata district.

## How the spa works

### What each area has

The ministry's table of public facilities shows how different the five areas are:

- **Hirayu**: three day-bathing facilities (one closed), four footbaths and the bus terminal.
- **Fukuji**: 11 places to stay, one day-bathing facility and a footbath.
- **Shin-Hirayu**: 25 places to stay.
- **Tochio**: seven places to stay, one day-bathing facility, a footbath, a roadside station and a clinic.
- **Shin-Hotaka**: 33 places to stay, four day-bathing facilities, the ropeway and a footbath.

The plan counts overnight guests at 313,000 in 2020, 211,000 in 2021 and 321,000 in 2022, against nearly 600,000 a year before the pandemic, about 90,000 of them from overseas.

The association says the area has one of the largest numbers of open-air baths in Japan; the Hida Takayama tourism site's English page (2018) says the largest. Neither gives a count or a source.

### Shared baths on trust

Two baths described by the association run on a small payment rather than a fixed fee. The association does not say whether these include tax, so the amounts are not given.

- **Hirayu no Yu**, beside the Hirayu folk museum: a small contribution (*sunshi*); there is **no washing area**, so pour water over yourself before you get in; 6:00 to 21:00 (8:00 to 21:00 in winter), irregular closures.
- **Kōjin no Yu**, Tochio: an unstaffed open-air bath with separate changing areas; put a contribution in the box; open until 22:00.

### Shin-Hotaka no Yu, in swimwear

The association describes **Shin-Hotaka no Yu** as the only mixed open-air bath among the area's day-bathing facilities. As checked on 13 October 2026:

- **What to wear.** Swimwear or a *yuami-gi* bathing wrap is **required**. Wraps are sold at Hikaku no Yu and at Nakazaki Sansō Okuhida no Yu.
- **Season.** 18 April to **30 October 2026**, 8:00 to 18:00; closed for the winter from 31 October to late April.
- **Other closures.** Cleaning on Mondays and Fridays; it may close when the river is high.
- **Changing.** Separate changing rooms.
- **Payment.** A cleaning contribution; the association does not say whether it includes tax.

### Falls and the ropeway

- **Hirayu Ōtaki** falls: 64 m high and 6 m wide. The association calls it one of the three great waterfalls of Hida and says it was chosen among Japan's 100 waterfalls; neither its page nor the ministry's plan names who chose them. It freezes in midwinter, with an ice festival every February.
- **Shin-Hotaka Ropeway**: open all year. The first ropeway leaves on the hour and half-hour, the second at 15 and 45 minutes past. The upper station, Nishi-Hotakaguchi, is 2,156 m up, and the association gives the length as 3,200 m. The operator calls its double-decker gondola **Japan's only** one; the association calls it **Japan's first**. On 13 October 2026 the operator showed both ropeways running. Fares are not given, because the fare table does not say whether tax is included.

### Parking and events

On 24 August 2026 the association announced a trial of online booking and charging at Shin-Hotaka's No. 3 car park. The ministry's plan mentions free entry to selected open-air baths on 26 June, read as "ro-ten-buro day", and a bath-hopping pass; whether these run now is not stated.

## Bathing there

### Tattoos and English

The association has an English site covering the springs, access, food and sights, but neither its English nor its Japanese pages give a tattoo rule or a guide to bathing. The Hida Takayama English page does not mention tattoos either. The ministry's plan mentions making the tourist signs at Hirayu and Shin-Hotaka multilingual, in English, Chinese and Korean.

JNTO's general advice is that public baths decide case by case, and suggests checking ahead, covering a tattoo or using a private bath. More in the tattoos section of the [water types guide](/en/soak/onsen-water-types/). Ask each facility in advance.

### Compared with city baths

No Okuhida source compares its water with baths elsewhere. The ministry's plan does say that water is added to many sources here because they are so hot, so read each bath's notice for added water and other treatments; see [*kakenagashi*](/en/soak/kakenagashi-explained/).

## Food

**Hoba miso and Hida beef.** The association's food page says Hida beef, river fish, mountain vegetables and highland vegetables can be had in the five areas, along with traditional tastes such as **soba, hoba miso** and local sake. The farm ministry places hoba miso in Hida and Takayama, and says that today Hida beef, mushrooms and spring onion are grilled on the magnolia leaf too. Okuhida is in Takayama city, so you can eat hoba miso here. This matches the [Chūbu local food guide](/en/eat/kyodo-ryori-chubu/).

**Not keichan.** The association lists a place in Tochio serving a keichan rice bowl, but the farm ministry places keichan in Gujō, Gero and southern Takayama, not in Okuhida. For Gero, see [Gero Onsen](/en/soak/gero/).

**Not kanboshi daikon.** The ministry's *kanboshi daikon-ni* uses the word "Okuhida" for its produce, but the ministry places the dish in Hida city, with its produce from Yamanomura in Kamioka, and not in the spa area in Takayama.

**From the heat.** The ministry's plan says the spring's heat is used to raise dragon fruit, sturgeon and soft-shelled turtle, and that new food is being developed.

## Getting there

As checked on 13 October 2026. Fares are not on this page, because the official pages do not say whether they include tax.

- **From Takayama.** Nōhi Bus gives about **53 to 58 minutes** from the Takayama Nōhi bus centre to Hirayu Onsen (for example 7:00 to 7:58), about once an hour. For Kamikōchi, change at Hirayu.
- **From Matsumoto.** About **1 hour 28 minutes** from Matsumoto bus terminal to Hirayu Onsen (7:40 to 9:08, or 9:55 to 11:23), on the line run by Nōhi Bus and Alpico.
- **From Shinjuku.** About **4 hours 40 minutes** from Busta Shinjuku to Hirayu Onsen (7:05 to 11:45, or 8:15 to 12:55); about 4 hours 50 minutes back (9:35 to 14:25). All seats are reserved.
- **By train.** The association recommends the limited express *Hida* from Nagoya or Osaka, the *Azusa* from Tokyo or Shinjuku, or the *Shinano* from Nagoya, then a route bus. It gives from 3 hours 30 minutes by train and bus from Nagoya and from 4 hours 30 minutes from the Kantō area; by car, from about 2 hours 45 minutes from Nagoya and about 4 hours from Kantō.

**Route 158.** On 25 February 2026 a landslide hit Route 158 at Shioya, Nyūkawa, in Takayama. The association's notice (updated 14 July 2026) says about 2.4 km between Shioya and Hatahoko is restricted, with no date for reopening, and that cars and route buses take a city-road detour adding about 10 minutes. On 13 October 2026 Nōhi Bus warned that its Takayama–Shinjuku, Takayama–Matsumoto and Takayama–Shin-Hotaka lines are arriving late because of the detour.

For Shirahone on the Nagano side of Norikura, see [Shirahone Onsen](/en/soak/shirahone/); for other spas in the region, see [Gero](/en/soak/gero/), [Togura-Kamiyamada](/en/soak/togura-kamiyamada/), [Yudanaka-Shibu](/en/soak/yudanaka-shibu/) and [Nozawa](/en/soak/nozawa/).

## What people get wrong

- **"Okuhida is one town."** It is five spa areas spread over about 18 km.
- **"Yakedake heats the springs."** The association credits the volcano, but no official source links its activity to the water.
- **"Hotaka is a volcano."** It is not on the Meteorological Agency's list of volcano names.
- **"The water is all straight from the ground."** The ministry's plan says water is added to many sources because they are so hot.
- **"You can bathe naked in the mixed bath at Shin-Hotaka."** The association says swimwear or a bathing wrap is required.
- **"Keichan is Okuhida's dish."** The farm ministry places it in Gujō, Gero and southern Takayama.
