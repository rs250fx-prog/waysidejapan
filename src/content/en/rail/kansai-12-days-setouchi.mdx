Twelve days is the length most British, Australian and Canadian visitors actually take, and it is an awkward number. Long enough that Kyoto and Osaka alone will not fill it. Short enough that you cannot simply drift west and see what happens.

The Setouchi Area Pass runs for **seven consecutive days**, which means the real decision in this trip is not where to go. It is **which seven of your twelve days the pass should cover**.

JR-WEST describes the pass as covering the **Sanyo Shinkansen between Shin-Osaka and Hakata**, limited express trains, and special rapid, rapid and local services on JR-WEST and **JR-SHIKOKU** conventional lines. The Miyajima ferry is included. The named areas are Kansai, Okayama, Hiroshima, Yamaguchi, Takamatsu, Matsuyama and Fukuoka.

Two things are explicitly outside it: the **Tokaido Shinkansen** towards Tokyo, and the **Kyushu Shinkansen** south of Hakata. So this pass takes you west to Hakata and across to Shikoku, and stops there.

The detail most people miss is JR-SHIKOKU. Matsuyama and Takamatsu are inside the pass, which is what makes a twelve-day trip work — you are not paying twice to cross to the island.

**Starting on day 4 is right for most trips.** The first three days are Kyoto and Osaka, where you will walk and take subways rather than long-distance trains — and the subways are not JR anyway. Activating the pass on the morning you leave Kyoto puts all of the expensive movement inside the seven days, and leaves the last two days back in Kansai, where the local fares are small.

Starting on day 1 wastes the pass on three days of city walking. Starting on day 6 works only if you are certain the second half is the heavy half.

## The route

Day 1–3 — **Kansai Airport, Kyoto, Osaka.** Pass not yet started. Jet lag, temples, and the food that most visitors come for in the first place.

Day 4 — **Kyoto to Okayama or Kurashiki.** Pass starts. First day on the Sanyo Shinkansen.

Day 5–6 — **Onomichi.** The hill town on the channel, and the start of the bridges across to Shikoku.

Day 7–8 — **Hiroshima and Miyajima.** The ferry is on the pass.

Day 9–10 — **Matsuyama and Dogo Onsen.** Covered, because JR-SHIKOKU is inside the pass. This is the part a nationwide pass would not do better.

Day 11–12 — **Back to Kansai, and out.** The pass expires at the end of day 10; the last two days are short local hops.

## What I would change

If you have a thirteenth day, put it in Onomichi rather than Hiroshima. Hiroshima is a city with a museum you will want a full day for; Onomichi is a town that rewards a second morning, and most itineraries give it an afternoon.

If you have only ten days, cut Matsuyama rather than shortening everything. A rushed Shikoku crossing is worse than no Shikoku crossing.
